# Zone field guides: what each zone physically is, at walking scale

Load when writing or updating a zone field guide, or the field-guide rows of the act handoff. The worked example is
`docs/studio/research/cartography/reports/Act I zone field guide.md` (Act I, 11 zones, about 30,000 words), built from
the notes in `docs/studio/research/cartography/research_notes/Act I zone field guide/`.

## Why the cartographer owns it

George, 13 Nov 2026: zones need things to build assets from (vegetation, trees, plants, ground material), and for open
ground that belongs to the geography skill. *PROJECT.* The split:

| Role | Owns | Example on the salt flats |
|---|---|---|
| **Cartographer (you)** | What physically exists here and why: ground, rock, water and minerals, plants and where they grow, wildlife signs, weather and light, and the land use that geography explains (fields, check dams, salt cuttings, tracks, camps) | Polygon halite crust, brine sheets, slab sizes and how slabs are cut, stacked and loaded, the caravan night camp |
| Narrative (vault, `arpg-narrative`) | What happened here: story props, who did what, names, canon | Who the captives are, what the overseer did |
| Environment art (`arpg-environment-art`) | The art: material set, kit, foliage set, set dressing, decals | Turns the inventory into 5–8 materials, a slab-stack kit, a halophyte foliage set |

Enclosed spaces (city interiors, ruins' insides, caves built by people) are environment art's and narrative's; the
field guide still gives the ground and rock they sit in.

## FG1. One sheet per zone, same headings, in act order

Overview · Ground (walking scale) · Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline · Water and minerals · Vegetation (and where
nothing grows) · Wildlife and signs · Human traces and material culture · Air, weather and light · Palette (hex,
estimates) · **Asset seeds** (ground materials / rocks and landforms / foliage / props and stackables / decals / water and
VFX) · **Side-area ideas** · Beauty moments · Deliberate grit · Story hooks (ideas for narrative) · Departures from the
real place · Reference links. Facts shared by every zone (climate, the plant list by habitat, wildlife, caravan culture,
the region palette) go in one region-wide section first; zones refer to it. *OBSERVED (the Act I guide's layout).*

## FG2. Walking scale first

The game camera shows about 15–25 m. Give sizes in cm and m (polygon cells 1–2 m with ridges up to ~25 cm; a salt slab
24 × 36 × 6 cm), species with common and scientific names, *where exactly* each plant grows (wadi floor, spring edge,
cliff ledge, nowhere), density and condition (alive, dying, dead). Then landmark scale (100 m–km) for skylines.

## FG3. Every claim shows its status

Sourced fact (with a link) · **[Analogue: place]** (a real place standing in where the act's own region has no source:
Ijen for sulfur mining, White Sands for gypsum dunes) · *[Inference]* (our reasoning). Never present an analogue or an
inference as the region's fact. Where sources conflict, give the range. Hex values are estimates until sampled from
photos.

## FG4. Side-area ideas are a deliverable

Real sites inside or beside a zone that could become an optional side area (the Act I examples: a sulfur mine in the
Acid Pits, the salt-block mining-camp ruins, a CO₂ lake ringed with dead birds, a salt-cutters' camp, caravan night
camps, a lava tube, a drained crater). Each with its real basis, what you would find, and why it is worth the detour;
graded Strong (sourced in the region), Medium, or Analogue. They are ideas for George and narrative, never decisions.
*PROJECT (George, 13 Nov 2026: "keep details like the sulfur mine in the acid pits, it gives us ideas for side areas").*

## FG5. Departures are listed, never hidden

Where the map bends the real place (an invented waterfall, the acid dome moved to the pan's south edge, a volcano on a
fault block, stylised pink brine), say so in the zone's Departures, with the reason (story first, P8). The departure
stands; the record lets artists know what is real and what is ours.

## FG6. The colour of age

Across the Act I research one rule held in almost every zone: pale or bright means new and active; dark, red or brown
means old or dead (fresh vs old acid pools, black vs rusted basalt, varnish on old stone, green vs grey palms, white vs
brown salt crust, live vs relict tufa). Use it to tell the player what is alive and dangerous. *INFERRED from the
sourced notes.*

## FG7. Living cultures

The real region's people are a living culture. If the game's peoples are fictional, keep the building logic (what the
land and climate make people build) and change ornament and names; flag it for George rather than deciding. *INFERRED.*

## FG8. How to research one

1. Split the act's ground into geology tracks (Act I: salt and salt trade; the acid field and sulfur; basalt and the
   volcano; escarpment, gorge and plateau farming; the basin floor and pastoral life; reef terraces and gypsum; water,
   the hub and the dying spring). One researcher per track, the same 11-point brief, the map's facts for each zone in
   the brief.
2. Sources, in order: scientific papers and surveys (geology, botany, wildlife), ethnographies, then quality photo essays
   and documentaries for visual detail. Name the vault's decisions in the brief so beauty and grit are framed by them.
3. Synthesize into one guide (FG1). Spot-check the load-bearing facts against their sources before handing it on (the
   Act I check caught a mining date that overstated its source).
4. Link each zone's guide section from the act handoff (`templates/act-handoff.md`, "Field guide" row).
