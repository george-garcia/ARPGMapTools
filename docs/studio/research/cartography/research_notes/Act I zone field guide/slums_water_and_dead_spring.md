# Water in a hot desert rift: the Slums at the falls (date palms, settlement) and the Dead Spring (failing oasis)

Scope: Act I is modelled on the Danakil Depression / Afar region. Afar sources come first; everything from elsewhere is marked **ANALOGUE** with its place. Every line under "Cited Findings" has a source. Lines under "Inferences" are my reasoning or general knowledge that I could not source in this pass. They are marked so artists and the writer can tell canon-grade fact from studio judgement. Hex colours are always inference.

Source quality: the best Afar sources found were (1) Chocian 2017, a TU Wien diploma thesis on Afar nomadic architecture with field measurements, and (2) Fensham et al. 2024, a peer-reviewed paper in *Water* on central Afar springs with temperatures, chemistry, plants and uses. Journalism (Arab News/AFP, Swisseduc photo notes) covers the salt trade. No source I reached described Hamed Ela's or Berahile's houses in detail. Treat those as gaps that need photo review.

## 1. Desert waterfalls and spring-fed streams at an escarpment foot (travertine/tufa, algae, moss, ferns, reeds, wet stone, splash zone)

### Takeaway
Real Afar springs are fault-line springs at graben flanks and escarpment feet. Many are hot (36-100 °C at the vent). They feed small outflow wetlands of sedge (*Cyperus laevigatus*), cattail (*Typha latifolia*), reed (*Phragmites australis*, *P. karka*), Bermuda grass and doum palm, and are used for drinking, bathing, livestock and mud cures. Carbonate (travertine) chimneys up to 50 m stand at Lake Abbe. A cold, freshwater, tufa-building waterfall with ferns is not documented in the Afar sources I reached; it is best built from a labelled rift analogue (Ein Gedi on the Dead Sea Rift) plus general seep ecology.

### Cited Findings
- **Afar spring setting:** Teo springs rise at the eastern escarpment on the downthrown side of a fault: more than 100 springs within about 10 ha. The highest vents boil, and people draw cooler water (~40 °C) from hand-dug holes — [Fensham et al. 2024, *Water* 16(24):3698](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- **Alalo-bad (Tendaho Graben flank):** about 30 springs with vents at 58-100 °C. The main warm pool cools to ~40 °C. pH 7.3-9.3; Na 500-595 ppm, Cl 603-760 ppm, SO4 209-334 ppm. The outflow wetland covers ~981 ha — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- **Dobi Valley:** four spring clusters about 10.5 km apart below ridges ~500 m high, with the water table under 2 m. Vents run 36-57 °C (e.g. Big Kio 49 °C, Little Kio 57 °C, Gunguna 36-40 °C from ~12 vents). A stream (Bagili-farg'e) rises ~14 km up-valley — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- **Afar spring plants (Afar names in brackets):** *Cyperus laevigatus* (dara) right at the springs; *Typha latifolia* (gadeyta), *Phragmites australis* and *P. karka* (hamelto) in the outflow wetlands; *Cynodon dactylon* (antarba); doum palm *Hyphaene thebaica* (garayto) at Dobi — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- **Uses:** drinking, bathing, watering livestock, steam and mud treatments, cooking in hot water at Air Hamadho, and gathering salt for cooking. Mud is smeared on milk animals at Teo. Palm thatch is soaked in the spring water at Dobi — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- **Travertine chimneys (Afar, Ethiopia-Djibouti border):** Lake Abbe has carbonate chimneys up to 50 m tall that pour steam. They form where lake water mixes with deep geothermal fluid. Flamingos use the lake — [Wikipedia: Lake Abbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Abbe)
- **ANALOGUE, Ein Gedi, Dead Sea Rift (Israel):** two perennial spring-fed streams (Nahal David, Nahal Arugot) plus the Shulamit and Ein Gedi springs, ~3 million m³/yr combined. The flora mixes tropical, desert, Mediterranean and steppe plants (Sodom apple, acacia, jujube, poplar). Wildlife includes Nubian ibex, rock hyrax and >200 migrant bird species — [Wikipedia: Ein Gedi Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ein_Gedi_Nature_Reserve)
- **ANALOGUE, seep cliff ecology (Texas):** maidenhair fern *Adiantum capillus-veneris* forms narrow horizontal bands on seepy cliff faces where water leaks from exposed limestone. Different ferns and herbs grow on the wet marly rubble at the cliff foot. Rare bryophytes (mosses) are typical — [USGS NVCS unit 689419](https://www1.usgs.gov/csas/nvcs/unitDetails/689419)

### Inferences
- **The Slums falls as a mixed spring.** Afar springs are fault springs, so a stream that cascades off the escarpment and is topped up by springs at the fault foot is plausible. A cold or warm fresh stream with a hot vent or two nearby fits both the sources and the story. A boiling-vent field (Teo-style) at the foot would read as hazard, not a hub, so I would keep hot vents off to one side.
- **Splash zone banding (from wet to dry; from seep ecology, not sourced in Afar):**
  - wettest band: black-green algal film and dark wet stone directly behind the falls;
  - then bright green moss cushions and maidenhair fern fronds (10-30 cm) in a ribbon on the dripping face;
  - then sedge and reed on ledges and the plunge-pool rim;
  - then dry rock 1-3 m beyond the spray line, abruptly bare.

  This hard edge between green and bare is the strongest visual tell of desert water.
- **Tufa.** Tufa or travertine curtains (pale cream-grey, lumpy, dripstone-like ledges with moss growing into them) are plausible only if the escarpment carries carbonate rock or the water is carbonate-rich. The Lake Abbe chimneys prove carbonate precipitation happens in Afar hydrothermal water. Check the vault's escarpment strata before committing (cartography decision).
- **Stone staining.** Dark vertical streaks (desert varnish, manganese/iron) on dry faces; white efflorescence (salt crust) on stone that wets and dries; rust-orange iron staining where spring water seeps out. All are general geology inference.
- **Splash-zone humidity** reaches only a few metres. Beyond it the air is dry (inference).

### Gaps
- No source found for a named waterfall on the Danakil western escarpment, or for photos of one. Possible leads, not checked: gorges on the Berahile-Mekele salt route (e.g. Saba river/Abala area).
- No quantified sizes for maidenhair fern, moss cushions, or tufa ledges in desert settings were retrieved.
- Ein Gedi waterfall heights and Nahal David flora (maidenhair, Phragmites, Salvadora) were not on the page fetched.

## 2. Date palms (*Phoenix dactylifera*) and doum palms (*Hyphaene thebaica*): form, cultivation, death in drought, building products

### Takeaway
A mature date palm has a single trunk up to 20 m tall and about 1-1.1 m round, clad in a lattice of cut frond bases. Its crown holds about 100-125 green fronds, each 3-6 m long, with spines up to 24 cm. Offshoots cluster at the base. Doum palm is the native Afar palm: up to ~17 m tall, its trunk forks in Ys, and it bears fan leaves ~120 × 180 cm. Its leaves are the raw material for Afar house mats. In Afar today it is being outcompeted by invasive *Prosopis juliflora*.

### Cited Findings
- **Date palm trunk:** up to 20 m tall (over 20 m including the crown), circumference ~1-1.1 m — [FAO, Date palm cultivation, ch. on morphology](https://www.fao.org/3/y4360e/y4360e05.htm)
- **Fronds:** 3-6 m long (≈4 m average), each living 3-7 years. An adult palm carries ~100-125 green fronds and makes 10-26 new ones a year. Fronds are arranged in 13 spiral columns. Each frond has 120-240 leaflets, 15 to >100 cm long and 1-6.3 cm wide — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/3/y4360e/y4360e05.htm)
- **Spines:** 10-~60 per frond (at the base), from a few cm up to 24 cm long and up to 1 cm thick — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/3/y4360e/y4360e05.htm)
- **Fruit:** a female palm produces ~12 spathes (bunches) a year on average, at about 8 fronds per bunch. Each bunch has 50-150 strands. Fruit is 18-110 mm long and 2-60 g — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/3/y4360e/y4360e05.htm)
- **Offshoots:** 3-5 per palm (cv. Barhee) up to 20-25 (cv. Medjool) — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/3/y4360e/y4360e05.htm)
- **Roots:** primary roots average ~4 m long (up to 10 m), ~9.5 mm thick. Roots are found up to 25 m from the trunk and more than 6 m deep; ~85% lie within 2 m of the surface — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/3/y4360e/y4360e05.htm)
- **Doum palm form:** up to ~17 m tall, girth up to ~90 cm, trunk forking two ways (dichotomous) with tufts of leaves at the branch tips. Leaves are fan-shaped, ~120 × 180 cm, on ~1 m stalks with upward-curving claws. Fruits are large, woody and shiny-crusted and stay on the female tree a long time. It grows in wadis, at oases and on groundwater, dislikes waterlogging, and resists fire — [Wikipedia: Hyphaene thebaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphaene_thebaica)
- **Doum products:** leaves and fibre for baskets, mats, coarse cloth, brooms, rope, string and thatch; leaf stalks for fencing; timber for posts, poles, furniture and beehives; sap for palm wine — [Wikipedia: Hyphaene thebaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphaene_thebaica)
- **Doum palm in Afar:** garayto palm at the Dobi springs gives thatch and sap (doma). *Prosopis juliflora*, which arrived ~20 years ago, has outcompeted it and the native pasture. *Prosopis* covers ~1,235 ha at Dobi and ~101 ha at Alalo-bad, and locals make charcoal from it — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- **Afar mats are doum palm:** Afar daboyta houses are covered with woven doum palm mats, bought at Asayita and Elidar markets or woven by women from raw leaves; reed mats are used around Gewane. Mats are replaced every couple of years — [Chocian 2017, TU Wien thesis](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- **ANALOGUE, dying date palms (Morocco, Drâa valley and nearby oases):** drought-stressed fronds lie curled on the ground at Tighmert. At Tanoueest near Assa only a few palms remain, "overrun by sand". About 10,000 palms burn each year in Moroccan oases. A blaze at Tighmert hit trees and houses — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/desert-oasis-morocco-vanishing)

### Inferences
- **Date palm visual anatomy.** These are from general horticulture knowledge; sizes are estimates.
  - Frond-base "boots" (cut petiole stubs, ~10-20 cm proud of the trunk) form a diamond lattice. Fibre mesh (brown and hairy) wraps the young trunk.
  - The skirt of dead brown fronds hangs below the green crown on unkempt palms. Tended palms are pruned to a clean "pineapple" trunk.
  - Fruit bunches hang on long orange-yellow stalks below the fronds; the dates turn green, then yellow or red, then brown.
  - Pollination is by hand: a worker climbs with a loop belt (a rope or fibre band around trunk and waist) and ties sprigs of male flowers into the female bunch.
- **How palms die in drought (unsourced):**
  1. The lower fronds yellow, then brown and droop; the crown thins to a few green spears.
  2. The trunk narrows where growth slowed (a "pencil-point" waist), a visible record of a bad decade.
  3. The crown dies, the fronds hang straight down in a dead brown skirt, and the bud rots or snaps off. This leaves a "beheaded" bare pole, 5-15 m tall, the classic dead-oasis silhouette.
  4. Poles fall or are cut, and fibrous stumps remain.

  Burnt palms are black poles with charred skirts (see the Morocco fire data above).
- **Slums palm line, condition gradient.** Suggest healthy, crowned palms only within ~5-10 m of the live channel; then stressed palms with brown skirts; then a few headless poles at the dry ends. This sells "failing as the stream shrinks" in a single screen.
- **Doum as the Afar-authentic palm.** Doum's forked silhouette is distinctive and native to the region. Date palm suits the irrigated, cultivated look. Recommendation: doum in the wild wadi/spring edges, date palms in the planted line at the Slums.
- **Palm materials in building (inference, consistent with the sources):** frond-rib fences and shade roofs (jarid), trunk beams and posts (palm wood is fibrous; it splits and rots rather than holding a clean edge), fibre rope, mats, baskets.

### Gaps
- No Ethiopian source on date palm cultivation in Afar was retrieved. I could not confirm whether date palms are grown at Afrera, Berahile or elsewhere in the Danakil.
- I found no agronomic source with numbers on drought mortality or symptoms (trunk constriction, crown collapse); the descriptions above are unsourced.
- Irrigation basin sizes and channel dimensions for date gardens were not found.

## 3. Building materials and layouts of poor Afar and hot-desert settlements

### Takeaway
The Afar house (ari/aari; the portable dome is the daboyta) is 3.6-4.4 m across and 2.0-2.8 m high. It has a flexible acacia/Cordia branch frame under doum palm or reed mats, inside thorn fences 1.5-3 m high. Volcanic stone is dry-stacked or clay-bonded for walls, corbelled pens, hearths and mat weights. Settled houses use ch'qa (wood frame plastered with mud). Hamed Ela is a "ramshackle settlement of huts" with little water. Berahile is a salt depot on a dry riverbed where ~5,000 salt blocks a day are moved onto trucks.

### Cited Findings
- **Daboyta (mobile dome):** average 3.60-4.40 m diameter and 2.00-2.80 m high (Lower Awash); size stays the same however many people live inside. The frame is flexible acacia branches and roots, plus Derrapto, Kelayto and Miderto (*Cordia*) because they are elastic. It is covered with doum palm mats (reed mats near Gewane). Women build it, entrance to the current wind. Three to four women take it down; one camel plus two donkeys carry it — [Chocian 2017](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- **Terms:** ari/aari (house), daboyta (round mobile house), olloyta (bed mat, part of a woman's dowry), buda (nuclear homestead), daala (fenced compound of related homesteads around a shared space), ganta (settlement of cooperating compounds), gaso (animal enclosures) — [Chocian 2017](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- **Who makes what:** women make the house, palm mats, sisal ropes and furniture; men cut tree stems for roofs and branches for fences. Grass bowls are sealed by smoking and used for milk — [Chocian 2017](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- **Fences:** small enclosures ~1.5 m high, main ones 2-3 m. Materials are acacia branches, dead trunks and *Dobera glabra* stalks, reinforced with thorn bush (Hedayto, Habaleyta). A thorn "second skin" around the house base keeps livestock off the mats. Temporary thorn corrals are built on the move, and men sleep beside them in the open — [Chocian 2017](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- **Stone:** volcanic stone, dry-stacked or bonded with clay mortar, for house enclosures. Corbelled stone domes shelter young animals at night. Stones weight down mats, form hearths and line bread ovens. Interiors have a small fireplace and beds with acacia wood frames; men sleep outside on mats in the hot season — [Chocian 2017](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- **Permanent grass hut:** larger and heavier than the daboyta: acacia frame, grass covering, Sarganto posts holding up the roof. Found in the western border areas (Chifra, Ewa, Gulina, Yalo) — [Chocian 2017](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- **Settling Afar:** permanent houses along trade routes use wood frames plastered with clay (the chikka/ch'qa technique), which the project calls unsustainable where trees are scarce. Earth is called the only building material sufficiently available in the region — [TU Wien, Afar earth architecture project](https://baugeschichte.tuwien.ac.at/website/afarearth/)
- **Water at Logya:** families draw water from the Tendaho canal and the river (banks too steep for livestock), buy expensive municipal piped water, or use NGO-built wells — [Chocian 2017](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- **Hamed Ela:** "a ramshackle settlement of huts near the salt fields". A miner says, "We have no water and sometimes we eat bad food." Pay is ~500 birr/day — [Arab News / AFP 2017](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp). About 4,000 villagers, who live by salt work — [Colored Splendor photo essay](https://coloredsplendor.myportfolio.com/the-village-of-hamed-ela)
- **Berahile (Berhale):** the main salt-trading outpost, on a dry riverbed at the edge of town. About 5,000 salt blocks arrive a day and are loaded onto trucks; a tarmac road reached Mekele ~5 years before 2017 — [Arab News / AFP](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp). Afar arho (camel drivers) take caravans only as far as Berahile, the edge of Afar territory — [Swisseduc, Salt of Africa 2008](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- **Salt blocks:** each ~7 kg, cut with axes from the pan crust; once used as currency. Each camel carries 20 blocks to Mekele (≈140 kg) — [Swisseduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html). Conflicting load figure: ~200 kg per camel — [Colored Splendor](https://coloredsplendor.myportfolio.com/the-village-of-hamed-ela)
- **Caravan rhythm:** hundreds of caravans gather at Hamed Ela, and a chief sets their order overnight. Thousands of camels leave at dawn for the pan. Tigrayan salt lifters (Focolo) leave as early as 3 am. Midday on the pan is above 40 °C, and the caravans vanish into mirage. Camels chew salt mid-morning — [Swisseduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html). Up to 50 °C on the cracked lakebed — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)
- **ANALOGUE, abandoned ksar (Bounou, Morocco):** 200 families once lived there and 5 remain. Rammed-earth walls are crumbling, and ground floors are given over to sand — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/desert-oasis-morocco-vanishing)

### Inferences
- **Slums at ~9,000 people:** a mixed fabric, not a mat-hut camp.
  - **Core:** ch'qa (mud on stick frame) and volcanic-stone rooms.
  - **Edges:** domed mat daboyta of recent arrivals and nomads.
  - **Everywhere:** thorn and dead-wood fences; corbelled stone pens for kids and lambs.
  - **Story-tunable layer:** salvage (corrugated sheet, drums, crate wood), which can stand in for this world's equivalent of trade scrap. Corrugated iron and plastic jerry cans are near-universal in present-day Ethiopian towns from general knowledge, but none of the sources fetched describe them; mark them unsourced.
- **Salt-block walls** are plausible for a salt-caravan hub: low walls and stacks of grey-white 7 kg blocks, about 30-40 × 20-30 × 5-10 cm (block size not sourced; my estimate). The Berahile depot suggests big stacks of blocks beside a dry riverbed, waiting for onward transport. Salt walls weather fast if wetted, so put them away from the spray zone.
- **Spring-side uses to stage:** mud-smeared animals; palm-frond thatch soaking in a channel; women filling containers at a cooler dug hole beside a hotter vent. All are drawn from the Fensham uses list.
- **Rubbish and heat (unsourced):** organic waste dries and mummifies rather than rotting wet. Plastic sun-bleaches and shreds. Dung dries into flat cakes, often collected as fuel. Flies cluster on wet or organic spots only.

### Gaps
- No direct description (or captioned photos) of Hamed Ela, Berahile, Afdera or Abala buildings, markets, salt depots or water storage was retrieved. Visual review of the photo essays listed in section 7 is needed.
- Salt block dimensions are not given in any source fetched.
- Goatskins, jerry cans, cisterns and plastic drums are not documented in the sources reached (Chocian excerpts did not cover water vessels).

## 4. Dying springs and failing oases (water table, stagnant pools, crusts, cracked mud, dead palms, abandoned wells, bones, insects)

### Takeaway
Real failing oases dry out through falling water tables, river diversion and salinisation, and then lose people. The look is a dry channel; curled dead fronds on sand; palms burnt or buried; sand-choked canals; crumbling earth walls. In salt basins it is also salt flats spreading across a lake's former floor and ground that collapses into sinkholes.

### Cited Findings
- **Afar shrinking lake:** by 1984 Lake Abbe had shrunk to about two-thirds of its 1940 area, and ~11,500 ha of salt flats formed to the southwest. Causes were drought and irrigation withdrawals from the Awash. Salt flats up to 10 km wide line its southwest and south shores. It is an "amplifier lake": small climate changes swing its level sharply. Rain <172 mm/yr; summer 30-40 °C — [Wikipedia: Lake Abbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Abbe)
- **Afar invasive plant at springs:** *Prosopis juliflora* (Afar name woyane) has spread at Afar springs since ~2009 (Alalo-bad) and ~20 years ago (Dobi), displacing doum palm and pasture. Locals cut it for charcoal — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- **Afar springs as contested ground:** the Issa incursion (2008-2022) killed ten people at Alalo-bad. Fighting around 1974 displaced locals from Teo for decades, and the town of Adaytu was destroyed and rebuilt. Floods at Dobi reach 4 m deep; 15 camels were lost in 2023 — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- **Lake Afrera:** hypersaline, ~115 km², 112 m below sea level, fed by numerous hot springs on its shores. It was ~50 m higher in the African Humid Period. Its salt became inedible after acid contamination from the 2011 Nabro eruption. Brine was once pumped into artificial evaporation ponds. Endemic fish: *Danakilia franchettii* (cichlid) and *Aphaniops stiassnyae* (pupfish) — [Wikipedia: Lake Afrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Afrera). The lake reads emerald green; huge white salt rocks build up along the shore; a freshwater hot spring beside the lake is used as a "natural jacuzzi" — [Brilliant Ethiopia](https://brilliant-ethiopia.com/lake-afrera)
- **ANALOGUE, Morocco (Drâa, Mhamid, Tighmert, Bounou, Merzouga):**
  - Mhamid's concrete bridge spans a dry sand-and-gravel Drâa bed.
  - One well deepened from 23 ft (7 m) in 1996 to 52 ft (16 m).
  - Brackish water from solar pumps raises soil salinity.
  - The oasis edge loses >300 ft (~90 m) a year to sand, and sand fills neglected canals and plots in a few windy days.
  - The population is down by a fifth in 20 years.

  — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/desert-oasis-morocco-vanishing). Residents report digging >40 m for water versus 7-10 m in the 1980s — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1627871)
- **ANALOGUE, Dead Sea Rift (Ein Gedi):**
  - The sea is falling >1 m/yr and has lost ~1/3 of its surface since the 1960s.
  - Freshwater dissolves buried salt and leaves cavities that collapse into sinkholes, some >10 m deep, by the thousand.
  - Near Ein Gedi, ~3 km of rocky sand between the old spa and the shore is "dotted with holes and crevices".
  - The former spa beach is cratered. A ghost-town tourist complex has gutted pavement, overturned lampposts and an **abandoned date plantation**. The shore is whitened, salt-crusted and lunar.

  — [AFP via phys.org 2021](https://phys.org/news/2021-10-sinkholes-receding-dead-sea-shore.pdf); [Wikipedia: Ein Gedi Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ein_Gedi_Nature_Reserve)

### Inferences
- **Dead Spring layering (from the centre outward; unsourced but consistent with the evaporite and spring sources above):**
  1. A few small stagnant pools (0.5-5 m across, 5-50 cm deep), clear but tinted by algae and minerals:
     - **Edges:** green to olive-brown algal scum on the surface; orange-rust iron floc on the bottom where iron-bearing water seeps; greasy grey-black anoxic mud with a sulphur smell in the deeper pools.
     - **Rims:** a white salt rind where the water has evaporated, often a stack of rings marking successive falling levels (a "bathtub ring" record).
  2. Polygonal cracked mud, plates 10-40 cm across with curled edges 1-3 cm thick, white salt on the crack lips. Further out the cracks fill with blown sand.
  3. Dead reeds and cattail: grey-straw stems standing or lodged flat, black bases, stubble burnt in patches.
  4. Dead scrub: grey skeletal acacia, *Tamarix*-like bushes with salt-encrusted twigs, and green, thorny invasive *Prosopis* as the only thriving plant (sourced for Afar). This is a strong theme note: the invader thrives while the native palm dies.
  5. Beheaded palm poles, fallen trunks, a dead frond skirt in the sand.
- **Human ruin at the Dead Spring:** sand-filled channel lines (bank humps ~30-60 cm high, 0.5-1 m apart); rectangular dried irrigation basins; a stone-lined well ring with a fallen frame and frayed rope; crumbling ch'qa/stone houses; empty thorn pens.
- **Bones:** livestock bones bleached white, sun-split; hide mummified on the bones in the heat. Camel bones are big landmarks: ribs ~60-80 cm, a skull ~50-60 cm long (rough sizes, unsourced). Sourced hook: 15 camels lost in a 2023 Dobi flood (Fensham).
- **Insects:** flies on the bones and the wet mud; mosquito larvae wriggling in the stagnant pools, adults rising at dusk. General knowledge, unsourced here.
- **Sinkholes:** the Dead Sea sinkholes are a strong, real hazard idea for a drying salt-basin spring. Collapse pits 1-10 m+ across with sharp rims and salt-crusted walls fit "hazardous dying world" exactly.

### Gaps
- No source was retrieved describing a specific dying spring inside the Danakil itself, or the condition of the Afrera hot springs (temperatures, palms).

## 5. Wildlife and signs around desert water

### Takeaway
The sourced wildlife is thin but specific: Aphanius-type killifish (~30 mm) in warm Afar spring ponds; endemic cichlid and pupfish in Lake Afrera; flamingos on Lake Abbe; camels at springs and floods; Nubian ibex, rock hyrax and many migrant birds at the rift-analogue oasis (Ein Gedi).

### Cited Findings
- *Aphanius* killifish (~30 mm) live in warm ponds in the Dobi Valley, ~170 km from known Lake Afdera populations — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
- Lake Afrera's endemic fish are *Danakilia franchettii* and *Aphaniops stiassnyae* — [Wikipedia: Lake Afrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Afrera)
- Flamingos on Lake Abbe — [Wikipedia: Lake Abbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Abbe)
- Livestock graze, drink and are treated with mud at Afar springs. Lactating and young animals stay inside the compound, the rest in pens at night — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698); [Chocian 2017](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/5313/2/Chocian%20Emilia%20-%202017%20-%20Wherever%20it%20rains%20we%20go%20there%20Afar%20nomadic%20architecture...pdf)
- Camels chew salt for energy on the pan — [Swisseduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- ANALOGUE, Ein Gedi: Nubian ibex, rock hyrax, >200 migrant bird species — [Wikipedia: Ein Gedi Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ein_Gedi_Nature_Reserve)

### Inferences
- **Slums:** tiny fish darting in the plunge pool; goats and donkeys drinking at the channel; camels kneeling by the salt stacks; weaver-bird nests hanging from palms or acacia; ravens and kites over the rubbish; frogs or toads calling at night by the pools. General East African knowledge, not sourced.
- **Dead Spring:** vultures or ravens on carcasses; flies; at most one or two flamingos or waders at a brine pool (flamingos need saline water, so they suit the dead mineral pools better than the living falls — inference from the Abbe record).

### Gaps
- No source on birds at Afar springs specifically (weavers, ravens, vultures) or on amphibians in Danakil springs.

## 6. Air, weather and light near desert water

### Takeaway
Sourced: extreme heat (above 40 °C by noon on the pan, up to 50 °C), mirages, dawn caravan departures, steam from hot springs and the Lake Abbe chimneys. Mist, humidity and evening light at a falls are inference.

### Cited Findings
- On the pan it is above 40 °C by noon, and caravans disappear into a distant mirage. Caravans leave at dawn and travel through the night — [Swisseduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Up to 50 °C on Lake Asale — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)
- Steam pours from the Lake Abbe chimneys. Boiling vents at Teo; 58-100 °C vents at Alalo-bad — [Wikipedia: Lake Abbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Abbe); [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)

### Inferences
- **Falls:** a fine drifting spray haze within ~5-15 m of the plunge. On cooler mornings the spray hangs as a thin white veil, and low sun through it can throw a small rainbow (a beauty moment). Elsewhere the air shimmers with heat; dust hangs in late afternoon, turning the light amber and then rust.
- **Dead Spring:** still, heavy air; heat shimmer over the white crust; faint steam wisps only if a warm vent survives; a sulphur or rotten-egg smell (could drive a VFX haze tint).

### Gaps
- No climatological humidity figures for the Danakil escarpment foot were retrieved.

## 8. Zone inventory: the Dead Spring (synthesis for asset lists)

### Takeaway
Build the Dead Spring as the act's failed oasis. Small mineral-tinted stagnant pools sit inside rings of white salt; polygonal cracked mud is salted at the lips; grey dead reeds stand around. Beheaded and fallen palms, sand-choked channels, a dead well, empty thorn pens and bleached bones complete it, and the only thriving green is thorny invasive *Prosopis*. Optional collapse sinkholes come from the Dead Sea analogue.

### Cited Findings
- Afar mechanics of decline: lake shrinkage and 11,500 ha of new salt flats at Lake Abbe, caused by drought and diversion — [Wikipedia: Lake Abbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Abbe)
- *Prosopis* replacing doum palm at Afar springs; springs as conflict zones; flood losses — [Fensham et al. 2024](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/24/3698)
