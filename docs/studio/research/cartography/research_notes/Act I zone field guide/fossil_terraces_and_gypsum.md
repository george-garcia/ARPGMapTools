# Fossil Terraces and Gypsum Steps (Danakil Depression, Act I): visual field notes

Scope: (a) Pleistocene fossil coral reefs and marine terraces of the Danakil; (b) gypsum pavement, crusts, karst, selenite and the evaporite sequence; (c) gypsum dunes (White Sands, New Mexico, as a labelled ANALOGUE); (d) saline pools and rims. Danakil facts come first. Anything from elsewhere is marked **[ANALOGUE: place]**. Anything not directly supported by a cited source is in the "Inferences" lists or marked **[INFERENCE]**. Sizes are in cm and m. Hex colours are always inference (no source gives colour values).

Research budget note: about 30 tool calls. Several key primary sources could not be read in full: Bonatti et al. 1971 (only a citation page), Rime et al. 2024/2025 *Sedimentology* (Wiley blocked; the University of Fribourg PDF came back as binary), the PMC paper on Egyptian Red Sea Pleistocene reefs (captcha), and Holwerda and Hutchinson 1968 (cited only second-hand). The gaps are listed per question.

## 1. The fossil reefs: where, how old, what is on them, how they weather, colours, how the terraces step

### Takeaway
Pleistocene coral-and-red-algae ("coralgal") fringing reefs ring the Danakil margins as at least four stacked units. The two youngest are dated by U/Th to about 122 ka (MIS 5e) and about 210–231 ka (MIS 7). They form resistant ledges 3–8 m thick, attached directly to volcanic rock. Under them is an older, more strongly cemented reef; between the two runs a bored erosion surface. Gypsum and halite lie over both. A good field-scale model for the game: two benches per locality, a cream carbonate cliff band breaking into ledges and fallen blocks, and a lava-gravel fan wash over it. The sources describe the macro-fauna (Tridacna giant clams, mussels, horn snails, serpulid worm tubes, metre-scale microbial domes) much better than they describe coral species or colours.

### Cited Findings
**Location and setting**
- Pleistocene coralgal reef terraces fringe the margins of the Danakil Depression. They record "at least four" Red Sea incursions, each followed by drying out. The two youngest terraces are dated to MIS 5e and MIS 7 — [Rime/Foubert group, EGU21-6135](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-6135.html)
- MIS 7 sediments on the northern and western margins are uplifted up to 170 m above sea level. Outcrops closer to the rift axis have subsided below sea level. The eastern margin moved less, but rose enough to keep the Holocene sea out — [EGU21-6135](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-6135.html)
- Western margin: 29 outcrops spread over about 70 km were studied, and 21 of them carry fibrous aragonite crusts or spherules. Relative elevations of the outcrops range from about −35 m to +80 m (examples: CO1 +4, CO3 −34, CO6-1 +80, CO13 +50, CO26 −35). The depression floor reaches about −120 m — [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019, *Sedimentology* 66:480–512](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- Warren (Saltwork Consultants): "a well-defined set of reef outcrops atop the eastern bajada" marks a Late Pleistocene marine episode. The outcrop rims of the basin step down in elevation, first an earlier reef phase and then a later gypsum phase. There is also an "extensive gypsum plain" northeast of the modern salt pan, recording a higher, less saline lake stage — [Warren, Saltwork Consultants: Dallol Saline Pan](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/dallol-danakhil-ethiopia/)
  - Note: the papers above are mostly about the WESTERN margin. Warren places reefs on the EASTERN bajada. EGU21 says the reefs fringe the margins in general. Read together, reefs occur on both margins. This suits the map, where the terraces step down eastward.
- Archaeological survey on the western foothills between Lakes Afdera and Assale (Afrera and Asale): "conspicuous and laterally extensive" fringing reefs running NNW–SSE. At **Girawi'en**, north-west of Hamed Ela, a steep reef terrace sits on an escarpment above a floodplain that runs several km toward Lake Assale — [Antiquity 2017, Archaeological reconnaissance of the Late Pleistocene Red Sea coast in the Danakil](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/archaeological-reconnaissance-of-the-late-pleistocene-red-sea-coast-in-the-danakil/6A77DA823BE599877B0920ECEF4A571C/core-reader)

**Ages**
- U/Th ages: corals of the upper succession are 121.9 ± 1.0 ka and 122.9 ± 1.7 ka (MIS 5e). *Tridacna* shells of the lower succession are about 231 ka and about 210 ka (MIS 7). The authors warn that Tridacna dates are uncertain — [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- Earlier C-14 and U/Th work on corals and bivalves gave roughly 230 ka to 24 ka for the marine phases — [Atnafu, Kidane, Foubert et al., Eos "Reading history in Afar"](https://eos.org/science-updates/reading-history-afar); the same range is cited from Bannert et al. 1971 and Bonatti et al. 1971 in [Antiquity 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/archaeological-reconnaissance-of-the-late-pleistocene-red-sea-coast-in-the-danakil/6A77DA823BE599877B0920ECEF4A571C/core-reader)
- The Last Interglacial terrace is roughly the same age as the Abdur Reef Limestone of Eritrea (about 125 ka) — [Antiquity 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/archaeological-reconnaissance-of-the-late-pleistocene-red-sea-coast-in-the-danakil/6A77DA823BE599877B0920ECEF4A571C/core-reader)
- Most of northern Afar was probably covered by the Red Sea until about 30 ka — [Bonatti et al. 2017, *Heliyon*, Lake Afrera](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/d51b78f1-0c13-459a-9e76-d848b3d18435/prod_416479-doc_146811.pdf)

**What you see on them (reef content)**
- Lower succession (MIS 7): a fringing reef of corals and red algae, topped by **up to 2 m of red-algal framestone** (encrusting coralline algae: *Lithophyllum* gr. *kotschyanum*, with *Porolithon onkodes* and others). Crusts fill cavities up to 30 cm wide. Most of the original aragonite has turned to calcite, and **gypsum** fills the remaining pore space after it — [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- Upper succession (MIS 5): **isolated coral frameworks, not a continuous reef**, with minor red algae. Fibrous crusts and build-ups lie directly on the marine beds, and evaporites lie on top of those — [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- Microbial / fibrous crusts are up to 30 cm thick. Their forms are microdigitate, columnar (branching and unbranched) and non-columnar. Columns are up to 6 cm in diameter. Shallow domes run from a few cm to **1 m** across. Spherules up to 1 cm across form a bed under 10 cm thick. Weathered surfaces show the lamination, and crusts from deeper water have a **"brain-like" surface**. The crusts grew down to at least 80 m below the old sea level, on slopes steeper than 30° — [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- Fauna: the bivalves *Brachidontes pharaonis* (a small mussel, often with both valves still joined) and *Tridacna* (giant clam); the gastropod *Pirenella conica* (a small horn snail of salty lagoons); serpulid worm tubes, common in the upper unit. The paper **names no coral genera** — [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- Eos (same team): at least four stacked coralgal units with eroded surfaces between them, laid over volcanic rock. Only one bivalve species and one gastropod species occur between the coralgal and microbial layers. Small stromatolites and thrombolites grow on reef slopes, inside reef cavities, and around old and modern hypersaline lake margins. Hot-spring carbonates line some lake and brine-pool deposits — [Eos, Reading history in Afar](https://eos.org/science-updates/reading-history-afar)
- In the reef: "reef skeletons and various types of shells", and the carbonate is "indurated" (hard and well cemented) — [Antiquity 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/archaeological-reconnaissance-of-the-late-pleistocene-red-sea-coast-in-the-danakil/6A77DA823BE599877B0920ECEF4A571C/core-reader)

**How the terraces step**
- At every locality there are **two marine terraces** separated by a sharp erosion surface. That surface is bored by *Gastrochaenolites* (bivalve borings, flask-shaped holes). The lower (older) unit is the harder-cemented one. In the north, local uplift can put the older terrace **above** the younger reefs. In the central and southern depression the height difference is smaller, and the younger unit is just a thin skin over the older reef — [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- The main reef terrace is **3–8 m thick**, "resistant", and at Girawi'en "steep". The top of the reef survives as **"intact ledges and rolling blocks"** that have broken away from the terrace. At most sites alluvium covers the reef, including **lava gravels** typical of the western rift margin — [Antiquity 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/archaeological-reconnaissance-of-the-late-pleistocene-red-sea-coast-in-the-danakil/6A77DA823BE599877B0920ECEF4A571C/core-reader)

**Human traces on the reefs**
- At Girawi'en, a small scatter of freshly eroded stone tools lies on or just under the reef deposits. They are mostly flakes, plus two prismatic cores and a convergent Levallois flake (Middle Stone Age character). The raw materials are obsidian, basalt and patinated chert, and some pieces still carry carbonate matrix. Artefacts are rare overall — [Antiquity 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/archaeological-reconnaissance-of-the-late-pleistocene-red-sea-coast-in-the-danakil/6A77DA823BE599877B0920ECEF4A571C/core-reader)

### Inferences
- **Terrace profile for level design [INFERENCE from the 3–8 m thickness, two units per site, ledges and rolling blocks]:** risers about 3–8 m (a cliff band one to two screens tall at 15–25 m per screen), with a talus apron of fallen reef blocks 0.5–3 m across at the foot. The two benches per locality can be separated by a few metres (south) to tens of metres (north). Tread widths are **not sourced**: treat them as a design choice, roughly 20–200 m, so each bench holds a combat arena.
- **Coral forms [INFERENCE / ANALOGUE: Red Sea Pleistocene reefs in general]:** the sources name no Danakil coral genera. Other raised Red Sea reefs are usually described with massive *Porites* domes, brain-type faviids and branching *Acropora*, and Tridacna is confirmed here. A safe asset set: massive domes 0.3–2 m, broken branching-coral rubble (finger-thick sticks), brain-coral heads with meandering grooves, Tridacna shells 20–60 cm with wavy, scalloped edges, small mussels and horn snails as decals. The papers do not confirm Porites or Acropora at Danakil sites.
- **Signature Danakil reef look [well-sourced core, inferred styling]:** thick pinkish-cream coralline algal crusts (layered and knobbly), brain-textured microbial domes up to 1 m, finger-columns up to 6 cm, and cavities filled with later gypsum crystals. This "reef choked by gypsum" look ties zone (a) to zone (b) and fits the story.
- **Weathering [INFERENCE, not sourced for the Danakil]:** raised carbonate reefs in hot deserts typically show honeycomb or tafoni pitting, sharp karren-like solution edges, a darker case-hardened rind on cliff faces, and patchy dark desert varnish on exposed upper surfaces. Expect a salt-weathering frost of white efflorescence near the base, where saline groundwater wicks up.
- **Colour [INFERENCE]:** fresh reef limestone is cream to pale buff. Coralline algal framestone, once recrystallised to calcite, reads pale pink-buff. Varnished and case-hardened faces go tan to ochre-brown. Lava-gravel alluvium on top is dark grey-black. That dark cap on a cream cliff is a strong silhouette.

### Gaps
- No source found gives coral genera or species for Danakil reefs (Bonatti et al. 1971, Lalou et al. 1970 and the Rime et al. 2024 *Sedimentology* full text were not readable).
- No measured tread widths or riser heights in m a.s.l. for individual terraces (Antiquity Fig. 1c has a profile but no numbers in the text).
- No sourced description of honeycomb weathering, desert varnish or colour on Danakil reef rock. Field photos would be needed (see the photo list in §5).
- The reefs are said to be attached to volcanic rock, but no source describes how the reef-basalt contact looks in the field.

## 2. Gypsum: pavement, crusts, karst, selenite, layered gypsum-anhydrite and the evaporite sequence

### Takeaway
In the Danakil, gypsum sits stratigraphically between the reefs and the halite. It covers reefs locally, fills reef pores with large crystals, forms an "extensive gypsum plain" northeast of the pan, and is interbedded with halite in the salt canyons. The basin holds up to roughly 1 km (locally possibly over 2 km) of evaporites, with a potash-bearing middle. Danakil-specific gypsum surface textures (polygons, blisters, roses) are poorly documented online. Shapes and sizes have to come from labelled analogues (Abu Dhabi, White Sands).

### Cited Findings
**Danakil sequence**
- From the bottom up: clays and conglomerates at the base; Lower Rock Salt; the Houston Formation (potash layers of kainite, carnallite and bischofite) capped by Marker Beds of halite and anhydrite; then Upper Rock Salt. The salts fill "the upper hundreds of meters" of the rift fill, "probably more than 2 km locally", thickening eastward. This is Holwerda and Hutchinson 1968, as summarised by Rauche and van der Klauw 2015. Lalou et al. 1970 gave a Pleistocene age — [Frontiers in Earth Science 2023, Yellow Lake Fissure / Dallol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1250702/full)
- One Pleistocene unit is mapped as "diatomite and gypsum" (after Hagos et al. 2016) — [Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1250702/full)
- Evaporite sequences up to about 1,000 m thick fill the basin centre. Evaporites lie over the reefs and also between reef units, showing that the sea dried out repeatedly. Salt "pillars" are alternating salt layers and thin clay films, probably one layer per annual evaporation cycle — [Eos, Reading history in Afar](https://eos.org/science-updates/reading-history-afar)
- A 625 m borehole in the basin centre is mostly halite-dominated evaporites, with clastic and carbonate beds, sapropel layers and magnesite from an older marine incursion. Several hundred metres of continental evaporites were laid down between the two marine events. The basin floor is about −120 m — [Rime et al., EGU2020-14917](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-14917.html)
- Gypsum lies over both reef successions, but over the MIS 7 reef it survives in only a few outcrops. Later large gypsum crystals fill pore spaces in the reef — [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- The Dallol pan floor is halite with lesser gypsum. The pan is over 40 km long and 10 km wide. An "extensive gypsum plain" lies NE of the pan — [Warren, Saltwork Consultants](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/dallol-danakhil-ethiopia/)
- Platy gypsum crystals occur in the bottom sediment of Lake Afrera, and halite, gypsum and sulphides may still be forming in the lake — [Bonatti et al. 2017, *Heliyon*](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/d51b78f1-0c13-459a-9e76-d848b3d18435/prod_416479-doc_146811.pdf)
- Photo evidence: the "salt canyons" near Dallol are captioned as made of "layers of halite and gypsum" — [Eric Lafforgue gallery, image 71700](https://ericlafforgue.com/gallery/countries/ethiopia/71700-Ethiopian+soldiers+in+the+salt+canyons+made+of+layers+of+halite+and+gypsum+in+the+danakil+depression%2C+Afar+region%2C+Dallol%2C+Ethiopia)
- Nearby salt-crust textures (useful at the gypsum-to-salt transition): salt polygons several cm thick with clay ridges and tilted, tepee-like plates; a red salt crust up to about 50 cm high; brown clay ridges — [Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1250702/full)

**Gypsum crust and crystal forms [ANALOGUE: Abu Dhabi desert soils, Shahid and Abdelfattah 2009]** — [Gypsum Polymorphism in the Desert Environment of Abu Dhabi Emirate](https://ramsap.biosaline.org/sites/default/files/publicationsfile/gypsum_polymorphism_in_the_desert_environment.pdf)
- Powdery surface on exposed porous gypsum. A **5 cm hard surface capping** of jumbled weathered lenticular crystals, reddish from iron staining, with a 5 cm gypsum hardpan at 30 cm depth.
- "Honeycomb" (puffed) gypsum in cemented horizons. It dissolves easily and can cause **subsidence** (a karst hazard).
- Massive outcrop gypsum is light reddish or pinkish and often half-buried in blown sand. Pure massive gypsum (over 90%) is whitish.
- **Desert roses:** pure gypsum roses have shiny surfaces and petals from under 1 cm to 5 cm across. Sand roses are concentric rosettes full of sand. Inland roses are pinkish, iron-rich and red-sand-laden.
- **Lenticular crystals:** greenish lens-shaped plates 5–40 cm across and 2–5 cm thick. Smaller ones (about 1 cm) cover 30–40% of the ground in places.
- **Fans:** white, 3–4 cm wide, **standing 30–40 cm** above the ground where wind has stripped the soil from around them.
- **Tubular:** tubes 5–15 cm long formed in old root channels. **Bottlebrush:** gypsum grown over dead plant material in a hypersaline basin.
- Fibrous satin spar (silky lustre), and euhedral sandy crystals 1–2 cm long, some twinned.

**Selenite [ANALOGUE: White Sands / Lake Lucero, New Mexico]**
- Selenite crystals form beneath the clay-silt crust of the Alkali Flat and in the mud of Lake Lucero. Freeze-thaw breaks large crystals into chunks, then into sand. Fresh fragments are transparent and turn white as wind-driven collisions scratch them — [NPS Lake Lucero](https://home.nps.gov/whsa/learn/nature/lake-lucero.htm)
- Larger crystals are "long jagged spars" and "round segmented disks the size of bicycle tires" (about 60–70 cm). Gypsum is softer than a fingernail. Wind lifts thin flakes ("cornflakes") — [NPS Geology of White Sands Dunefield 2021](https://home.nps.gov/whsa/learn/upload/Geology_of_White_Sands_Dunefield_2021_FINAL.pdf)
- Selenite crystals there reach up to 3 ft (about 0.9 m) long — [DesertUSA, White Sands](https://www.desertusa.com/wsand/du_wsmdesc.html)
- A thin crust of gypsum, halite and Epsom salt can form on the playa surface and then be swept away by wind. Steep gullies on the Lake Lucero trail expose selenite in the old lake floor — [NPS Lake Lucero](https://home.nps.gov/whsa/learn/nature/lake-lucero.htm)

### Inferences
- **Feel and breakage [INFERENCE from the hardness fact]:** gypsum (Mohs 2) can be scratched with a fingernail, crunches and powders underfoot, and splits along one perfect cleavage into thin flexible sheets (selenite) or silky fibres (satin spar). Good material for footstep audio (soft crunch, not the sharp crack of halite) and for "flake-off" break VFX.
- **Gypsum steps between reef and salt [INFERENCE from Warren's "reef rim then lower gypsum rim" and Jaramillo-Vogel's gypsum-over-reef]:** a lower, lighter bench of bedded gypsum. White-to-cream laminated layers in 1–30 cm bands, with brownish clay partings, outcropping as stepped low scarps of about 0.5–3 m. Their tops weather to a powdery, polygon-cracked pavement.
- **Polygons and blisters [INFERENCE / general evaporite knowledge, not sourced here]:** desiccation and expansion polygons about 0.3–3 m across with raised rims. Hydration of anhydrite to gypsum causes swelling, which makes domed **tumuli** (blister mounds, often hollow) from tens of cm to a few m across, typically studied at Sorbas, Spain [ANALOGUE]. Dimensions are **not sourced** in these notes.
- **Sheen [INFERENCE]:** selenite has a glassy to pearly lustre on cleavage faces, which suits specular glints on a mostly matte ground; satin spar has a silky lustre (silky lustre is sourced in Shahid). Weathered gypsum pavement is matte and chalky.
- The Danakil gypsum plain NE of the pan and "gypsum after reef" give a canon-safe reason for gypsum crystals growing out of reef cavities.

### Gaps
- No Danakil-specific measurements of selenite blade size, desert roses, gypsum polygons, tumuli or sinkholes were found. Rime et al. 2024 (*Sedimentology*) probably holds bottom-grown/selenite facies descriptions, but the PDF could not be read.
- Gypsum karst dimensions (tumuli, dolines) were not obtained. The Calaforra and Pulido-Bosch 1996 abstract (Sorbas) has no numbers.
- No source on anhydrite colour or banding in Danakil outcrops.

## 3. Gypsum dunes: shapes, heights, ripples, interdunes, light and colour [ANALOGUE: White Sands, New Mexico]

### Takeaway
No source found documents a true gypsum dune field in the Danakil. The "gypsum dunes off the pan" are therefore a design choice built on the White Sands analogue. White Sands has dome dunes near the source playa, barchans and transverse ridges in the centre, and plant-anchored parabolic dunes at the edges. The interdunes are damp, with a water table at 30–60 cm. The sand is snow-white when dry and tan when wet.

### Cited Findings
- About 275 sq mi (about 710 km²) of gypsum dunes. They come from Lake Lucero and the Alkali Flat, and the sand moves northeast a few inches at a time by saltation — [NPS Lake Lucero](https://home.nps.gov/whsa/learn/nature/lake-lucero.htm)
- Dune types: **dome** (low mounds, the first to form downwind of Lake Lucero, up to about 9 m/yr), **barchan** (crescents, horns pointing downwind, can move over 100 m/yr), **transverse** (long wavy ridges where barchans merge), **parabolic/U-shaped** (at the edges, arms anchored by plants, under about 2.4 m/yr). The dunes began forming about 25 ka — [New Mexico Bureau of Geology, White Sands FAQ](https://geoinfo.nmt.edu/tour/federal/parks/whitesands/faq.html)
- Dunes in the heart of the field move about 3.7–4 m (12–13 ft) per year; edge dunes move cm to under 1 m. The perched water table is 30–60 cm (1–2 ft) below the interdunal flats. Edge dunes are held by plants and soil crust, and dune hearts carry only occasional grasses. The sand looks "snowy" because scuffs on the grains reflect light — [NPS Geology of White Sands Dunefield 2021](https://home.nps.gov/whsa/learn/upload/Geology_of_White_Sands_Dunefield_2021_FINAL.pdf)
- The dunes look white from scratched crystal faces and dry sand. **After rain they look tan** until they dry — [NPS Lake Lucero](https://home.nps.gov/whsa/learn/nature/lake-lucero.htm)
- Sand-moving winds of at least 15 mph come mostly from the SW, February to April. Soaptree yucca stretches its stem up to about 30 cm a year to keep its leaves above the sand. Other plants are left standing on **sand pedestals** after the dune moves on. White-coloured animals include a pocket mouse, a lizard, camel crickets and a white grasshopper. Most wildlife is nocturnal, and morning tracks of rodents, rabbits, kit fox, coyote and porcupine are visible — [DesertUSA, White Sands](https://www.desertusa.com/wsand/du_wsmdesc.html)
- The bleached earless lizard is about 10 cm long, flat-bodied, white with two black side spots. It buries itself in sand in the midday heat, and evolved its white colour in the roughly 7,000 years the dunefield has existed — [NPS Bleached Earless Lizard](https://www.nps.gov/whsa/learn/nature/bleachedearlesslizard.htm)

### Inferences
- **Dune heights [INFERENCE / commonly reported, not verified here]:** White Sands dunes are widely quoted at up to about 18 m (60 ft) and mostly 3–10 m. For isometric zones, use dune slopes of 3–10 m (slip faces about 32–34°, the normal angle of repose for dry sand) with long gentle windward backs.
- **Ripples [INFERENCE]:** wind ripples typically 5–15 cm apart and 0.5–1 cm high on windward slopes. They are a good normal-map detail and catch low sun.
- **Interdune [INFERENCE from the shallow water table]:** firm, damp, slightly darker grey-cream flats, with a thin efflorescent or cyanobacterial crust, polygonal cracks, plant pedestals, and buried root mats exposed in the lee of a migrating dune.
- **Light [INFERENCE]:** gypsum dunes reflect very strongly. Shadows on slip faces pick up a strong blue cast from the sky, and at sunrise and sunset the white takes the sky colour (peach, pink, lavender). Midday is flat and blinding. This "colour-catching white" is the key beauty asset. Dunes that stay cool underfoot are commonly reported (gypsum reflects light and the moist base evaporates water), but this is **not sourced** here.
- **Danakil fit [INFERENCE]:** a small dune patch (hundreds of m to a few km) downwind of the gypsum plain, with dome and barchan forms only, fits a 100 km basin better than a White Sands-scale erg. The ±30–60 cm water table makes a good reason for brackish seep-pools in the interdunes.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found for gypsum dunes in the Danakil itself. The map's dunes are an authored feature on an analogue basis.
- NPS gives no sourced dune heights, ripple sizes or sand temperatures in the pages fetched.

## 4. Saline mineral pools: colours, rims, crystals, smell

### Takeaway
The Danakil pools that are famous for colour (Dallol) are acidic and hot. The map's pools are deliberately saline but not acidic, closer to the ambient-temperature salt-flat brines and the "green-tinted open-air pond" at Lake Asale than to Dallol's yellow-green acid pools. Sourced chemistry: salt-flat pore brines at air temperature hold over 260 g/L of dissolved salts. Black Mountain pools reach a moderate pH of about 3–6.6 and are rich in Mg-K-Cl.

### Cited Findings
- Salt-flat pore brines: at ambient temperature, over 260,000 mg/L total dissolved ions, held in halite-dominated pan sediment. Dallol Mound springs: about 100–110 °C, pH near 1 or lower, with sulphur and Fe-Mn giving "spectacular colouration". Black Mountain dolines: 50–70 °C, pH about 3–3.5 (a 1926 pool measured about 110 °C, about 426 g/L, pH about 6.6); low sulphate and high Cl-Mg-K favour bischofite and carnallite — [Warren, Saltwork Consultants](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/dallol-danakhil-ethiopia/)
- Lake Asale at noon: blinding white salt with a green-tinted open-air pond, and the lake surface mirroring the sky in the morning. Dallol has deep-blue acidic ponds, vivid greens and yellows, amber, brown oxidation patches and sulphurous gas — [Eastern Backpacker trip report, Nov 2023](https://www.zuyetawarmatrip.com/en/trips/2023-nov-hamedela-dallol-ertaale-ethiopia/)
- Hot-spring carbonates line some lake and brine-pool deposits, and stromatolites and thrombolites grow at the edges of ancient and modern hypersaline lakes — [Eos](https://eos.org/science-updates/reading-history-afar)
- Halite, gypsum and sulphides may be precipitating in Lake Afrera today, and platy gypsum crystals occur in its bottom sediments — [Bonatti et al. 2017](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/d51b78f1-0c13-459a-9e76-d848b3d18435/prod_416479-doc_146811.pdf)
- [ANALOGUE: Lake Lucero] the playa holds water about 10 sq mi (about 26 km²) in area after heavy rain. A thin crust of gypsum, halite and Epsom salt forms, and selenite grows in the mud under the lake — [NPS Lake Lucero](https://home.nps.gov/whsa/learn/nature/lake-lucero.htm)

### Inferences
- **Colour cause [INFERENCE]:** the dull blue-grey teal of a non-acidic brine pool comes from depth and sky reflection over a white floor, fine suspended clay or gypsum, and possibly salt-tolerant microbes. Greener tints suggest algal or microbial films; pinkish edges in very salty shallows would suggest halophilic archaea (common in salterns worldwide, not sourced for these pools).
- **Rims [INFERENCE from the Lake Afrera / Lucero findings]:** gypsum rims as raised lips 2–20 cm high of white platy or bladed crystals. On the brine side, a halite crust of floating "rafts", cubic hopper crystals and a ring of white efflorescence. Under the shallows, bladed selenite rosettes of 2–20 cm in grey mud. A microbial-mat collar of brown-green laminated film, with small stromatolite knobs at the margin (sourced as occurring at Danakil lake margins).
- **Smell [INFERENCE]:** non-acidic saline pools mostly smell of hot salt and brine, with a sulphide (rotten-egg) note where black anoxic mud is disturbed. This is not the sharp acid and sulphur smell of Dallol.

### Gaps
- No sourced colour or chemistry for a specific non-acidic, gypsum-rimmed pool in the Danakil. The "Lake Asale green pond" is the only eyewitness colour note.
- No crystal-size data for Danakil pool-rim gypsum.

## 5. Vegetation, wildlife, human traces, air and light, and reference photos

### Takeaway
Reef rock and gypsum in the Danakil are essentially barren. Life clusters in wash channels and around Hamed Ela, where palm-leaf thatch shows there are palms nearby. The defining human trace is not quarrying but salt mining: blocks cut with axes and hoes and carried away by camel caravans. Reef-stone quarrying, lime burning and fossil collecting in the Danakil were not documented in any source found. The air: about 34 °C average, near 40 °C by 10 a.m. at Dallol, with rain almost never falling.

### Cited Findings
- Hamed Ela: women and children gather palm leaves to thatch roofs, golden goats graze among the rocks, and palm groves and shrubs grow by a dry riverbed where camels are kept. Patches of yellow dry grass and small wildflowers grow on the lava route south — [Eastern Backpacker trip report](https://www.zuyetawarmatrip.com/en/trips/2023-nov-hamedela-dallol-ertaale-ethiopia/)
- Salt is hacked out of the crust with traditional axes and hoes and carried by camel caravans on a 4-day round trip to Berahile (2 days' walk each way). The average temperature is 94 °F (about 34 °C), rain almost never falls, and there is a paved road from Mekele to Dallol — [VOA, Ethiopia's salt trade](https://www.voanews.com/a/ethiopia-salt-trade-danakil-depression-afar-mining-extraction-industry/1662972.html)
- Dallol reached nearly 40 °C by 10 a.m., and the white ground reflects intense sun — [Eastern Backpacker](https://www.zuyetawarmatrip.com/en/trips/2023-nov-hamedela-dallol-ertaale-ethiopia/)
- Middle Stone Age stone tools (obsidian, basalt, chert) lie on the reefs at Girawi'en — [Antiquity 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/archaeological-reconnaissance-of-the-late-pleistocene-red-sea-coast-in-the-danakil/6A77DA823BE599877B0920ECEF4A571C/core-reader)

Reef benches and gypsum pavement: almost bare, a few dead stems salt-crusted into "bottlebrush" gypsum (sourced as a real form in Abu Dhabi). Pan: nothing grows. Check the species against the vault's flora notes before use.
- **Wildlife signs [INFERENCE]:** goat and camel tracks and dung along washes, lizard drag-marks, raptor perches on reef ledges, and insect burrows in damp interdunes.

- **Air [INFERENCE]:** heat shimmer and mirage over the pan (mirage is in the photo titles), a pale gypsum haze when wind lifts the "cornflake" flakes (sourced at White Sands), and hard white glare at midday.

## 6. Synthesis for environment art: per-zone inventory, palette, asset seeds, beauty and grit (mostly inference built on the findings above)

### Takeaway
Three zones step down eastward: **Fossil Terraces** (cream coralgal cliffs with dark lava-gravel caps, giant-clam and brain-crust detail, gypsum-choked cavities), **Gypsum Steps** (laminated white-cream low scarps, chalky polygon pavement, selenite glints), and the **salt-flat edge** (halite, teal brine pools with gypsum lips). Rust-red wash channels cut between the benches, and gypsum dunes stand off the pan. Each list below is built from the cited findings in §1–5. The styling choices are inference.

### Cited Findings
- Reefs: 3–8 m thick, ledges and rolling blocks, lava-gravel cover, Tridacna, *Brachidontes*, *Pirenella*, serpulids, red-algal framestone up to 2 m, brain-like crust domes up to 1 m, columns up to 6 cm, gypsum-filled pores, *Gastrochaenolites* borings on the erosion surface — [Antiquity 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/archaeological-reconnaissance-of-the-late-pleistocene-red-sea-coast-in-the-danakil/6A77DA823BE599877B0920ECEF4A571C/core-reader); [Jaramillo-Vogel et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/200256845.pdf)
- Rims step down from reef to gypsum; a gypsum plain NE of the pan — [Warren](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/dallol-danakhil-ethiopia/)
- Gypsum forms and sizes (roses up to 5 cm, fans 30–40 cm high, lenticular plates 5–40 cm, selenite disks about 60–70 cm and up to about 0.9 m) — [Shahid and Abdelfattah 2009](https://ramsap.biosaline.org/sites/default/files/publicationsfile/gypsum_polymorphism_in_the_desert_environment.pdf); [NPS 2021](https://home.nps.gov/whsa/learn/upload/Geology_of_White_Sands_Dunefield_2021_FINAL.pdf); [DesertUSA](https://www.desertusa.com/wsand/du_wsmdesc.html)

### Inferences
**Zone A: Fossil Terraces**
1. Ground: cream reef-flat limestone pavement, rough and pitted. Shell hash (broken shells, 0.5–3 cm). Scattered dark basalt and obsidian gravel washed over from the fans. Patches of chalky gypsum fill.
2. Landforms: risers 3–8 m with overhangs where the soft lower beds undercut. Talus of rolling blocks 0.5–3 m. Two benches per locality. Skyline: the black basalt escarpment and rift wall behind, with a white pan glow on the eastern horizon.
3. Water: dry. Optional seeps at the base of a riser, with a white efflorescent tide line.
4. Vegetation: almost none on the benches. Dead, salt-crusted shrub skeletons.
5. Wildlife signs: goat tracks, raptor whitewash on ledges.
6. Human traces: Middle Stone Age flakes on the reef (sourced), cairns, stone hut walls of reef blocks [INFERENCE].
7. Air and light: shimmer, low-angle light raking across honeycomb faces.
- **Asset seeds:** a modular reef-cliff kit (3 m and 6 m risers, corners, overhang); 6–10 fallen reef boulders; massive coral dome props (0.3–2 m); brain-crust microbial dome (up to 1 m); finger-column clusters (6 cm columns); Tridacna shell props (20–60 cm, open and closed); mussel/horn-snail/serpulid-tube decals; a *Gastrochaenolites* boring decal (flask-shaped holes, 1–5 cm); a gypsum-crystal cavity insert (selenite blades growing out of reef vugs); a lava-gravel cap blend material; a desert-varnish streak decal.

**Zone B: Gypsum Steps**
1. Ground: chalky white gypsum pavement with polygon cracks 0.3–3 m, a powdery top and crunchy sub-crust. Lenticular-crystal litter (about 1 cm crystals covering 30–40% of the ground, sourced in the analogue). Desert roses (1–5 cm petals). Flat selenite plates catching light.
2. Landforms: laminated gypsum scarps 0.5–3 m with clay partings. Blister and tumulus domes (some broken open to show hollow interiors). Small sinkholes as a hazard. Gypsum fans standing 30–40 cm proud of the ground.
3. Water: rare. Brine seeps at the scarp foot.
4–5. Vegetation: none on pavement. Gypsum "bottlebrush" casts of dead plants. Lizard tracks.
- **Asset seeds:** gypsum pavement material (2–3 variants: chalky, polygon, rose-studded); laminated scarp kit; a selenite blade cluster (blades 10–90 cm); a "bicycle-tyre" selenite disk (about 65 cm); a desert-rose scatter; a tumulus dome (intact and collapsed); a sinkhole decal and mesh; satin-spar vein decals; wind-lifted gypsum "cornflake" flake VFX; footstep dust puff VFX.

**Zone C: Pools and pan edge**
- Asset seeds: a teal brine water shader (opaque-ish, reflective, low wave amplitude); a gypsum rim lip (2–20 cm raised white platy crust); halite raft and hopper-cube floating crusts; a submerged selenite-rosette floor; a microbial-mat collar (brown-green laminated ring) with stromatolite knobs; a white efflorescence tide-line decal; heat haze and mirage VFX; salt-mining props (cut pits, stacked blocks, staves, ropes, camel saddle frames).

**Gypsum dunes (off-pan, analogue-based)**
- Asset seeds: dome and barchan dune meshes (3–10 m) with 32–34° slip faces; ripple normal maps (5–15 cm wavelength); a damp interdune material with polygon crust; plant-on-pedestal props (a 0.5–2 m sand pedestal with a shrub on top); stretched-stem yucca-like plants [ANALOGUE only: replace with a Danakil-plausible species]; a spindrift VFX of sand blowing off crests; a white lizard critter.

**Wash channels between benches**
- Rust-red [INFERENCE: iron-oxide clay and weathered basalt fines] braided channels 2–20 m wide with lava cobbles, cracked red mud plates, and doum palms and acacias where groundwater allows. This is the zone's colour counterpoint.

**Beauty moments [INFERENCE]**
- Looking east from a reef ledge: cream benches stepping down to a blazing white pan with a mirror-like brine sheen (the morning mirror of Lake Asale is sourced).
