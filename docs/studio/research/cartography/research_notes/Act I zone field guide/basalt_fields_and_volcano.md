# Basalt Fields and the Volcano: Erta Ale and Afar lava fields as a visual basis

Scope: two Act I zones. **Basalt Fields** = weathered older basalt lobes on the basin floor below the fault wall, plus fresh charcoal-black tongues reaching down onto them. **Volcano** = an Erta Ale-style shield on the north fault block with a summit caldera, pit craters and a small (play-exaggerated) lava lake.

Conventions: "Cited Findings" are sourced facts (paraphrased). "Inferences" are my reasoning or general volcanology knowledge I could not source in this pass; treat them as art-direction proposals, not facts. Hawaiian material is labelled **[analogue: Hawaii]**. Hex values are always inference.

Important currency note up front: Erta Ale changed hard in July 2025 (pit craters collapsed, lake collapse reported) and a neighbouring volcano, Hayli Gubbi, erupted in November 2025. Most photo reference of "the lava lake" predates this. See Q3.

## Q1. Lava surfaces at walking scale (centimetres to a few metres)

### Takeaway
Erta Ale's own ground is mostly pahoehoe: fine ropes, folded and twisted crusts, small toes and miniature tubes, with Pele's hair and pale ash lodged between the ropes. Fresh basalt is black to dark grey with a glassy sheen. With age the iron oxidises and it goes lighter and redder. White wind-blown ash and dust settles into the cracks and hollows. A'a and clinker are not described in the Erta Ale sources I found, so for a'a surfaces use the labelled Hawaiian analogues.

### Cited Findings
- The caldera floor is barren and covered in black pahoehoe; one group camped on it for six days — [SwissEduc virtual walk](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/virtualwalk/index-en.html?id=5)
- Near the summit camp there are small pahoehoe features, very small lava ropes, several pahoehoe types side by side, a miniature lava tube, and fine light-coloured ash settled between the ropes — [SwissEduc lava patterns 2002](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/lava-en.html)
- Pele's hair (fine volcanic glass strands) lies among wavy pahoehoe ridges at the summit, and large parts of the area are covered by intricately folded and twisted crust of older pahoehoe — [Volcano Adventures, Nov 2015 expedition photos](https://www.volcanoadventures.com/tours/ethiopia/nov2015/impressions/p5.html)
- Large lake bubbles fling lava into the air, and it lands as sticky black blobs that cool to rock. This is a spatter source at the pit rim — [Stanley Stewart, Condé Nast Traveller (PDF)](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf)
- The hike up is over large rocks, and the crossing of the solidified flow inside the caldera is rough ground — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia); [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf)
- Colour versus age (Afar, Landsat): young basalt such as Alayta's lava reads black or dark grey. Over time its iron oxidises and it turns lighter and redder. Erta Ale feeds the lava field NW of Lake Afrera, and Borawli's older lava lies to the east — [NASA Earth Observatory, "Lava around Lake Afrera"](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/lava-around-lake-afrera-84081/)
- In the Dabbahu rift (Afar), dark basalt flows are streaked and cut by white wind-blown ash and dust, with buff ash bands on fresh fault surfaces — [Univ. of Leeds Dabbahu photo page](https://homepages.see.leeds.ac.uk/~eartjw/dabbahu/photos.html)
- The 2017 flank flows were numerous pahoehoe flows fed by lava tubes that broke out at their ends. A'a is not mentioned — [Smithsonian GVP, Erta Ale](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080)
- **[analogue: Hawaii]** Pahoehoe is thin, smooth or ropy, low-viscosity lava. Ropes form when the moving lava drags its cooling crust into ridges. *Shelly* pahoehoe forms in gas-rich flows: thin crusts over open blisters and tubes. *Slabby* pahoehoe is jumbled, overturned plates of broken crust and grades into a'a. *Spiny* pahoehoe is also transitional. Pahoehoe toes are small lobes at a flow front, usually up to a few feet wide, and they inflate and merge. A'a is thick and rough, with a jagged clinkery top and base over a dense interior. A'a is the most common basalt flow type and travels between levees in channels — [NPS, Lava flow forms](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/lava-flow-forms.htm)

### Inferences
- **Fresh flows (0 to about 10 years; the "charcoal-black tongues").** Jet to charcoal black. Fresh pahoehoe crusts often carry a thin glassy skin that gives a silver-grey to faintly blue or bronze iridescent sheen in raking light. On the brightest days this sheen is the key "beauty" read. The skin is brittle: shelly crusts 1-5 cm thick collapse underfoot into hollows 10-50 cm deep. Ropes are typically 2-10 cm wide in bands 0.5-2 m across. Toes are 0.2-1 m. Entrail pahoehoe (sausage-like tubes, about 10-30 cm thick) appears where lava oozed down steeper slopes or pit walls. Pele's hair mats (golden, finger-length to tens of cm) collect in the lee of rocks downwind of the vent, and Pele's tears (mm-sized black glass drops) collect in hollows. These sizes are general volcanology knowledge, unsourced here.
- **A'a analogue, if wanted for variety on steeper fronts.** Clinker from fist size to about 1 m blocks. Flows 2-10 m thick with a rubble front. Not documented at Erta Ale in my sources, so use it sparingly or for the steeper fault-block slope.
- **Old lobes (hundreds to thousands of years; the "weathered older basalt").** The glass skin spalls off, and the surface goes matte dark grey-brown, then rust-brown. Desert varnish (a dark brown-black manganese/iron film) builds on stable upper faces, while the undersides of blocks stay greyer. Ropes soften but stay readable. Cracks and hollows fill with pale buff or white wind-blown silt and ash, which makes a strong black-and-cream crack pattern. Iron staining bleeds into the silt. The NASA "redder with age" finding supports the colour direction, but the varnish timescale (generally centuries to millennia in deserts) is unsourced here.
- **Few years versus thousands.** Within a few years the sheen dulls, glass hair breaks up and blows away, and dust begins filling cracks. Over thousands of years the surface goes red-brown with varnish, crack fill turns to continuous sandy pans, and lobes break up into slabs and blocks.

### Gaps
- No Afar-specific measurement of rope, toe, shelly-crust or clinker sizes was found. The sizes above are analogue or general knowledge.
- Whether a'a occurs on Erta Ale's 2017 or 2025 flows: GVP mentions only pahoehoe and tubes for 2017, and COMET does not give the 2025 flow type.
- No source on iridescence or desert-varnish ages for Afar basalts specifically.

## Q2. Landforms (1-100 m) and skyline (km)

### Takeaway
At 1-100 m the documented Afar forms are hornitos (7 m to "dozens of metres"), fissure rows of hornitos, lava tubes with skylights and tube-fed breakouts, and, from the rifting, fields of open cracks about 0.5 m wide and fault scarps over 3 m high. At km scale, Erta Ale is a very low, broad shield about 50 km across with only about 500 m of relief, and other shield silhouettes line the horizon. Tumuli, pressure ridges, squeeze-ups and kipukas are not described in my Afar sources, so they come from analogue knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- **Shield scale.** Erta Ale is a basaltic shield about 50 km across at the base, with about 500 m of height (1971 report). Its summit caldera is elliptical, about 0.7 x 1.6 km, elongated NNW-SSE, with North and South pit craters in its northern half. A larger 1.8 x 3.1 km depression lies SE of the summit, bounded by curved fault scarps — [Smithsonian GVP](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080)
- **Skyline.** From the rim at dawn, the silhouettes of other shield volcanoes stand on the horizon, and a "look-out hill" rises inside the caldera — [Volcano Adventures 2015](https://www.volcanoadventures.com/tours/ethiopia/nov2015/impressions/p5.html)
- **Hornitos.**
  - The 2008 route crossed hornitos on a fissure from a recent flank eruption on the western flank — [SwissEduc climb 2008](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/climb-2008-en.html)
  - April 2017: two coalesced hornitos about 7 m high stood in the South Pit, giving off SO2 and occasional lava. December 2022: a hornito about 10 m high grew in the South Pit. By March 2023 it was "dozens of metres" high, and two large hornitos were building in the North Crater. Actively spattering hornitos also stood on the new 2017 field — [GVP](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080)
- **Tubes and skylights.** The 16 km NE flow of 2017 had skylights and overflows (December 2017 to January 2018), and later breakouts occurred 10-15 km from the vent — [GVP](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080)
- **Flow-field scale (2017-18).**
  - The 21 January 2017 SE-flank fissure opened 3-4 km from the South crater.
  - The NE flow field was about 1.4 km wide, over 7 km long by April and more than 16 km long by October 2017. It advanced onto the alluvial plain by March 2018.
  - The SW field reached about 5.1 km by July 2017.
  - In April 2017 a lava lake about 80 x 175 m formed at the flank vent.
  - Source: [GVP](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080)
- **2025 fissure.** A line of vents about 1.5 km long, inside and outside the caldera, fed flows about 1.1 km west and 2.3 km east. A dike then ran about 30 km SE toward Hayli Gubbi and Lake Afrera, and a graben about 2 km wide subsided along its southern part — [COMET event report 2.1 (preliminary)](https://comet.nerc.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/COMET_EventReport2.1_ErtaAle.pdf)
- **Rift cracks (Dabbahu 2005, Afar).**
  - The 60 km rift segment opened by up to 8 m in about 3 weeks.
  - A vent about 60 m wide opened explosively beside a 200 m wide, 4 km long zone of open fissures and normal faults.
  - Cracks and faults about 0.5 m wide formed above the dike.
  - Fault scarps moved more than 3 m; fresh slip shows as a buff band on the scarp face.
  - A later vent was about 500 m long, with layered ash, a tunnel at its south end, and blown-out pale rhyolitic blocks.
  - Source: [Leeds Dabbahu photos](https://homepages.see.leeds.ac.uk/~eartjw/dabbahu/photos.html)
- **[analogue: Hawaii]** Inflation of pahoehoe toes into broad fronts, tube formation under an insulating crust, and channels between levees — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/lava-flow-forms.htm)

### Inferences
- **Analogue sizes (general knowledge, unsourced here).**
  - Tumuli: domed blisters 1-5 m high and 5-30 m long, with a gaping axial crack 0.3-1 m wide.
  - Pressure ridges: 1-5 m high, tens to 100+ m long.
  - Squeeze-ups: 0.3-2 m bulbous or toothpaste extrusions from cracks.
  - Skylights: 1-10 m holes onto glowing tubes.
  - Collapse pits over drained tubes: 5-50 m across, 2-15 m deep, with rubble floors.
  - Spatter cones: 3-20 m, with a pasty spatter-agglutinate skin.
  - Kipukas (islands of older ground surrounded by younger flow): 10 m to km. **Kipukas are the best device for the map brief:** islands of rust-brown old lobe, carrying scrub and dust, ringed by fresh black.
  - Flow fronts: 1-3 m (pahoehoe) or 3-10 m (a'a) high.
  - Levees: 1-5 m.
- **Gaining-ground fissures (good gameplay).** Use the Dabbahu sizes directly: crack fields 0.3-1 m wide (jumpable or blocking), occasional gaping fissures several metres wide where opening concentrates, and scarps 1-5 m high stepping the floor. All cut across both old and young basalt, with white dust sifting into them.
- **Map divergence.** The real Erta Ale is a low shield on the rift floor, not perched on an uplifted fault block. For the brief's north-fault-block volcano, keep the shield shape (gentle 2-6° slopes, low broad skyline, not a cone). Have flows tumble over the fault wall as steeper slabby or a'a cascades and lava falls. That is a plausible exaggeration.

### Gaps
- No Afar source in this pass describing tumuli, pressure ridges, squeeze-ups, kipukas or collapse-pit sizes. Treat them as analogue.
- Summit elevation above sea level was not verified (a 1971 relief figure of about 500 m only).

## Q3. Erta Ale itself: caldera, pits, lava lake, rim, hike, huts, 2017+ changes, other centres

### Takeaway
The iconic scene is a pit 150-350 m wide inside a 0.7 x 1.6 km caldera of black pahoehoe. In it, a dark crust is broken into plates, with glowing orange-red cracks that spread and subduct at centimetres per second. Gas bubbles burst every few minutes, flinging spatter metres to tens of metres high, and hornitos build lava stalactites on the shore. Visitors walk about 10 km and 3 hours at night from the Afar village of Dodom, with camels and armed escorts, sleep in stone huts on the rim, and watch at dusk and dawn. The 2017 flank eruption drained the lake by 80-100 m and sent pahoehoe 16 km. July 2025 collapsed both pits, by at least 170 m in the North pit, so the "lake" in current reality may be absent or changed. That gives the game licence to exaggerate.

### Cited Findings
- **Pit and lake dimensions over time.**
  - North pit about 200 x 300 m (1971).
  - South crater 160 x 130 m (2002), 190 x 150 m (2009), 180 x 140 m (2010).
  - South lake about 200 m across (2021-22).
  - New North Pit lake about 350 m across and 15 m deep (January 2023).
  - South lake about 5 m below the rim in March 2023.
  - Lakes have been present since 1967, and possibly since 1906.
  - Source: [GVP](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080)
- **Lake surface behaviour.**
  - Dark solidified crust slabs keep shifting over a bright orange glow, with bright orange lava in the cracks and spatter tens of metres high (2022-23).
  - Lake level cycled every ~30 minutes between 40 and 50 m below the caldera floor (April 2017).
  - Spatter rose up to 30 m in gas-bubble explosions (January 2017).
  - Source: [GVP](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080)
- **Crust "plate tectonics".**
  - Crust sinks at one shore (NW in 2008) while glowing lava squeezes up through cracks.
  - Stressed crust stretches until red shows through, and plates sometimes override one another, exposing an incandescent leading edge.
  - Crust too thick to sink piles up on the shore as shiny folds.
  - Upwelling at the opposite shore (SE) lifts glossy crust and spills bright yellow lava over darker crust.
  - Waves reach about 1 m. Big bubble waves build lava stalactites on the lips of a shore hornito and erode its vent.
  - Photos were taken from about 70 m away.
  - Source: [SwissEduc lake tectonics 2008](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/lake-subduction-2008-en.html)
  - The crust breaks into slabs edged by bright lines like a jigsaw, and the molten layer moves at centimetres per second — [Highly Allochthonous / all-geo](https://all-geo.org/highlyallochthonous/?p=3628)
  - Glowing "rift zones" streak like lightning across the surface, sometimes three meeting at the centre. Fountains pull the lake surface in toward themselves. At dusk, blue sky light reflects off the dark lava — [SwissEduc lava patterns](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/lava-en.html)
- **Colours.** Fresh lava is bright yellow, cooled crust darker, and stressed zones red — [SwissEduc 2008](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/lake-subduction-2008-en.html). In photos, short exposures make fountains red and long ones yellow — [SwissEduc lava patterns](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/lava-en.html). At dusk the zigzag cracks and the lake margin glow red while the crust goes dark — [Volcano Adventures 2015](https://www.volcanoadventures.com/tours/ethiopia/nov2015/impressions/p5.html)
- **Observer experience.**
  - Red and black veins slide over each other like a churning sea, and big bubbles swell and burst every few minutes.
  - The heat feels like standing a few feet from hot coals, and the lava light turns faces red.
  - Above the summit the sky glows red, with sparks.
  - Source: [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf)
  - The lake "blisters and explodes" across a wide area, a glow rises above the crater rim, and the lake smoked over after about an hour. "Erta Ale" means "smoking mountain" in Afar — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia)
- **Hike and camp.**
  - The walk starts from Dodom, an Afar village with wells at the foot. It is about 10 km by camel caravan over the western flank, past hornitos, to shelters on the western caldera rim — [SwissEduc climb 2008](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/climb-2008-en.html)
  - Hikes start around 6 pm to avoid the heat and take about 3 hours over large rocks. Guests sleep on thin mattresses in a stone Afar hut, or under the stars on makeshift wooden beds — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia)
  - Camels carry luggage, food, mattresses and camping gear and are unloaded at the rim — [Volcano Adventures](https://www.volcanoadventures.com/tours/ethiopia/nov2015/impressions/p5.html); [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf)
  - Viewers watch from the vent rim or the old caldera rim at dusk and dawn — [Volcano Adventures](https://www.volcanoadventures.com/tours/ethiopia/nov2015/impressions/p5.html)
- **2016-2019 changes.**
  - Rim overflows occurred in January and November 2016. In January 2017 overflows covered about 70% of the rim and about 1 km² of the caldera floor.
  - A new oval pit (about 150 x 30 x 20 m) formed at the North crater, and the South crater roughly doubled by collapse.
  - Ash rose 700-800 m.
  - On 21 January 2017 an SE-flank fissure opened. The lake dropped 80-100 m, then recovered to 40-50 m below the rim by mid-February. By October 2018 it was 70-90 m below.
  - Source: [GVP](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080)
  - NASA's EO-1 imaged the 2017 rift eruption: a crack opening and fountaining, with flows spreading away — [NASA JPL PIA11239](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia11239-nasa-spacecraft-watches-as-eruption-reshapes-african-volcano/)
- **2025 changes.**
  - Overflows and thermal anomalies continued until July 2025.
  - On 15 July 2025 an explosive eruption sent ash and SO2 plumes to about 2 km a.s.l., and SO2 output was 4-10 kt/day.
  - Both pit craters collapsed, by at least 170 m (North) and 57 m (South). A "lava lake collapse" is reported.
  - The report is preliminary.
  - Source: [COMET report 2.1](https://comet.nerc.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/COMET_EventReport2.1_ErtaAle.pdf)
- **Other centres.**
  - Hayli Gubbi, at the SE end of the 2025 dike, erupted in late November 2025 after roughly 12,000 years without recorded activity, sending ash toward Yemen and Oman. This comes from headlines; I did not read the articles — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/11/24/ethiopian-volcano-erupts-after-lying-dormant-for-12000-years); [AP via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/gallery/news/2025/11/24/volcano-erupts-in-northern-ethiopia-sending-ash-plumes-toward-yemen-and-oman/)
  - Alayta is a large shield of about 2,700 km² with dark flows, Afdera has paler rhyolitic flows, and Borawli is an inactive stratovolcano — [NASA EO Afrera](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/lava-around-lake-afrera-84081/)

### Inferences
- **Sound (unsourced in this pass).** Expect a constant low surf-like roar or hiss from degassing, sharp cracks and pops as plates break, deep "whumps" as bubbles burst, the patter of spatter landing, and the tinkle of cooling glass crust. The GVP "noisy degassing of SO2" at hornitos supports a hissing jet sound.
- **Game lake.** A lake 30-80 m across seen from a 15-25 m screen reads well, and a 150-350 m real pit makes a great skyline or arena. Layer it as follows:
  - crust plates 1-5 m polygons;
  - glowing seams 5-30 cm wide;
  - a "spreading" shore emitting yellow-white lava;
  - a "sinking" shore with piled shiny folds;
  - a hornito on one shore with dripping stalactites;
  - bubble bursts every 1-3 minutes throwing 5-30 m spatter arcs;
  - the level cycling up and down (the real 30-minute cycle can compress to 2-3 minutes);
  - black "bathtub rings" of old high-stands on the pit wall.
- **Story hook (real basis).** Post-2025 reality has the lake dropping into deep collapsed pits. A "dying world" variant could show a lake draining away, with stepped terraces of old lake crust on the pit walls.

### Gaps
- Erta Ale's state as of November 2026 (whether a lake re-formed after July 2025) was not found.
- The 2008 Alu-Dalafilla eruption: no source fetched. Treat it as unverified that it was a November 2008 fissure eruption north of Erta Ale with large dark flows.
- No measured lake temperature in my sources. Literature values around 1,150-1,200 °C are commonly cited but unverified here.
- Summit hut construction details beyond "stone Afar hut" and "shelters on the western rim" were not found.

## Q4. Vegetation on basalt, and wildlife and signs

### Takeaway
On older Danakil lava fields, sparse thorn scrub of *Acacia mellifera* and *Rhigozum somalense* dominates. Doum palms stand in depressions and wadis, and scattered *Acacia tortilis*, *A. nubica* and *Balanites* grow on sandy plains. Young flows and the summit are barren. Fauna is sparse but iconic: wild ass, gazelles, dik-dik, geckos, a lark. Herded camels and goats are the commonest living things near people.

### Cited Findings
- **Basaltic lava fields** in the Djibouti xeric shrublands (which include the Danakil) are dominated by *Acacia mellifera* (black thorn, hook thorn) and *Rhigozum somalense*. *Acacia tortilis* (umbrella thorn), *A. nubica* and *Balanites aegyptiaca* (desert date) are scattered on sandy plains. *Hyphaene thebaica* (doum palm) grows in stands in depressions and along wadis. The Danakil has about 200 plant species, about 25 of them endemic — [One Earth ecoregion profile](https://www.oneearth.org/ecoregions/djibouti-xeric-shrublands/)
- Gravel plains carry thin scrub, and Afar camps use thornbush corrals for camels and goats — [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf)
- The caldera floor is barren black pahoehoe — [SwissEduc virtual walk](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/virtualwalk/index-en.html?id=5)
- **Fauna.**
  - Mammals: African wild ass (critically endangered, relict Danakil populations), Dorcas and Soemmerring's gazelles, Salt's dik-dik, gerenuk, beira, and reduced beisa oryx. The Berbera gerbil is endemic.
  - Birds: poor; Archer's lark is the endemic.
  - Reptiles: relatively rich, including Arnold's leaf-toed gecko and an endemic sand gecko.
  - Source: [One Earth](https://www.oneearth.org/ecoregions/djibouti-xeric-shrublands/)
- Camels carried the Dabbahu field team's equipment — [Leeds](https://homepages.see.leeds.ac.uk/~eartjw/dabbahu/photos.html)

### Inferences
- **Placement rules (my reasoning).**
  - Fresh flows (under ~50 years) get zero plants.
  - Old lobes get scrub only where wind-blown silt has filled cracks and hollows: one shrub per 10-50 m², often in a line along a crack.
  - Kipukas and lobe margins against sand get the densest scrub.
  - Doum palms only where water collects: wadi mouths below the fault wall, or seep lines at the base of flows.
- **Forms.**
  - *A. mellifera*: a multi-stem, flat-topped thicket 1-4 m, grey bark, small hooked thorns, often leafless and grey in the dry season.
  - *Rhigozum*: a rigid spiny shrub 1-2 m with small leaves.
  - *A. tortilis*: an umbrella tree 3-8 m.
  - Doum palm: forked trunks 5-15 m with fan leaves, plus a skirt of dead fronds.
  - Also include dead and goat-browsed stumps, and dry grass tufts in silt.
  - These sizes are general botany knowledge, unsourced here.
- **Wildlife signs for decals and props.** Camel and goat dung, hoof trails polished across lava, gazelle tracks in silt pockets, gecko scuttle on warm rock, bleached bones, and raptor or crow perches on spatter cones. Use the wild ass as a rare "living beauty" encounter.

### Gaps
- No source on lichens on Afar basalt or plant cover per area. Lichens are probably minimal in this hyperarid heat, but that is unverified.
- No Erta Ale-specific species list near the summit.

## Q5. Human traces and material culture

### Takeaway
Humans live at the edges of the lava, not on it. Afar villages such as Dodom sit at the shield's foot by wells, with domed tents and thornbush corrals. Camel trains and foot tracks cross the lava. The summit has stone huts and shelters on the rim, wooden bed frames, and tourist litter. Armed escorts accompany every group. Salt evaporation ponds at Lake Afrera and bulldozed 4x4 tracks are the industrial traces.

### Cited Findings
- **Settlement and tents.** Dodom is an Afar village with wells at the foot of Erta Ale — [SwissEduc 2008](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/climb-2008-en.html). Afar camps are round domed tents like upturned boats, covered in brightly coloured cloth, inside thornbush corrals holding camels and goats — [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf). Small Afar villages lie along the descent route over dusty sand and lava fields toward Amadelah — [Volcano Adventures](https://www.volcanoadventures.com/tours/ethiopia/nov2015/impressions/p5.html)
- **Summit camp.** There is a stone Afar hut with thin mattresses side by side, and makeshift wooden beds in the open. Ground near the camp is littered with plastic bottles and toilet paper. Gas masks are handed out — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia). Shelters stand on the western caldera rim — [SwissEduc 2008](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/climb-2008-en.html)
- **Military.** Two escorts carry automatic rifles, one walking at the back — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia). An armed man is silhouetted against the sky, and security men bring up the rear — [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf)
- **Tracks.** 4x4s lurch over rocks and cracks, and lava fields slow vehicles to walking pace — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia); [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf)
- **Salt industry.** There are white rectangular salt evaporation ponds on Lake Afrera's shore — [NASA EO](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/lava-around-lake-afrera-84081/). SwissEduc has galleries of salt caravans and Afrera salt — [SwissEduc index](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/index-en.html)
- **Science traces.** GPS and seismic stations were brought in by camel to the Afar rift — [Leeds](https://homepages.see.leeds.ac.uk/~eartjw/dabbahu/photos.html)

### Inferences
- **Stone-wall vocabulary (inference).** Dry-stacked basalt blocks, unmortared, 0.5-1.5 m high, built as circular hut bases, windbreak crescents and corral walls. Roofs would be sticks, palm-frond mats or tarps. The domed Afar house (often called *ari*) is traditionally a frame of bent sticks covered with palm mats; Stewart's account gives cloth covers. Mats are unverified here.
- **Trail tells.** Cairns marking the night route; a smoothed, dust-whitened path over pahoehoe (crushed shelly crust turns grey-white); camel dung lines; plastic water bottles; spent torch batteries; tarps.
- **Game translation.** Escort posts become checkpoints. Abandoned tourist huts on the rim become ruined shelters in a dying world.
- **Unverified context.** The armed escorts are commonly linked to a 2012 attack on tourists near Erta Ale. I did not source that in this pass.

### Gaps
- No good source on Afar walls or graves on lava fields, or on how the summit huts are built (dimensions, roofing).
- No source on how Afar people use the lava fields themselves, beyond transit and camping at the edges.

## Q6. Air, weather and light

### Takeaway
It is extremely hot (40-45 °C by day), so activity happens at dusk, night and dawn. At night the summit sky glows red above the rim and gas plumes drift off the lake. SO2 is strong enough that guides hand out gas masks. Fine pale ash and dust drift into every crack. Gas or steam can veil the lake within an hour. In eruptions, ash and SO2 rise to about 2 km.

### Cited Findings
- **Heat.** The Danakil regularly reaches 45 °C — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia). Days were over 40 °C during a March fieldwork visit in Afar — [Leeds](https://homepages.see.leeds.ac.uk/~eartjw/dabbahu/photos.html)
- **Glow and light.**
  - A red-glowing sky above the summit with sparks, lava light turning faces red, and otherwise only torches and stars at night — [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf)
  - Golden evening light on the lake at sunset, red seams appearing as daylight fades, and dawn viewing — [Volcano Adventures](https://www.volcanoadventures.com/tours/ethiopia/nov2015/impressions/p5.html)
  - Blue sky reflecting off dark lava at dusk — [SwissEduc](https://swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/lava-en.html)
- **Gas.** Hornitos gave off SO2, with noisy degassing (2017), and a steam plume accompanied the 2022 activity — [GVP](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=221080). The 2025 eruption put ash and SO2 plumes to about 2 km a.s.l. — [COMET](https://comet.nerc.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/COMET_EventReport2.1_ErtaAle.pdf). The lake "smoked over" after about an hour — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia)
- **Dust.** Sand whips against car windows on the approach — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/visiting-erta-ale-volcano-ethiopia). Haze hides the distant Tigray highlands — [Stewart PDF](https://journeysbydesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The-Edge-of-Reason-Stanley-Stewart-Conde-Nast.pdf). White wind-blown ash and dust lie across dark basalt — [Leeds](https://homepages.see.leeds.ac.uk/~eartjw/dabbahu/photos.html)

### Inferences
- **Smell.** SO2 gives a sharp, struck-match, acrid smell that catches the throat. H2S (rotten egg) is likelier at fumaroles and the salt-side springs than at an open lake. This is general knowledge.
- **Heat haze.** Heat shimmer rises over black basalt by day; black rock absorbs heat and radiates it after sunset. Use it for warm night air and a VFX distortion layer.
- **Fumaroles and sublimates (Q-brief item 3).** None are described in my sources. Plausibly there are steaming cracks on the caldera floor and pit rims with yellow sulfur, and white-yellow salt and sulfate crusts, as at many basaltic summits. Unverified for Erta Ale here.

### Gaps
- No source on fumarole fields or sublimate colours at Erta Ale, and no wind regime data.
- No recordings or descriptions of lake sound beyond "noisy degassing".

## Q7. Per-zone build sheets (brief items 1-11: palette, asset seeds, beauty/grit, reference)

### Takeaway
The two zones share one rock but read differently through age and light. The Basalt Fields are a daylight zone: rust-brown, varnished old lobes with cream dust seams and sparse grey thorn scrub, crossed by glossy black young tongues and white-dusted rift cracks. The Volcano is a dusk-and-night zone: black pahoehoe, golden Pele's hair, a red-veined lake, a red sky-glow, and stone huts on the rim. All facts below are cited in Q1-Q6. This section is synthesis, so most of it is inference.

### Inferences

#### Zone A: Basalt Fields (floor below the fault wall)
1. **Ground (walking scale).** These run from oldest to youngest, so they can blend:
   - (a) old lobe: matte rust-brown and varnished ropy pahoehoe, softened ropes, slabs breaking up;
   - (b) cream and buff silt and ash pans filling hollows and crack networks, with wind ripples and hoof prints;
   - (c) mid-age grey-black pahoehoe with dull sheen and dust in the rope troughs;
   - (d) fresh charcoal tongues: glossy black and silver-sheened ropes and toes, shelly crust with collapse holes, and Pele's hair in lee pockets;
   - (e) trampled trail: crushed grey-white crust, camel dung.
2. **Landforms.**
   - 1-10 m: lobe margins as 0.5-2 m steps, tumuli with axial cracks, pressure ridges, squeeze-ups, small collapse pits over tubes, a skylight onto a dark tube.
   - 10-100 m: young tongues 50-300 m wide and 1-3 m thick fronts advancing over old lobes; kipukas of rust ground with scrub; rift crack fields 0.3-1 m wide crossing everything; fault steps 1-5 m.
   - Skyline: the red-ochre fault wall to one side, and the low black shield with its smoke plume beyond.
3. **Water and minerals.** None at the surface on the lava. Use white salt or alkali efflorescence in crack bottoms near the salt-pan edge, rust bleed lines where old lobes meet damp ground, and a possible seep with a doum palm stand at the foot of the fault wall.
4. **Vegetation.** *Acacia mellifera* thickets 1-4 m and *Rhigozum somalense* 1-2 m, growing only in silt-filled cracks and kipukas (1 per 10-50 m²; zero on young tongues). Occasional *A. tortilis* umbrellas on the sand margins, doum palm stands in wadis below the wall, dead grey stumps and dry grass tufts.
5. **Wildlife and signs.** Dorcas gazelle tracks, dik-dik dung middens, gecko on warm rock, raptor on a spatter cone, wild ass (rare), camel and goat trails.
6. **Human traces.** Cairns, dry-stone windbreaks and corral rings, a collapsed domed-tent frame with a thornbush corral at the margin, a 4x4 track (wheel ruts in silt, crushed crust), an abandoned seismic or GPS station, discarded bottles.
7. **Air and light.** White-hot noon, heat shimmer, haze softening the wall, dust devils over silt pans, low warm raking light at dawn and dusk to show the sheen and ropes.
8. **Palette (hex values are inference).**
   - Fresh basalt #141416 / #1E1F22, with sheen highlights #8A8F99 (silver) and #6B5A3A (bronze iridescence).
   - Mid-age #2B2A28.
   - Old lobe #4A3B30 / #5E4434, varnish #2E2219, rust accents #8C4A2A.
   - Dust and ash fill #CDBFA6 / #E3D9C6.
   - White salt fringe #F2EFE8.
   - Escarpment red-ochre #A0522D / #B5653A.
   - Scrub grey-green #7C7F62, dead wood #9C9384.
9. **Asset seeds.**
   - *Materials:* old pahoehoe (rope, varnish, dust masks), fresh pahoehoe (glossy, with a sheen/anisotropy parameter), shelly crust (with a broken variant), silt and ash pan, trail.
   - *Rock kits:* old-lobe slabs and plates (0.5-3 m); fresh ropy toes and lobes (0.2-1 m); a tumulus (3 sizes, with an axial crack); a pressure ridge segment; squeeze-up blobs; a collapse pit rim and rubble floor; a tube mouth and skylight; a spatter cone or hornito (3-10 m); a flow-front edge module (1-3 m step, modular).
   - *Rift kits:* crack decal and mesh set (5 cm, 30 cm, 1 m widths); gaping fissure segments 2-5 m wide; fault-scarp cliff modules 1-5 m with a fresh buff slip band.
   - *Decals:* dust in rope troughs, rust bleed, hoof trail, wind ripples, Pele's hair tuft, salt efflorescence.
   - *Foliage:* *A. mellifera* (3 variants, leafed and leafless), *Rhigozum*, *A. tortilis*, doum palm (single, clump, dead skirt), grass tufts, dead stumps.
   - *Props:* cairn, dry-stone wall (straight and curved), corral ring, domed-hut frame (bare, cloth-covered, collapsed), camel saddle and water jerrycans, sensor station.
   - *VFX:* heat shimmer, dust devil, blowing dust ribbons over crust edges, glinting sheen sparkle.
10. **Beauty and grit.**
    - *Beauty:* a glossy black tongue spilling across rust-brown old lobes with white dust seams; the silver sheen at sunset; one green-grey acacia in a black crack; the red wall above black ground under white-hot sky.
    - *Deliberate grit:* shelly crust collapsing underfoot (a hazard); rust bleed; bones in a fissure; a bulldozed track scarring the old lava.

#### Zone B: The Volcano (shield on the north fault block)
1. **Ground.**
   - Flanks: fresh and mid-age pahoehoe with frequent shelly crust and spatter-rag fields near fissures, plus dust-whitened trails.
   - Caldera floor: barren black pahoehoe, intricately folded and twisted old crust, small ropes with pale ash between them, mats of golden Pele's hair, Pele's tears in hollows.
   - Pit rim: spatter agglutinate (rough lumpy blobs welded together), black glassy splash blobs, and overflow sheets (smooth glossy).
2. **Landforms.**
   - Hornito rows along a flank fissure (3-10 m).
   - Caldera wall steps of 10-50 m, some sections low and gentle.
   - The 0.7 x 1.6 km caldera: game scale perhaps 150-400 m, with a look-out hill.
   - The pit: real 150-350 m wide and tens of metres deep; for play, the lake perhaps 30-80 m across, 10-30 m below the rim, with black overflow terraces on the walls and a shore hornito with stalactites.
   - Optionally a second collapsed, drained pit (the 2025 story).
   - Skyline: a broad, low shield (not a cone), other shields on the horizon, a red night glow.
3. **Water and minerals.** The lava lake itself, with its crust plates. Fumaroles in rim cracks with yellow sulfur crusts and white salt-sulfate sublimates (unsourced and inferred; use as an accent).
4. **Vegetation.** None in the caldera or on young flanks. Last scrub at the shield foot near Dodom-style wells.
5. **Wildlife and signs.** Almost none on top. Camels at camp, crows or ravens at the litter (inference).
6. **Human traces.**
   - Stone huts on the rim: dry-stacked basalt, perhaps roofed with sticks, mats or tarp; thin mattresses; wooden bed frames.
   - A guard post with an armed escort, a camel unloading point, litter (bottles, toilet paper).
   - A rope or cairn line to the viewing edge, gas masks.
   - Below: Dodom-style wells and domed cloth-covered tents with thornbush corrals.
7. **Air and light.** Mostly dusk to dawn. Red glow under a gas plume, steam veils that hide and reveal the lake, spark showers, red-lit faces, stars, the blue sky reflected on dark crust at dusk, the golden sunset on the lake, the SO2 haze, heat radiating from the rim.
8. **Palette (hex values are inference).**
   - Crust black #0E0E10, cooling crust dull red #5A1A10.
   - Crack glow: deep red #B3200E → orange #FF5A12 → yellow #FFC23A → white-yellow core #FFF1B0 (yellow = freshest, per SwissEduc).
   - Sky glow #6E1E14 over a night sky #0B1020.
   - Pele's hair gold #C9A25A, ash between ropes #BDB3A3, sulfur accent #D9C23A, sublimate white #EDE8D8.
9. **Asset seeds.**
   - *Lake shader:* a Voronoi crust-plate field 1-5 m with emissive seams; a spreading edge (emission bias) and a subducting edge (plates fold and pile); bubble bursts (a dome rise, a burst, spatter ribbons 5-30 m, a ripple ring about 1 m); level cycling; a crust-overriding event; cooling (emission → black over ~seconds to minutes).
   - *Rock kits:* pit-wall strata with overflow drapes and bathtub rings; rim spatter agglutinate; hornito with lip stalactites; folded old-crust slabs; small rope and toe pieces; overflow sheets.
   - *Decals:* Pele's hair mats, ash in ropes, glassy splash blobs, scorch and sulfur rings at vents.
   - *VFX:* rising gas plume lit from below, drifting steam veil, spark embers, Pele's-hair drift particles, heat distortion over the lake, spatter, a distant red sky-glow card for other zones' skylines.
   - *Props:* stone hut (intact and ruined), wooden bed frame, mattress, jerrycan, gas mask, rifle-escort post, camel packs, cairns, litter set.
   - *Audio seeds:* roar and hiss, crack and pop, bubble whump, patter of spatter.
10. **Beauty and grit.**
    - *Beauty:* zigzag red seams appearing at dusk across a dark lake; a bubble burst lighting the pit walls; golden Pele's hair glinting on black ropes; seeing the lake's glow from the Basalt Fields at night as a skyline beacon.
    - *Deliberate grit:* litter and toilet paper around tourist huts (a dying-world echo of past visitors); an escort's rifle silhouette; gas forcing masks; a drained, collapsed pit as the "dying" variant of the lake.
