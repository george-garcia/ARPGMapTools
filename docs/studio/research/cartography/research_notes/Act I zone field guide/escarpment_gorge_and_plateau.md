# Escarpment, Gorge and Plateau: visual field guide (western Afar / Tigray escarpment above the Danakil)

Scope: the red-brown fault escarpment on the basin's west and north rim, its benches, gorges and boulder fans; springs and falls where water leaves the plateau; the switchback caravan climb (real analogue: the salt road from the Danakil via Berahile up to Mekelle); and the highland plateau with Tigray dryland farming and stone villages. Game targets: Slums at the foot of the falls, the upper slaver city on an escarpment shelf at the head of the graded climb, and the plateau beyond.

Reading key: **Cited Findings** are sourced facts (paraphrased). **Inferences** are my reasoning or general knowledge that I could not verify with a source in this pass; treat them as art direction, not fact. Approximate hex values are always inference (estimated from rock and soil types described), never measured.

Research budget note: about 25 searches and fetches. Several key pages (BioOne, Springer chapters) were paywalled or blocked by bot protection, so some topics (waterfall form, escarpment botany below 1,400 m, crop calendars) rest on general knowledge and are flagged as such.

## 1. Escarpment rock sequence, colours, fault scarps, benches, talus and gorges

### Takeaway
Seen from the basin, the Tigray side is a stack of near-horizontal layers on a crystalline base: grey-green Precambrian metavolcanics and slates at the bottom, then yellow-to-red-to-pink Adigrat sandstone (up to about 700 m), then a thick pale cream-to-yellow Antalo limestone and marl series with karst and tufa, then dark-grey Agula shale with gypsum, a thin variegated purple-red-white Amba Aradam sandstone, and finally black Trap basalt cliffs with a white lake-sediment band between flows. The flat-lying layering gives alternating cliffs and benches, which is the real basis for "fault benches" on the game's rim.

### Cited Findings
**Stratigraphy, base to top (Geba basin mapping, western Tigray, next to the rift escarpment):** [Geba basin geological mapping, Tigray Livelihood Paper 9, 2010](https://core.ac.uk/download/55812716.pdf)
- Precambrian basement: low-grade meta-volcanics (green or grey), slates and phyllites, meta-conglomerate, meta-greywacke, black carbonate; the Negash granite covers about 50 km².
- Enticho Sandstone: up to about 100 m, white, cross-bedded, with iron-stained (ferruginous) layers.
- Edaga Arbi Tillite (glacial): regionally 150-180 m thick, with boulders up to 6 m across; coloured purple, red, olive-green and grey.
- Adigrat (Lower) Sandstone: up to about 700 m near Abi Adi; yellowish to red or pink, with white upper beds; exposed around the edge of the Mekelle Outlier and in "deeply incised gorges" of the Geba and its tributaries.
- Antalo Limestone supersequence: about 1,100 m in total; the lower limestone-marl part 250-350 m; marl light yellowish when fresh and yellow-green when weathered; a karstified limestone cliff about 8 m thick caps part of the section.
- Agula Shale: up to about 400 m; laminated, light-to-dark grey, fissile, with black lime mudstone, gypsum layers (seen along the Mekelle to Hagere Selam road) and limestone beds up to about 10 m.
- Dolerite sills and dykes: 80-130 m thick and up to 20 km long; form steep cliffs or flat mountain tops.
- Amba Aradam (Upper) Sandstone: up to about 50 m in the basin (100-200 m elsewhere in Tigray); variegated white, purple, red and yellow fluvial sandstone and shale; overlain by Trap basalt.
- Trap basalts: fine black basalt 40-50 m at the base, then about 15 m of white lake sediment, then coarser black basalt 60-80 m thick that forms cliffs.
- Faults (Chelekot, Mekelle, Wukro) run roughly WNW-ESE and cut the area into blocks; drag folds at scarp bases form small local basins; the Mekelle and Wukro basins are fault-controlled depressions with thin alluvium. Agula shale is exposed near the rift escarpment north-east of Quiha. The report is internally inconsistent about whether these faults are pre-rift or young.
- Adigrat sandstone reaches about 1,770 m elevation in the Danakil Alps on the Red Sea side (cited secondhand in the same report).

**Antalo limestone detail:** limestone, marl and calcareous sandstone with some shale; grainstone, wackestone, fine micritic limestone and shell (coquina) beds; chert at the base of the upper member; a coral-like stromatoporoid level; Late Jurassic shallow tropical sea; 300-800 m thick (sources in the article disagree); sub-horizontal layering gives alternating cliffs and flats; dissolution caves (for example St Mika'el's cave at Kurkura, Dogu'a Tembien). — [Antalo Limestone (Wikipedia mirror)](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Antalo_Limestone)
- The western Mekelle Outlier limestone landscape has caves, speleothems, karren (lapiez, i.e. fluted and pitted bare limestone) and "a large number of relict tufa dams". — [Dramis & Fubelli, "Limestone, Karst and Tufa Dams in the Western Part of the Mekelle Outlier", in Geo-trekking in Ethiopia's Tropical Mountains (Springer 2019), abstract](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-04955-3_7)

**Escarpment as seen from the caravan trail:** the lowland landscape below the escarpment is a below-sea-level desert with volcanoes, craters and "high escarpments"; the highland trail runs from over 2,000 m down to about 200 m below sea level over about 160 km. — [Woldekiros 2019, Chungara (salt caravan archaeology)](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Lowland trail surfaces are dense gravel, sand or fine river sediment and pebbly dry riverbeds; the last about 7 km to the salt flat is a rugged wet sandy plain. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- A botanical transect on the eastern Rift escarpment from the Tigray highlands to the Afar lowlands ran from about 2,740 m to 1,400 m (Esot to Koneba) and about 2,300 m to 1,000 m (Agoro to Shaigubi), confirming roughly 1,300-1,700 m of relief on the upper face. — [Tropentag 2008 abstract, escarpment vegetation](https://www.tropentag.de/2008/abstracts/full/190.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Which layers to show on the game face (inference, art-directed):** the real Mekelle-to-Danakil face mixes these units, and which appear depends on the block. For George's "red-ochre escarpment" brief, the honest choice is to make the Adigrat sandstone (yellow-red-pink, up to 700 m) the dominant mid-face band, sitting on a dark grey-green basement plinth at the foot and capped by pale Antalo limestone ledges, with a black basalt cornice only on the highest skyline. This matches the sequence above; it is a selection, not a cross-section of one real place.
- **Benches:** sub-horizontal strata erode into "cliff, slope, cliff" steps. Hard units (sandstone, limestone cliff beds, dolerite sills, basalt flows) make vertical walls of tens of metres; soft units (marl, shale, tillite) make debris-covered slopes and flat benches. A city shelf is most plausible on a soft-over-hard bench, for example on top of the sandstone cliff where the marl or shale above has retreated. Small faults stepping the face down would add repeated benches with tilted (back-rotated) tops.
- **Boulder fans and talus (inference from rock type):** sandstone talus breaks into blocky, rectangular slabs (0.3-3 m) with rounded edges; limestone talus into angular pale blocks with fluted surfaces; basalt into columnar or angular black blocks; tillite releases odd rounded erratics up to 6 m (cited size) of mixed rock in a purple matrix, which is a gift for "out of place" giant boulders at the foot. Fans spread where gorges exit, sorted coarse at the apex to gravel at the toe.
- **Gorge walls:** a gorge cut through this stack would show colour banding top to bottom: black basalt rim, cream limestone ledges with dark karst holes and tufa curtains, red-pink sandstone walls (often vertical, cross-bedded, with dark desert varnish streaks), and a grey-green basement slot at the bottom where water polishes the rock.
- **Approximate palette (inference):** Adigrat sandstone red-ochre #A8553A, pink-tan #C98E6A, white upper bed #E6D8C3; Antalo limestone cream #D9CBA8, weathered marl yellow-green #B5AE7A; Agula shale grey #6E6A66; tillite purple #6B4A5A; basement grey-green #5E6A5C; basalt #2B2A2A; gypsum veins #EFEDE6; desert varnish #3B2A22.

### Gaps
- I found no source that describes the exact layer sequence on the face directly above Berahile or the Desa'a forest, so the order of exposures on the real escarpment there is not confirmed.
- No measured cliff heights, bench widths or talus fan sizes on the Afar face itself were found.
- Leeds University's Afar pages on the Danakil block were surfaced but not read.

## 2. Vegetation along the gradient (hot foot to cool plateau)

### Takeaway
Sources confirm acacia woodland in the Afar lowlands just below the escarpment, a species-richness peak at about 1,900-2,100 m on the face, and Juniperus procera with wild olive (Olea) dominating the upper escarpment forest (Desa'a/Dessea), now degraded with many dead trees and stumps. On the plateau, protected exclosures are shrubby with Dodonaea, Euclea, Acacia etbaica and juniper, while open grazing land is about 83% low shrubs. Cactus pear (beles) covers about 7% of Tigray on stony slopes and around farmsteads.

### Cited Findings
- Escarpment transects (about 2,740 m to 1,000 m): species richness is lowest at 1,000 m (as few as 2 species per 2,500 m² plot), rises steadily to a peak of up to 45 species per plot at 1,900-2,100 m, then falls above about 2,000 m. — [Tropentag 2008 abstract](https://www.tropentag.de/2008/abstracts/full/190.pdf)
- Highland zone dominated by *Juniperus procera* (African pencil cedar); the Dessea (Desa'a) escarpment forest is dominated by *J. procera* and *Olea* species; *Erica arborea* (tree heath) in the wetter Tigray highlands; acacia woodland in the Afar lowlands within about 5 km of the highland sites. — [Tropentag 2008](https://www.tropentag.de/2008/abstracts/full/190.pdf)
- Condition: forests degraded and fragmented by fuelwood cutting, farming and livestock; Dessea forest in "retrogressive succession" with large areas replaced by invasive shrubs (*Calpurnia/"Cadia" purpurea*, *Tarconanthus camphoratus*); many large trees removed, shown by numerous stumps; high mortality on the Esot side with many dead *Juniperus* and *Olea* still standing. — [Tropentag 2008](https://www.tropentag.de/2008/abstracts/full/190.pdf)
- Tigray highland exclosures (about 2,200-2,900 m): most abundant woody species *Dodonaea angustifolia* (hop bush), *Becium grandiflorum* (a woody aromatic shrub), *Euclea racemosa*, *Acacia etbaica*, *Maytenus arbutifolia*, *Acacia seyal*, *Juniperus procera*, and planted *Acacia saligna* and *Eucalyptus camaldulensis*. Exclosures had 60 woody species against 28 on grazed land; exclosures were about 35% trees and 40% shrubs, grazed land about 83% shrubs and 6% trees; about 91% of exclosure plants regenerated naturally; foot slopes richer than upper slopes. Rainy season roughly June to September. — [Restoration of degraded drylands through exclosures, Tigray highlands (CyberLeninka)](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/restoration-of-degraded-drylands-through-exclosures-enhancing-woody-species-diversity-and-soil-nutrients-in-the-highlands-of-tigray)
- Cactus pear (*Opuntia ficus-indica*, beles) covers more than about 379,000 ha, about 7.4% of Tigray; grows mainly on hilly, sloping, stony land unfit for crops (about 1,000-3,500 m); spineless forms are planted around backyards and farmsteads to control livestock; the spiny form is used as a live fence; cladodes (pads) are fed to livestock, often after burning and chopping off the spines; it is an emergency food and feed in drought; introduced in the 19th or early 20th century (accounts differ); the cochineal insect, released in 2004 in Raya Azebo, now heavily infests and kills plants in the south ("out of control"). — [Livestock Research for Rural Development 25(2), 2013](https://lrrd.org/lrrd25/2/moen25026.htm)
- Stone-bund vegetation in Tigray: *Leucaena* and *Sesbania* shrubs at about 1,000 plants/ha plus local grasses on bund faces. — [WOCAT stone-faced soil bund of Tigray](https://qcat.wocat.net/en/wocat/technologies/view/permalink/981/)
- Caravan rests are chosen for shade from large acacias or rock shelters, and wild grass grows for grazing in both highlands and lowlands. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)

### Inferences
Belt model for the game face (general knowledge of Ethiopian escarpments, not verified species by species in this pass):
- **Foot, fans and wadi floors (about -100 to 600 m; hot):** scattered umbrella acacias (*Acacia tortilis*, flat crowns 4-8 m tall, often browsed into hourglass shapes), *Prosopis juliflora* thickets along wadis (an invasive, dense, thorny, 3-6 m; a good "grit" plant for Slum edges), doum palm (*Hyphaene thebaica*) only where groundwater is shallow. Bare between: most of the fan surface carries nothing. Density: one tree per 20-100 m except along water lines.
- **Lower face and benches (about 600-1,400 m):** *Acacia etbaica* (cited on the plateau; also typical of the lower escarpment), *Acacia mellifera* hook-thorn, *Commiphora* shrubs with peeling papery bark, aloes (*Aloe* spp., rosettes 0.3-1 m, coral-red flower spikes in the dry season) on ledges, and candelabra euphorbia (*Euphorbia abyssinica* / *E. candelabrum*, 5-12 m, grey-green, many upright arms) standing out on cliff ledges and steep scree where goats cannot reach.
- **Upper face (about 1,400-2,100 m; species-richness peak, cited):** mixed shrub woodland: *Dodonaea* (glossy olive-green, 1-3 m, papery winged pink-red seed capsules), *Euclea*, *Carissa*, wild olive (*Olea europaea* subsp. *cuspidata*, dark grey-green, silvery leaf undersides, 5-10 m), figs at seeps.
- **Rim and Desa'a-type forest (about 2,100-2,700 m; cited):** *Juniperus procera* (conical to flat-topped, 10-25 m, reddish fibrous bark) with olive; many dead silver-grey snags and stumps (cited); invaded by *Tarconanthus* (grey-green felted leaves) and *Calpurnia* (yellow pea flowers).
- **Springs (any altitude):** *Ficus sycomorus* or *Ficus vasta* (huge buttressed trees, 15-25 m, deep shade) are the classic spring trees in northern Ethiopia; maidenhair ferns (*Adiantum*) and mosses on wet rock; reeds/sedges in pools. Not verified for a specific escarpment spring.
- **Drought condition:** acacias leafless or yellow-green, grass grey-blond and grazed to stubble, euphorbias shrivelled and leaning, cactus pads wrinkled and yellowed, cochineal-killed cactus as grey-white crusted skeletons (the cochineal kill is cited; its look is inference: white waxy fluff on pads, crimson stain when crushed).

### Gaps
- No source found in this pass with species, heights and densities for the escarpment below 1,400 m on the Afar side, or for *Euphorbia abyssinica*, *Aloe* or *Ficus* positions on the face; those are general knowledge.
- No plant heights were given in either survey.

## 3. Springs, waterfalls and tufa where water leaves the plateau

### Takeaway
Calcium-rich water from the Antalo limestone deposits travertine (tufa) at spring outlets, falls and rapids around Mekelle, and the western Mekelle Outlier is full of relict tufa dams, which were later cut by streams after forest loss about 3,000 years ago. This gives a sourced basis for a falls stream that drapes cream tufa curtains and builds stepped pools.

### Cited Findings
- Travertine forms at spring outlets, waterfalls and rapids around Mekelle; the Alula Aba Nega spring near Agbe has active stalactite and stalagmite growth; incision into the travertine is linked to forest loss starting about 3,000 years before present. — [Geba basin mapping 2010](https://core.ac.uk/download/55812716.pdf)
- The western Mekelle Outlier has "a large number of relict tufa dams", plus caves, speleothems and karren. — [Dramis & Fubelli 2019, abstract](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-04955-3_7); related study: Dramis & Fubelli (2015), "Tufa Dams in Tigray (Northern Ethiopia) as Late Pleistocene-Holocene Climate Proxies", in *Landscapes and Landforms of Ethiopia* (title only seen).
- In the highlands, runoff after the rains collects in pockets along riverbeds; caravaners time travel so rivers are neither in flood nor fully dry; lowland water is very limited, and old campsites sit near rivers and wells. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Gypsum layers in the Agula shale (mineral source for white crusts and bitter water). — [Geba basin mapping 2010](https://core.ac.uk/download/55812716.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The falls (art direction grounded in the tufa facts):** a limestone-fed stream on this escarpment would build a tufa apron: rounded cream-to-honey lobes and "frozen curtain" drapes 1-20 m tall over the cliff lip, with moss-green and black algae streaks where water runs, dry white crust where it no longer does. Below, rimstone pools (terraced basins with curved rims 5-50 cm high) step down the gorge. Relict, dry, cut-through tufa dams upstream show the stream used to be bigger: a ready-made "dying world" storytelling device that is also real.
- **Wet rock staining:** black-brown vertical streaks (manganese and algae) under every seep line along a bedding plane; orange iron stain where water exits sandstone; white gypsum or salt efflorescence near the foot as water evaporates in the heat.
- **Seep lines:** springs emerge where permeable limestone or sandstone sits on impermeable shale, tillite or basement; so seeps form horizontal lines of green along one bench, a strong readable cue from an isometric camera.
- **Plunge pool and Slums:** at the foot, water sinks into the boulder fan within a short distance in the hot season (losing stream), so a Slum would crowd the pool and the last few hundred metres of surface flow. Inference, consistent with the cited lowland water scarcity.
- **Palette (inference):** tufa cream #E3D2A6, wet tufa honey #B8955C, moss #4F6B3A, algae black streak #26261F, pool water green-brown #5C6B4A, gypsum crust #F1EEE7.

### Gaps
- No source found for a named waterfall on the Afar face, its height, or flow by season. The falls are invented, though grounded.
- Fern, moss and fig species at specific escarpment springs were not confirmed.

## 4. The caravan road up the escarpment (Berahile to Mekelle salt route)

### Takeaway
The real salt road is about 160 km from the salt flat up to the Tigray highlands, mostly following river valleys to keep gradients low, with Berahile as the Afar market and tax town where lowland camel caravans hand over to highland donkey caravans. Night camps hold up to about 1,000 people and 6,000 animals with circular corrals and three-stone hearths. A paved road now follows the old route and is replacing the caravans.

### Cited Findings
- Route: about 160 km, from over 2,000 m to about 200 m below sea level; about 110 km of highland section (Mekelle to Desi'a) and about 72 km of lowland section. Three highland routes down: the Desi'a route (most used), the Usot route (Atsbi caravaners), and the Shiket pass (about 28 km south of Desi'a, 1,350 m, flat and good for camels but too hot). Lowland towns: Ab Ala (Abala), Korha, Darguni, Berhaile, Melabdi, Asa Bolo (Sabba), Hamed Ela, ending at the salt flat. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Highland trails mostly follow rivers and streams because valleys let travellers through steep country at good slopes; surfaces are silty clay, gravelly paths and grassland. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Camps: short rests of 1-2 hours for water, grazing and shade (big acacias or rock shelters); overnight stops after about 20 km, arriving about 7 pm and leaving about 3 am; sites next to small farming or pastoralist villages for food, water and protection from jackals, foxes and raids; up to 700-1,000 people and 3,000-6,000 animals a night. Layout: a circular animal corral with fodder piled in the middle, a gear and sleeping area for saddles and water sacks, hearths upwind. Tea and coffee hearths are three stones of about 15-25 cm; bread hearths are 73-171 cm across, of small rounded stones that serve as a griddle. On the return, salt is stacked in two rows and sleepers ring the animals and salt. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Animals and gear: highlanders prefer donkeys, lowlanders camels (sample: 196 donkeys, 11 mules, 566 camels); goatskin water sacks (*sar*), flour, tea, coffee, hay or chaff; pack pads, hooks and grass-fibre or leather straps; standard salt slab sizes (*gole'o*, *gerawayni*, *ankarabe*). — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Archaeology of old camps: floodplain sites near rivers and wells with scatters of obsidian and chert tools, Aksumite brown-ware pottery (about 5th century), animal bones and dung. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Salt blocks of about 7 kg are a unit of currency; 20 blocks per camel to Mekelle; the Afar caravan leader (*arho*) takes the caravan only as far as Berahile, the edge of Afar country; caravans travel overnight from about -100 m up toward Mekelle at 2,300 m. — [SwissEduc, The Salt of Africa (2008 photo essay)](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Up to 30 bricks per camel, some loads heavy enough to make camels collapse; a 1-2 day walk from the mines to Berhale "in nearby mountains". — [Al Jazeera photo gallery 2016](https://www.aljazeera.com/gallery/2016/1/13/endangered-camel-caravans-of-the-ethiopian-salt-mines)
- Camels are loaded in large baskets and tied nose to tail behind a lead camel; donkeys crowd camels for shade and carry water. — [Andy Explores, Danakil part 2](https://andyexplores.squarespace.com/blog-1/danakil2)
- A paved road links Mekelle with Dallol and cut the Mekelle to Berahile drive from five to three hours; miners fear it will bring mechanised salt firms. — [Gulf Times / AFP](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/352948/change-looms-for-ethiopias-ancient-salt-trade)

### Inferences
- **What a switchback mule and camel road on this face would look like (general knowledge of Ethiopian and other mountain pack trails; not sourced to this route):** tread 1.5-3 m wide (camels need about 2 m and gentle grades, donkeys manage steps 20-30 cm); hairpins built on dry-stone revetment walls 1-4 m high of unmortared blocks, uphill face battered; worn steps polished smooth with dished tops; drainage cross-bars of laid stone; a low stone kerb 20-40 cm on the drop side; dung pellets and urine stains concentrated at hairpins and rest spots; scattered broken salt fragments, lost straps and pads, dead donkey bones in gullies. Rest spots at hairpins with a flat, a shade tree or overhang, a three-stone hearth ring and soot on the rock.
- **Old versus new:** old route = narrow paths in the valley floor and on spurs, cairns, hearths and dung; new route = a graded, blasted road with cut-and-fill scars, retaining walls of gabion baskets, concrete culverts and a paved surface, crossed by the old trail. The game's "only graded climb" can mix both: a carved, revetted slaver road (built with forced labour, deliberate grit) beside older erased paths.
- **The city at the head of the climb:** Berahile is a lowland Afar market and tax town where caravans are counted (cited). A slaver city at the head of the climb is the inverse: a checkpoint where goods and people are taxed on the way up. Camp layout facts (circular corrals, salt stacked in rows, sleepers ringing goods) translate directly into caravanserai props.

### Gaps
- No source found describing actual revetments, steps or switchback engineering on the Desi'a or Usot descent, nor the gradient in numbers (the paper explicitly gives none).
- Woldekiros does not name springs, gorges or cairns on the route.

## 5. Tigray dryland agriculture on the plateau

### Takeaway
Plateau fields are rainfed with one growing season (roughly June/July to December), framed by stone-faced soil bunds about 0.5-0.75 m high, 1 m wide at the base and 30 cm at the top, laid along contours about 60-80 m apart and 1 m vertical interval apart, which over time turn 12% slopes into near-level 3% terraces. Gullies carry stone, mortared or gabion check dams 1-2 m high that pond water and trap silt.

### Cited Findings
- Stone-faced soil bund: stone wall on the downslope side with a soil embankment upslope, along the contour; foundation trench about 30 cm wide; wall height 0.5-0.75 m; base 1 m, top 30 cm; medium-sized stones; spacing 60-80 m; vertical interval 1 m; slope reduced from 12% to 3%; bunds planted with *Leucaena*, *Sesbania* and local grasses; area 1,000-2,500 m, drought-prone, organic matter under 1%; crops teff, barley, wheat, sorghum, faba (horse) bean, sunflower; free grazing after harvest; one season, longest July to December (about 150 days); built January to April, repaired every January. — [WOCAT, stone-faced soil bund of Tigray](https://qcat.wocat.net/en/wocat/technologies/view/permalink/981/)
- Check dam ponds (Kilte Awlaelo, Tigray, 450-550 mm rain): walls of stone, concrete or gabions (stone-filled wire baskets), mortar-plastered with a concrete upstream face; 1-2 m high and 1-2 m wide; length spans the gully; sluice gates about 1 m wide; store flow for irrigation and livestock; trap sediment; good surface water September to January. — [WOCAT, check dam ponds](https://qcat.wocat.net/af/wocat/technologies/view/permalink/1548/)
- Further WOCAT Tigray technologies exist for large semi-circular stone bunds and stone-faced trenches (photos on the pages). — [WOCAT semi-circular stone bunds](https://qcat.wocat.net/fr/wocat/technologies/view/permalink/1547); [WOCAT stone-faced trench](https://qcat.wocat.net/af/wocat/technologies/view/permalink/5527)
- Highland caravan trail surfaces of silty clay, gravel paths and grassland; highland forest largely cleared over the past 2,000 years. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Exclosures (fenced-off, ungrazed hillsides) hold about three times as many woody plants as grazing land. — [CyberLeninka exclosure study](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/restoration-of-degraded-drylands-through-exclosures-enhancing-woody-species-diversity-and-soil-nutrients-in-the-highlands-of-tigray)
- Hidmo roofs are used to dry harvested straw. — [Shimizu et al. 2019, hidmo of Inderta](https://www.janestudies.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/NES_no242019_Shimizu.et_.al_.pdf)
- Cactus pear is grown on stony slopes unsuited to crops and fed to livestock in drought. — [LRRD 2013](https://lrrd.org/lrrd25/2/moen25026.htm)
- Nyssen et al. (2009, Earth Surface Processes and Landforms) on Tigray land management is available. — [Nyssen et al. 2009 PDF](https://www.africamuseum.be/publication_docs/NyssenEtAl2009ESPLPRESS.pdf) (not read in this pass).

### Inferences
Crop look through the season (general knowledge; not sourced here):
- **Teff (*Eragrostis tef*):** fine grass 0.4-1.2 m; bright fresh green in August, a soft hazy wash of tiny red-brown or straw panicles by October; harvested by sickle into sheaves; fine pale stubble afterwards. Reads as a "soft, blurred" field texture.
- **Sorghum:** tall (1.5-3 m), broad strap leaves, heavy drooping or compact heads in cream, red-brown or dark red; stalks left standing or stacked as fodder and fuel; the strongest silhouette crop for game readability.
- **Finger millet / pearl millet:** 0.6-1.5 m, claw-like or candle heads, red-brown.
- **Barley and wheat:** 0.6-1 m, golden in November, on the higher, cooler plateau.
- **Chickpea and faba bean:** low (30-60 cm) blue-green bushy rows, often sown late on residual moisture.
- **Dry season (January to May):** bare red-brown and grey ploughed soil, stubble, stone bunds standing out as pale lines across every slope; oxen grazing stubble.
- **Ox plough (*maresha*):** a wooden ard about 2-3 m long with a single iron point, a yoke beam over two oxen, held by one man with a whip; scratches the soil without turning it (general knowledge).
- **Threshing floor (*awdi*):** a round floor 6-12 m across on a raised spot, smoothed with dung-and-mud plaster, ringed with stones; oxen walk in circles over spread sheaves; winnowing with wooden forks into the wind leaves a chaff plume (general knowledge).
- **Stacks and stores:** conical or ridge haystacks 2-4 m tall near houses and on roofs (roof drying cited); grain kept in clay-and-dung bins or in the upper floor of the hidmo (cited below).
- **Flood farming and check dams:** silt wedges behind each check dam become small flat fields of dark, finer soil; gully walls of red-brown soil 1-5 m tall, with fresh slumps.
- **Hedges:** cactus-pear rows (cited for fencing) and euphorbia hedges (general knowledge) around homesteads.
- **Palette (inference):** ploughed soil red-brown #7A4A33, vertisol dark grey-brown #4A4038, dry stubble #C9B58A, teff ripe haze #B07A55, sorghum red head #8C3A2A, fresh crop green #7F9A4A, bund stone pale grey-cream #B9AE98, cactus pad green #6F8A5A, cochineal white #EDE8E0.

### Gaps
- No source read in this pass gives Tigray crop calendars, plant heights or harvest colours; these come from general knowledge.
- Threshing floor dimensions and maresha dimensions were not sourced.

## 6. Villages and stone architecture (hidmo, walls, churches)

### Takeaway
The Tigray farmhouse (*hidmo*) is a thick-walled dry-stone rectangle with a flat or gently sloped roof of gravel and red or white earth over timber beams and V-pattern planking, a low door about 1 m high, tiny high windows, and a curved stone courtyard wall around house, kitchen and animal pens. Around Mekelle the stone is local Antalo limestone in black, white and yellowish-white.

### Cited Findings
All from [Shimizu et al. 2019, Nilo-Ethiopian Studies 24, hidmo of Inderta](https://www.janestudies.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/NES_no242019_Shimizu.et_.al_.pdf):
- Walls are stacked stone masonry (*mendeg*) from the neighbourhood; around Mekelle mostly Antalo limestone in black, white and yellowish-white; sandstone and granite elsewhere; sedimentary stone is easier to work than basalt, and hidmo is absent in basalt areas.
- The top course uses at least two layers of thin, broad stones (*qatsela*) to keep water out; rows of scaffold holes (*bukko*) are left in walls, some later filled.
- Timber is scarce (eucalyptus commercial; juniper and olive rare and protected); more wood meant more prestige. A central timber pillar (*'amdi*) carries a main beam (*gadi'm*); smaller beams (*serayt*) in alternating pairs; planks (*mi'buts*) fill gaps, usually in a V (herringbone) pattern; church buildings use a cross pattern.
- Roof flat or slightly sloped, of gravel and soil with hard-packed mud, using red and/or white soils; used for sleeping, watching the plot and drying straw.
- Door about 1.02 m high, deliberately low; timber door panels and lintels, never arched; small high windows (*meshkot*) with stone lintels, often partly blocked, so interiors are dim; a larger upper-floor window with a timber lintel; some doors have a stone eave that grows grass.
- Parcels enclosed by curved piled-stone walls (or sometimes wattle); courtyard (*mereba*) holds the main house, kitchen, cattle space and small-animal pen.
- Interior: mud-plastered stone benches (*medeb*) along walls, built by women; tool rail, grain box (*gotera*), wall niches; upper floor stores grain; lower parts hold livestock.
- Inderta parcels are curved and often on hillsides; 'Agame (north) parcels are rectilinear; a round variant (*kibi-hidmo*) exists. Hidmo dates back to at least the late first millennium BC (Hawelti).
- Mekelle has a heritage of hidmo houses promoted by the regional tourism office. — [Tourism Tigrai, Mekelle hidmo houses](https://mail.tourismtigrai.com/attraction/view/MTM2)

### Inferences
- **Wall texture at walking scale:** irregular courses of flat limestone or sandstone slabs 10-40 cm long, chinked with small stones, no visible mortar; walls likely 50-80 cm thick (not given in source); occasional larger cornerstones; bands of colour where black, white and yellow limestone are mixed.
- **Roofs from an isometric camera:** flat, earth-coloured (red or white per source), with a raised parapet of qatsela slabs, drying straw, stacked fuel and spouts; perfect for top-down readability of the upper city.
- **Churches:** Tigray churches typically sit on hilltops or cliff ledges in a grove of old juniper, olive or fig (the only big trees left in a cleared landscape), inside a stone compound wall; rock-hewn churches occur in Tigray's sandstone cliffs. General knowledge; not sourced here.
- **Slaver city mapping:** hidmo construction fits a stone city on a sandstone or limestone bench: dense walled courtyards stepping down the shelf, timber as a sign of wealth (cited), low doors and small windows (defensive by design), animal pens repurposed as holding pens (deliberate grit).
- **Palette (inference):** limestone wall cream #CFC3A6, black limestone #3A3734, yellowish stone #C8B07A, red roof earth #8E5238, white roof earth #D8D0BE, eucalyptus beam grey-brown #6B5A48, juniper beam red-brown #7A4B33.

### Gaps
- Wall thickness, room size and roof thickness are not given by the source.
- No source read on church siting or on Afar settlements in the lowland foot (Afar domed mat houses, *ari*), which would suit the Slums.

## 7. Air, weather and light

### Takeaway
Sources give the contrast: the depression floor sits at about 34°C average with summer peaks of 45°C and almost no rain, with glare and heat haze over the salt; the plateau has a June to September rainy season and caravaners travel by night (arrive about 7 pm, leave about 3 am). Seasonal wind directions are given for camp siting.

### Cited Findings
- Depression average about 34°C, rain almost never falls. — [Gulf Times / AFP](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/352948/change-looms-for-ethiopias-ancient-salt-trade)
- Summer temperatures to 45°C; daytime winter over 35°C; salt looks pink, grey or white depending on the time of day; wind sometimes pushes lake water over the flats. — [Al Jazeera gallery 2016](https://www.aljazeera.com/gallery/2016/1/13/endangered-camel-caravans-of-the-ethiopian-salt-mines)
- Above 40°C by mid-morning; glare off the lake; heat haze; sand in the hair after night breezes; dust from mining work. — [Andy Explores](https://andyexplores.squarespace.com/blog-1/danakil2)
- At noon, heat well above 40°C; caravans disappear into a mirage; caravans climb overnight from about -100 m to about 2,300 m. — [SwissEduc 2008](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Wind blows south from March to September and east-west from September to March; hearths go upwind of camps. — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Highland growing season 90-120 days; rains roughly June to September. — [CyberLeninka exclosure study](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/restoration-of-degraded-drylands-through-exclosures-enhancing-woody-species-diversity-and-soil-nutrients-in-the-highlands-of-tigray)

### Inferences
- **Two skies:** from the plateau (2,000-2,700 m), crisp, dark-blue sky and hard shadows; looking down into the basin, a flat milky-yellow or bleached-blue haze layer that swallows the far rim (a dramatic "edge of the world" view from the city shelf). From the foot, the escarpment glows red-orange in early morning light: the west-rim face looks east into the basin, so it is lit at sunrise and falls into its own shadow by mid-to-late afternoon, when the Slums at its foot are shaded first (inference from the stated west-rim position). The raised north rim faces south and is lit most of the day.
- **Weather events:** afternoon convective storms over the plateau in the rainy season, with dark anvils above the rim while the basin stays clear; flash floods down the gorge (caravaners avoid flood-high rivers, cited); morning mist and low cloud spilling over the rim lip on cool mornings (inference); dust devils on fans.
- **VFX seeds:** heat shimmer near the foot; mist tendrils at the falls spray; dust plumes behind caravans; spray rainbows in the falls in morning sun (beauty moment); chaff plumes from threshing floors on the plateau.

### Gaps
- No source found on mist or cloud spilling over the rim, or on visibility and haze from the escarpment top.

## Zone cards and cross-cutting asset seeds (all inference, built from the findings above)

### A. Escarpment face, benches and boulder fans (foot below the city; Slums ground)
- **Ground (walking scale):** coarse gravel fan of mixed colours (red sandstone, cream limestone, black basalt, grey-green basement); sand runnels; cracked silt in hollows; scattered blocks 0.3-3 m; occasional giant tillite erratics up to 6 m (cited size).
- **Landforms:** 20-80 m cliff bands; talus cones at cliff bases; benches 10-200 m wide; fault steps; gorge mouth with a fan apex.
- **Water and minerals:** white gypsum crust, dark varnish, seep lines.
- **Vegetation:** sparse umbrella acacia, Prosopis thicket by water, aloes and euphorbias on ledges; bare elsewhere.
- **Wildlife and signs (inference; not sourced in this pass):** hyrax latrines (white urine streaks on ledges), baboon troops on ledges, vultures and lammergeier on thermals, goat tracks and droppings, jackals (named as a camp threat, cited).
- **Human traces:** goat pens of thorn branches, trail cairns, three-stone hearths, dung, salt fragments.
- **Asset seeds:** sandstone, limestone and basalt boulder sets (3 sizes each); talus scatter decals; gravel fan ground material with colour-mix masks; gypsum crust decal; desert varnish streak decal; seep-line moss strip; acacia (umbrella, browsed hourglass), Prosopis thicket, aloe rosette with red spike, candelabra euphorbia (healthy, shrivelled, toppled), dead acacia snag; thorn-brush corral; cairn.
- **Beauty and grit:** red-ochre face in dawn light above a black-shadowed fan; deliberate grit: carcasses and bone piles of collapsed pack animals (collapse under load cited) in a gully at the foot of the climb.

### B. The falls gorge
- **Ground:** wet polished basement slabs, tufa crusts, travertine rimstone pools, sand bars, flood debris lines (driftwood and dung at a fixed height).
- **Landforms:** slot 5-30 m wide with vertical red sandstone walls 30-100 m; tufa curtain at the lip; relict dry tufa dams upstream, cut through (cited as a regional landform).
- **Vegetation:** figs at the spring, ferns and moss on wet walls, reeds in pools.
- **Asset seeds:** tufa curtain mesh (modular), rimstone pool set, moss and algae streak decals, wet-rock shader with dripping, spray and mist VFX, rainbow, falling water ribbons, fig tree (buttressed roots), fern clumps, flood debris line decal.
- **Beauty:** cream tufa and green moss against red walls; spray rainbow at morning. **Grit:** latrine runoff from the Slums where they draw water.

### C. Caravan climb and city shelf
- **Ground:** packed earth and gravel tread, worn polished steps, dung-stained hairpins.
- **Structures:** dry-stone revetment walls 1-4 m, drop-side kerbs, cross drains, rest flats with hearths, circular corrals, salt stacks in rows, gabion retaining walls for a later "graded" road.
- **Props (from cited facts):** 7 kg salt blocks and stacks of 20 or 30 per animal load; goatskin water sacks; pack pads, hooks, fibre and leather straps; large baskets; camel strings nose-to-tail; donkeys; three-stone tea hearths (15-25 cm stones); bread hearth (73-171 cm ring of small stones); fodder piles in corral centres; Aksumite-style sherd scatter and obsidian flakes for ancient-camp decals.
- **City:** hidmo blocks, curved courtyard walls, flat earth roofs with parapets, straw drying on roofs, small high windows, low doors, a church or shrine with old juniper grove at the shelf edge.

### D. Plateau farmland and villages
- **Ground:** red-brown and dark grey ploughed soil, stubble, stony grazing land, gully walls.
- **Structures:** stone-faced bunds (0.5-0.75 m tall, 1 m base, 30 cm top, medium stones; cited), terraces stepping slopes, check dams 1-2 m tall (stone, gabion, mortar; cited), silt fields behind dams, threshing floors, haystacks, cactus and euphorbia hedges, exclosure boundary walls, hidmo farmsteads.
- **Vegetation:** teff, sorghum, millet, barley, chickpea fields by season; Dodonaea, Euclea, Acacia etbaica scrub; dead or living junipers and olives; eucalyptus plantations (cited as present); cactus pear with and without cochineal.
- **Asset seeds:** bund wall modular kit; check dam (stone and gabion variants) with sluice gap; crop field materials for four seasons; sorghum and teff foliage cards; sheaf, haystack, threshing floor; maresha plough and yoke; oxen; winnowing fork; cactus pear plant (healthy, drought-wrinkled, cochineal-killed); exclosure sign or cairn; eroded gully mesh.
- **Beauty:** golden November barley against black basalt cliffs and the hazy drop to the basin. **Grit:** shrinking stream, cochineal-killed cactus fields, dead junipers, stumps (cited).
