# Salt flats, brine margins and the Afar salt trade (Lake Asale / Karum, Danakil): visual field guide

Scope: (a) the salt-flat zone, (b) the brine-pool margin, (c) the real salt-cutting operation and caravans, as the visual basis for George's Act I pan, its west-arm salt cuttings and the salt track to the Slums. Real Danakil/Afar first. Other salt pans appear only as labelled ANALOGUES.

Reading key:
- **Cited findings** are paraphrased from the linked source.
- **Inferences** are my reasoning, art direction or analogue transfer. Hex values are always inference.
- Where sources disagree, both are given.

Source quality, best first:
1. Woldekiros 2019, a peer-reviewed archaeology/ethnography paper (*Chungara*).
2. Warren (Salt Work Consultants), an evaporite geologist's review drawing on Holwerda & Hutchinson 1968 and later work.
3. Lake Karum microbiome paper (PMC).
4. Lasser et al. arXiv on salt polygons (analogue mechanics).
5. AFP via Arab News 2017; SwissEduc expedition pages; NASA/ESA image notes.
6. Tour and travel blogs, used for eyewitness texture only. They are often unsourced and inconsistent.

## 1. Salt crust at walking scale: polygons, thickness, colour, wet and dry states, margins, seasons

### Takeaway
The Asale pan is a bedded halite crust laid down in centimetre-thick layers with millimetre-to-centimetre mud partings. Its surface is ridged into (mega)polygons and is re-flooded and re-crusted every year, so it is never perfectly smooth. Thin wind-driven brine sheets can cross it in hours and turn it into a mirror. Measured analogues give polygons about 0.5-3 m across (usually 1-2 m) with ridges up to about 20-25 cm high. The Danakil-specific polygon dimensions were not found.

### Cited findings
**Size and setting**
- The halite-floored pan is about 40 km long and 10 km wide, in the deepest sump of the northern Danakil. It lies about 5 km from the foot of the western escarpment, below the Balakia Mountains — [Warren, Danakhil Potash 1 of 4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- Lake Assale's water surface is about −115 m. Do not confuse it with Lake Asal in Djibouti (−155 m) — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- Other figures for the surface level are −116 m ([NASA EO](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/curiosities-of-the-danakil-depression-84239)) and −120 m ([Wikipedia: Lake Karum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Karum)).

**Crust structure and pattern**
- The surface is a bedded salt crust, "mostly megapolygonal". It is made of stacked, upward-pointing halite chevrons. Each layer is centimetres thick, and layers are separated by mm-to-cm mud layers. The pan is described as "polygonally ridged" — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- In the core, the deeper Upper Rock Salt is coarse, massive halite in brown-reddish, yellow and grey-to-colourless varieties. This is subsurface, but it is what a quarry wall or cut face would expose — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- One visitor saw snow-white flats broken by "polygonal crazing", with distant brown humps that turned out to be camels — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- A tour page describes the white crust pattern as "squares" rather than hexagons — [Road to Danakil](https://www.roadtodanakil.com/danakil-depression/lake-assale-karum/)
- Another says it looks "more like an Arctic desert than a saline lake", with white crust in irregular patterns — [Brilliant Ethiopia](https://www.brilliant-ethiopia.com/region/danakil-depression/salt-lakes)
- The crust is layered and compacted, splits into plates, and carries cut lines and worn tracks. This source is unsourced and low quality — [Danakil Insider](https://danakilinsider.com/danakil-salt-mining/)

**Flooding, brine sheets and renewal**
- Frequent southerly winds push the Lake Assale brine edge back and forth across the pan. Thin brine sheets can cross kilometres in a few hours and leave new halite behind — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- Rare rainstorms send sheetfloods off the wadi belt that lay silt-mud layers on the pan. Groundwater inflow roughly equals outflow, so the brineflat is stable under today's climate — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- In the June-September rains, plateau runoff floods the flats and a new crust forms each year. This leaves workers a "virtually virgin" surface — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- Another page says the flats are completely flooded in the next rainy season, and that areas already cut leave empty white spaces — [Independent Travellers](https://independent-travellers.com/ethiopia/danakil_depression/salt_mine/)
- Lake Asale is dry over most of its extent and holds water only in the rainy season — [The One-Armed Crab](https://theonearmedcrab.com/hamed-ela-and-lake-asale/)
- A thin water layer turns the salt into a sky mirror — [Road to Danakil](https://www.roadtodanakil.com/danakil-depression/lake-assale-karum/)
- The pan grades into clear water so gradually that the shoreline is hard to place — [Brilliant Ethiopia](https://www.brilliant-ethiopia.com/region/danakil-depression/salt-lakes)

**Total evaporite thickness (conflicting)**
- "Up to 1,000 m" — [Independent Travellers](https://independent-travellers.com/ethiopia/danakil_depression/salt_mine/)
- "Up to 3 km" — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- 800 m — [Wikipedia: Danakil Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Desert)
- None of these is the *surface crust* thickness that matters for art.

**Pan area (conflicting)**
- ~50 km² — [Road to Danakil](https://www.roadtodanakil.com/danakil-depression/lake-assale-karum/)
- ~200 km² — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- 40 × 10 km — [Warren](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- The tour figures probably refer to the worked or flooded area only.

**ANALOGUE: polygon mechanics at Owens Lake, Badwater (Death Valley) and Sua Pan** ([Lasser et al., arXiv 1902.03600](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.03600))
- Polygons are about 1-2 m across. Measured pattern wavelengths range from 0.53 ± 0.20 m to 3.02 ± 1.40 m between sites.
- Ridges are narrow and cover about 24 ± 8% of the surface. Elevation maps span about 0-20 cm, with peaks to about 25 cm.
- Crusts run from under 1 cm to about 30 cm thick.
- Ridges sit above downwelling plumes of salty groundwater and grow about twice as fast as polygon centres. At Owens Lake that is 1.4-1.8 vs 0.6-0.9 mm per month. Sua Pan ridges briefly reached about 10 mm per month while thrusting.
- A crust takes about six months to develop at Owens Lake. The same polygon pattern is reported at Salar de Uyuni, Chott el Djerid, Dasht-e Kavir and the Abu Dhabi sabkhas.

### Inferences
**Ground tiers to build.** These combine the Warren layering, the annual flood renewal and the analogue dimensions.
- **Fresh flat crust** (just after a flood): near-smooth, crisp white, faint 1-2 m polygon lines only 1-3 cm high.
- **Mature polygon crust**: 1-2 m cells with ridges 5-20 cm high, often doubled, upturned or slightly thrust plates at the ridge.
- **Old or broken crust**: plates tilted and buckled, with dust in the hollows.
- Warren's "megapolygonal" suggests a second, larger order of cracks, likely tens of metres, on which the metre-scale ridges sit. This is an inference; no dimension was found.
- Two nested tiling textures would sell this: a 1-2 m ridge pattern plus a sparse 10-30 m crack network.

**Cut faces.** Because the crust is layered, every quarry cut face should show white halite bands a few cm thick, striped with thin brown-grey mud partings. This ties directly to the 5-6 cm slab thickness (section 4). Deeper faces can show reddish, yellow and grey coarse salt.

**Colour states.**
- Dry crust: chalk-white, with a faint blue cast in sun from the sky.
- Ridge tops: whitest, because fresh precipitate collects there.
- Polygon hollows: catch wind-blown dust, so grey-beige.
- After a sheetflood: brown silt films and drapes (Warren).
- Wet brine sheet: near-black at grazing angles, mirror at low sun.
- Damp crust: translucent grey.

**Mudcracks.** Expect them where the fans' silt reaches the pan edge (Warren's sheetflood silt-mud). These are classic desiccation polygons in brown mud, cracks a few cm wide, cells about 10-40 cm. That is general geology knowledge, not Danakil-specific measurement.

**Seasons (author's dying-world framing).**
- The real pan re-floods every summer.
- For a drying basin, read "fresh crust" as rare and stale.
- Show older, dustier, buckled crust as the norm, with mirror sheets only in the lowest arm near the brine pools.

### Gaps
- No measured polygon size, ridge height or surface crust thickness was found for Asale itself. The analogue numbers above are all I have.
- No sourced description of salt blisters, pressure ridges or "teepee" structures at Asale. Warren states only that the crust is megapolygonal.
- No source describes Asale at different times of day apart from mirage and midday heat (section 7).

## 2. Landforms (1-100 m) and skyline (km) around the pan

### Takeaway
The pan is ringed by:
- active wadi-fed alluvial fans (bajada) on the west, under the escarpment about 5 km away;
- a broad gypsum plain with pavements on the east and northeast, the shoreline of a higher former lake;
- eroded salt mountains with steep valleys and knife-edged spines to the north;
- abrupt red rock outcrops and the Dallol mound;
- dark basalt volcanoes (Gada Ale) to the south.

### Cited findings
**Margins and plains**
- Wadi-fed alluvial fans (bajada) fringe the pan, with the most active on the west. The Musley fan has cut into the gypsum pavement and feeds water and sediment straight onto the salt-flat edge — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- Most of the area east and northeast of the pan is an extensive gypsum plain. Its gypsum pavements mark a higher, less saline former lake stage, still below sea level — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- Subaqueous gypsum rim deposits stand above today's pan level along the western margin — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- Brine springs feed dry mudflats. Hydrothermal springs and collapse dolines lie near the Dallol mound — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)

**Salt and rock landforms**
- Further north, rain has cut huge salt mountains into steep valleys, leaving "sharp spines" along the eroded surfaces — [The One-Armed Crab](https://theonearmedcrab.com/hamed-ela-and-lake-asale/)
- Grey-brown salt towers rise in jagged shapes near Dallol — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- The "Asale Red Rocks" rise abruptly from the salt sheet, with a small water hole beside them — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)

**Volcanoes and the rift frame**
- Gada Ale is a 287 m basaltic stratovolcano southwest of Lake Karum, with gentle slopes. Its youngest lava flows are the darkest — [ESA Earth from Space 2025](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2025/11/Earth_from_Space_The_Danakil_Depression)
- A 2 km-wide salt dome southwest of Gada Ale has pushed ancient lava up to 100 m high — [NASA EO](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/curiosities-of-the-danakil-depression-84239)
- The depression lies between the Danakil Alps to the east and the Ethiopian Plateau to the west — [NASA EO](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/curiosities-of-the-danakil-depression-84239)
- The caravan descent runs from Mekelle at about 2,300 m down to about −100 m — [SwissEduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html). Woldekiros gives highlands above 2,000 m down to about 200 m below sea level over about 160 km — [Woldekiros 2019](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)

### Inferences
**Skyline from the west-arm cuttings.**
- West: a high, layered escarpment wall close by (about 5 km in reality). Stage it as the red-ochre and grey stratified rim in the reference.
- East: a low, pale gypsum plain.
- North: a broken skyline of salt spines and the Dallol mound.
- South: dark basalt cones.
- Because the pan is perfectly flat, any vertical form reads at huge distance and floats in mirage. Use a few strong silhouettes rather than clutter.

**Landform props at 1-100 m.**
- Fan-toe gravel lobes with braided dry channels 0.5-3 m wide.
- Gypsum pavement steps 0.2-1 m high (the old shoreline benches).
- Isolated salt spines and pinnacles, 2-15 m tall, fluted by rain.
- Red rock knobs standing straight out of white salt.
- Collapse dolines (pits) near spring zones.

### Gaps
- No measured heights or widths for the salt spines, red rocks or gypsum benches were found. The sizes above are art inference.
- I did not separately research gypsum dune form, which the map places near the pan. Warren notes only the gypsum plain and pavements.

## 3. Brine pools and lakes (Lake Karum/Asale and margins): colour, chemistry, depth, rims, smell, rafts

### Takeaway
Real Lake Karum is a shallow, ephemeral, hypersaline brine sheet. The sampled water was 0-50 cm deep and 28-30 °C, and the brine is unusually calcium-rich. Eyewitnesses describe it as clear and reflective, not pink. Its microbial life is 60-88% haloarchaea, the very group that colours crystalliser ponds pink-red elsewhere. The map's pink brine pools are therefore a plausible stylisation, not a documented Asale look.

### Cited findings
**Chemistry and depth** ([Lake Karum metagenome study, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13595746))
- Lake Karum is "elongated, shallow, ephemeral, and highly saline". It periodically floods the Assale salt plain after rain and is fed by underground water that dissolves older salt.
- Sampling sites:
  - emergent crust with no standing water;
  - shallow water 5-10 cm deep;
  - the deepest point sampled, 40-50 cm.
- Water was 28-30 °C, pH 6.7-8.0, water activity 0.746-0.760.
- Ions (mg/kg): Na 92,500-137,000; Ca 20,100-25,700; K 4,110-6,050; Mg 3,880-4,500.
- Microbes: Halobacteriota (haloarchaea) make up 60-88% of sequences, with *Salinibacter*, cyanobacteria (*Dactylococcopsis*, *Halothece*) and some green-algal chloroplasts. *Dunaliella* (the orange-red brine alga) was not detected.
- The paper does not describe water colour or pigments.

**Appearance**
- The pan becomes "submerged in clear water" so gradually that the edge is hard to find, and the surface reflects — [Brilliant Ethiopia](https://www.brilliant-ethiopia.com/region/danakil-depression/salt-lakes)
- A thin water layer mirrors the sky — [Road to Danakil](https://www.roadtodanakil.com/danakil-depression/lake-assale-karum/)
- A pool of brine and mud lies beneath the salt crust — [Road to Danakil](https://www.roadtodanakil.com/danakil-depression/lake-assale-karum/)
- In false-colour satellite views, Karum and Bakili appear black (an effect of the NIR band). Dallol's hydrothermal field shows pink in that false-colour view, which is a display artefact, not true colour — [ESA 2025](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2025/11/Earth_from_Space_The_Danakil_Depression)

**Nearby pools**
- By the Asale Red Rocks, a small water hole dried on swimmers' skin within seconds, leaving salt crust — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- A nearby oval pit ("Lake Assal" in that blog, probably the Black Lake/Gaet'ale-type pool, not Karum) held yellowish-brown, bubbling, oily-looking but cold liquid. Its margin mud is said to be used for skin, and the water is said to be fatal to drink — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/). Low-reliability identification.

**Comparator: Lake Afrera (Afdera)**, the other Danakil salt lake
- Shimmering green, with salt mounds on its shores.
- Afar collect salt there in rectangular shoreline evaporation basins.
- Source: [Brilliant Ethiopia](https://www.brilliant-ethiopia.com/region/danakil-depression/salt-lakes)
- Modern lake-fed evaporation ponds also operate near Asale. A 2017 plant manager said their salt is higher quality but dearer than mined blocks — [AFP via Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)

**Margin hydrology**
- Fan water salinity rises from about 760 ppm to over 23,400 ppm toward the salt flat — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- On the route from Dodom to Hamed Ela, a salty spring waters hundreds of camels — [The One-Armed Crab](https://theonearmedcrab.com/hamed-ela-and-lake-asale/)

### Inferences
**Why pink is defensible.** Haloarchaea carry red bacterioruberin pigments, and *Salinibacter* is also red-pigmented. Where brine sits concentrated and still, in shallow evaporating pockets, analogue saltworks crystallisers turn pink to red. This is general microbiology, not a Karum observation.

**Recommended pink pool look.** Show pink only in small, shallow, isolated evaporating pools at the margin and in quarry pits. Keep the main brine sheet clear: glassy, reflecting sky, grading to milky turquoise over white salt. This keeps the realism defensible and makes pink a jewel accent.

**Rim crusts.** Crusts on evaporating pools commonly show:
- concentric bathtub rings of white halite at successive lower levels, each a few cm apart vertically;
- thin floating halite "rafts" and skin, millimetre plates that sink as hopper crystals;
- cauliflower efflorescence on wet edges.

This is analogue knowledge, not Asale-sourced. The calcium-chloride-rich brine (Ca about 2-2.5%) is hygroscopic. That suggests the margins stay dark and damp-looking even in heat, with sticky grey-black mud under a thin crust. Again an inference.

**Smell (inference).** Clean halite pans are near-odourless. Anoxic black mud under the crust in analogue pans smells of hydrogen sulphide (rotten egg) when broken. Treat this as an audio/VFX cue: bubbles and sulphur haze only where the mud is churned.

### Gaps
- No true-colour description of Karum beyond "clear" was found.
- No source documents pink colour at Asale.
- Nothing found on salt rafts, smell or rim-ring geometry at Karum.
- No maximum lake depth or seasonal area figures were found.
- The yellow and black pools near Dallol are a separate zone and were not researched here.

## 4. The Afar salt-cutting operation: tools, slab names and sizes, roles, pay, daily rhythm

### Takeaway
Crews cut long grooves in the crust with axes, push wooden sticks into the grooves as levers, and pry up large layered plates. Shapers then trim them into standard tiles about 22-24 × 30-36 × 5-6 cm. Highland Tigrayan labourers do the lifting; Afar shapers do the shaping. Work starts before dawn and stops for midday heat. Names and weights vary by source:
- ganfur ~4 kg, ghelao ~8 kg;
- a 7 kg standard block;
- Woldekiros's gole'o, gerawayni and ankarabe sizes.

### Cited findings
**Tools and method**
- Workers chop the crust into large slabs with an axe, then fit sticks into the axe grooves and use them as levers to lift each slab — [Independent Travellers](https://independent-travellers.com/ethiopia/danakil_depression/salt_mine/)
- Photo captions there show homemade tools for stripping the mud layer and carving slabs square — same source
- Afar quarry the bedded pan salt by hand with axes and wooden levers — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- Hundreds of men cut salt each morning with hand axes — [AFP via Arab News 2017](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)
- Sticks lift a large slab, which is then cut into standard tiles — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- Axes, wedges and poles are listed in [Ecotourismnet](https://www.ecotourismnet.com/2026/05/where-salt-shapes-survival-afar-trails.html), which is uncited on mechanics.

**Slab standards (sources conflict)**
- **Woldekiros 2019 (peer-reviewed, p. 105).** The Afar cut three standard forms, once used as currency. No weights are given — [Chungara PDF](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
  - **gole'o**: 24 × 36 × 6 cm
  - **gerawayni**: 23 × 30 × 6 cm
  - **ankarabe**: 22 × 30 × 5 cm
- **Weight-named tiles: ganfur about 4 kg and ghelao about 8 kg** — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf); [Independent Travellers](https://independent-travellers.com/ethiopia/danakil_depression/salt_mine/); [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- Stock-photo captions from 28 March 2013 use "amole-salt slabs of the ganfur-4 kg size" — [Alamy](https://www.alamy.com/stock-photo-danakil-ethiopia-march-28-afar-worker-loads-dromedary-with-amole-salt-101740712.html); [Stocklib](https://www.stocklib.com/media-1-55245384/stacks-of-amole-salt-slabs-of-the-ganfur-4-kgsize-ready-to-be-loaded-on-dromedary-camels-and-donkeys-to-transport-them-to-berahile-town-75-kmsaway-across-the-danakil-desert-afar-region-ethiopia.html)
- A 4-kilo tile is called ganfur — [FairPlanet](https://www.fairplanet.org/story/the-hottest-place-on-earth-the-salt-mines-of-danakil-depression/)
- The Hadali Mera shape blocks of "precisely 7 kg", and one block is the trade's unit of currency — [SwissEduc 2008](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Salt chunks weigh about 7 kg and were historically currency — [AFP 2017](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)
- **Amole** are the smaller blocks re-cut in the highlands for markets such as Mekelle and Axum — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- A museum "1 amoli" salt bar measures 198 × 45 × 58 mm, of salt and natural fibre — [Bank of Canada Museum](https://www.bankofcanadamuseum.ca/collection/artefact/view/1981.0050.00001.000/ethiopia-1-amoli)
- A Harvard collection item is titled "Block of salt broken into three pieces and tied together with a leaf, Ethiopia" — [Harvard Art Museums image record](https://haaimagehosting.omeka.fas.harvard.edu/items/show/1318). Title only seen.

**Roles** ([SwissEduc 2008](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html))
- **Focolo**, "salt lifters": poor immigrants from Tigray at the bottom of the local social scale. They leave Hamed Ela ("Ahmedela") as early as 3 am.
- **Hadali Mera**: always Afar. They shape the blocks, starting at dawn.
- **Arho**: the cameliers. They load each camel with the same amount, which is later taxed.
- **Shumbahri**: the chief, who gives each caravan a night departure slot.

**Roles in other sources**
- Tigray miners dig out the slab; Afar workers cut and shape it to standard sizes. Other actors are warehouse and shop owners, women who make water sacks, tea and coffee vendors, and salt producers — [Woldekiros 2019 pp. 104-105](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- A father and son work as one crew — [Independent Travellers](https://independent-travellers.com/ethiopia/danakil_depression/salt_mine/)
- "Haro" workers break and chip slabs into rectangles every day except Friday — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- Hundreds or thousands of animals arrive daily except Friday in the dry season — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- Merchants must pay a salt-collection tax before leaving — [FairPlanet](https://www.fairplanet.org/story/the-hottest-place-on-earth-the-salt-mines-of-danakil-depression/)

**Pay and output**
- About 1 birr per tile, up to 200 tiles a day. A miner walks about 2 hours each way and works early — [Independent Travellers](https://independent-travellers.com/ethiopia/danakil_depression/salt_mine/); [FairPlanet](https://www.fairplanet.org/story/the-hottest-place-on-earth-the-salt-mines-of-danakil-depression/)
- One miner quoted 500 birr (about $22) a day in 2017 — [AFP 2017](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)
- FairPlanet also claims 750 registered miners and 1.3 million t a year. That conflicts with Woldekiros's 10,000-20,000 t a year carried by about 70,000 animals ([p. 96](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)). Treat FairPlanet's tonnage as doubtful.

**Daily rhythm**
- Before dawn, the Focolo walk out. At dawn, thousands of camels leave Hamed Ela in assigned order for the lake centre.
- Mid-morning they arrive; the exhausted camels chew salt.
- At noon they turn back. Caravans then travel through the night — [SwissEduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- A second account has caravans leaving the lake each late afternoon and travelling only by night — [The One-Armed Crab](https://theonearmedcrab.com/hamed-ela-and-lake-asale/)

### Inferences
**Weight check (my arithmetic).** Halite density is about 2.16 g/cm³, and slab porosity is unknown.
- gole'o: 5,184 cm³, about 11 kg solid.
- gerawayni: 4,140 cm³, about 9 kg.
- ankarabe: 3,300 cm³, about 7 kg.

So the 7 kg standard block is consistent with the ankarabe or gerawayni. The ~8 kg "ghelao" may be the same as the gole'o, given porosity and spelling drift; that link is speculation. A ~4 kg ganfur would be roughly half the area or thickness, about 22 × 15-30 × 3-5 cm.

**Recommended game standard.**
- One hero slab: 24 × 36 × 6 cm, about 8-10 kg.
- A smaller, thinner variant: 22 × 30 × 5 cm.
- A half tile: about 22 × 15 × 5 cm.
- Amole bars for the Slums market: 20 × 4.5 × 6 cm, fibre-wrapped.

**How the pit should look.**
- Rectangular strip workings a slab-thickness or two deep (5-15 cm), in long rows of grooved lines.
- Pried-up plates propped on edge, with offcut chips in white drifts.
- Sticks still jammed in grooves.
- Trimmed blocks stood on edge in rows to dry.
- A brown mud layer scraped off the slab undersides into dirty heaps beside the pit.
- The cut floor floods with brine first. Use this as the location for pink pools.

**Story fit.** The Focolo is a real lowest-status, poor migrant labour class doing the heaviest lifting, under an Afar shaper elite and a chief who allocates slots and taxes loads. This maps cleanly onto captive cutters, overseers, a tallyman or tax post, and slab-as-currency tokens. The caravan departure order fits a gate or ledger prop.

### Gaps
- No exact Afar names for the axe or the lever stick were found.
- No detail on the shapers' trimming tool (adze, small axe, blade) beyond "homemade tools".
- How the slabs are marked or checked for size is unknown.
- How Woldekiros's names map onto ganfur and ghelao is unresolved.
- Trojer's ethnographic film *Arho* (2021, 13 months of fieldwork) would likely answer these, but only its abstract was reachable — [Journal of Anthropological Films](https://boap.uib.no/index.php/jaf/article/view/3146)

## 5. Loading, caravans, route, camps and debris

### Takeaway
Each camel carries about 20-30 slabs, a roughly 120-140 kg load, slung on hide, wool or cloth pads stuffed with chaff or feathers. Wooden pack hooks hold the slabs, and the lashings are grass-fibre and leather ropes. Caravans are single-file nose-to-tail strings, travelling mostly by night.

The route runs Lake Asale → Hamed Ela → Berahile (about 75 km, 2-3 days) → Mekelle. Caravans overnight every ~20 km at camps for up to 1,000 men and 6,000 animals. The trail is marked by footpaths, dung, bones, worn gear and three-stone hearths. Since about 2010, trucks have been replacing camels.

### Cited findings
**Loads**
- Each camel carries 20 blocks to Mekelle — [SwissEduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Up to thirty bricks of about 4 kg each per camel — [Wikipedia: Danakil Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Desert)

**Loading gear** ([Woldekiros 2019, Table 1, p. 106](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf))
- Pack pads **gula** and **kuda**: hide, wool or cloth stuffed with feathers or chaff.
- Pack hooks: **anabu** (horizontal), and **koree** and **koyeta** (vertical). Koyeta are used only for salt.
- Ropes and straps of grass fibre and leather.
- Goatskin water sacks (**sar**), jerry cans, and flour sacks (**madaberia**).

**Padding and stringing in practice**
- Some owners pad camels thickly, others with a single thin pad, and camels' backs are scarred — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- Camels are tied together and led single file on foot — same source
- Donkeys are used by those who can't afford camels — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- Donkeys are the main transport over rough ground — [FairPlanet](https://www.fairplanet.org/story/the-hottest-place-on-earth-the-salt-mines-of-danakil-depression/). These two sources conflict in emphasis.
- Mules also accompany the caravans — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)

**Volumes and caravan size**
- About 2,000 camels and 1,000 donkeys a day carry tiles about 75 km to Berahile — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf); [FairPlanet](https://www.fairplanet.org/story/the-hottest-place-on-earth-the-salt-mines-of-danakil-depression/)
- In the dry season, hundreds of camels move in a single endless line — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- Strings contain dozens of animals — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- Woldekiros's sample was 158 caravaners with 566 camels, 196 donkeys and 11 mules — [p. 99](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- About 5,000 blocks a day arrive at a trading post on a dry riverbed at the edge of Berahile (2017) — [AFP](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)

**Route and timing**
- Two days by night to Berahile market, then on to Mekelle. Caravans return empty a few days later — [The One-Armed Crab](https://theonearmedcrab.com/hamed-ela-and-lake-asale/)
- Three days to Berahile, formerly seven — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- Formerly a four-day trek to Mekelle "through rock-strewn gullies". After the tarmac to Berahile (about 2012), caravans end at Berahile and trucks take over — [AFP 2017](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)
- The Afar arho leads only to the edge of Afar country at Berahile — [SwissEduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Since 2010, trucks have slowly replaced caravans — [Trojer 2021](https://boap.uib.no/index.php/jaf/article/view/3146)
- The return route follows a dirt road along the Saba canyon past Afar villages to Berahile — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)
- Guards protect the camels from bandits — [Wikipedia: Danakil Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Desert)

**Route details from Woldekiros 2019** ([pp. 101-108](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf))
- The trail is about 160 km. About 72 km of it is lowland caravan route, from −120 m to +658 m.
- The last ~7 km to the flat is a "rugged, wet sandy plain".
- Lowland towns: Korha, Darguni, Berhaile, Melabdi, Asa Bolo (Sabba), Hamed Ela.
- Lowland caravaners take 3-4 days each way and make 38-54 trips a year. Highlanders take 7-10 days each way and make about 14 trips a year.

**Camps** ([Woldekiros 2019, pp. 105-106](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf))
- Overnight every ~20 km: arrive about 7 pm, leave about 3 am. Short breaks last 1-2 h, long ones 4-6 h.
- Sites are chosen for water, grazing and shade (acacia or rock shelters), on flat vegetated valley floors, with fires sheltered from wind.
- Camp layout:
  - an animal corral with feed piled in the centre;
  - an unloading and gear area where men sleep on their saddles;
  - a hearth area.
- Tea and coffee hearths are three-stone rings 15-25 cm across. Bread hearths are 73-171 cm across, with small stones forming a griddle.
- Big camps hold 700-1,000 men and about 6,000 animals.
- Animals are tied together at night. Salt piles are laid in two rows enclosed by sleeping spaces against theft.
- Camps sit near villages for food, water and protection from jackals, foxes and raids.

**Debris and archaeology** ([Woldekiros 2019, pp. 103-108](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf))
- The trail is marked by footpaths, animal bones, dung and worn gear. Modern and ancient campsites lie side by side.
- Excavated camp deposits are 30-40 cm deep. They contain obsidian and chert flakes, Aksumite-era (about 5th-century) brown-ware sherds, cereal grains, dung and cooking stones.

**Hamed Ela village**
- About 4,000 people, wholly dependent on salt — [The One-Armed Crab](https://theonearmedcrab.com/hamed-ela-and-lake-asale/)
- Rudimentary huts of acacia branches, no electricity, open cooking fires on hauled wood, water by truck — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- A miner says Hamed Ela is a settlement of huts with poor water and food — [AFP](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)

### Inferences
**Loaded camel silhouette.** A wide, flat-topped double pannier of slabs stacked on edge in two banks, one on each flank. Each bank is about 10-15 slabs, held between vertical wooden hooks or frames (koree/koyeta) and lashed with pale grass rope and dark leather straps over a lumpy padded hide or cloth saddle-pad. Woldekiros lists the hooks and ropes but not the stacking geometry, so the two-bank layout is my reading of the stock-photo captions and should be checked against the photos listed in section 8.
- Load about 120-140 kg.
- The load stands about 40-60 cm above the hump and is about 1-1.2 m wide.

**Donkey load.** Smaller, about 4-8 slabs (inference: donkey loads are typically 50-80 kg). Two small banks on a cloth pad.

**Caravan track at walking scale.**
- A braided band of hoof-trodden crust about 2-10 m wide. Crust is crushed to grey-brown granular salt and dust.
- A single-file centre groove 30-50 cm wide.
- Dung pellets, dropped broken slabs and chips, frayed grass-rope ends, and the occasional camel skeleton or scattered bones.
- Off the pan, it becomes stone-strewn gully paths.

Dropped broken slabs are not explicitly documented. They are a strong, plausible inference given brittle slabs and huge throughput.

**Rest stop kit.**
- Rings of three blackened stones.
- Larger stone griddle hearths.
- Dung-trodden corral patches with chaff and grass piles.
- Saddles laid in rows as beds.
- Salt stacked in two guarded rows.
- An acacia shade tree or rock overhang.

**Slums and Berahile-style terminus.** The real Berahile trading post sits on a dry riverbed. Use this for the Slums' salt yard: stacks of slabs on a sandy wadi floor, a tax tally, and a truck-era equivalent such as carts.

### Gaps
- The exact stacking geometry (on edge vs flat, how many per side) and knot types are unverified. Check them in the reference photos.
- No detail on mats specifically (palm-leaf mats are often assumed but not found).
- Donkey slab counts were not found.
- There is no text description of what a camp looks like after abandonment on the pan itself; Woldekiros's camps are on the route.

## 6. Vegetation and wildlife on the pan and at its margins

### Takeaway
Nothing grows on the salt pan; no source mentions any life on it beyond microbes in the brine. Sourced margin vegetation is thin: acacia (shade trees, hut branches) and wild grasses along the route. Invasive mesquite (*Prosopis juliflora*) covers about 3,600 km² of the Afar region. Sourced wildlife is limited to jackals and foxes around caravan camps, and to the caravan animals themselves.

### Cited findings
- The pan supports a hypersaline microbial community: haloarchaea, *Salinibacter* and halophilic cyanobacteria — [Lake Karum metagenome](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13595746)
- Hamed Ela huts are built of acacia branches, and a vehicle route crushes "small trees" en route from Dodom — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/); [The One-Armed Crab](https://theonearmedcrab.com/hamed-ela-and-lake-asale/)
- Camps seek shade under acacia trees or rock shelters. Fodder is highland chaff plus wild lowland grass. Lowland water is very limited — [Woldekiros 2019 pp. 100-106](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- Jackals and foxes threaten caravan camps — [Woldekiros 2019 p. 105](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)
- *Prosopis juliflora* invades about 3,605 km² of the Afar region, with 5,024 km² more potential habitat (Wakie et al., *PLoS ONE* 2014) — [USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70138054). This is not specific to the Asale margin.
- Camels chew salt at the lake centre to recover energy — [SwissEduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Hamed Ela residents keep goats and sheep, drinking their milk and blood — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)

### Inferences
**Planting rule.**
- The pan and brine margin are barren: zero foliage.
- The first plants appear only on fan toes and wadi channels where fresh water seeps in, Warren's low-salinity fan water.
- Plausible halophyte and desert species there (NOT verified for Asale; common across Red Sea and Afar lowlands):
  - toothbrush tree (*Salvadora persica*), sprawling evergreen shrubs 1-3 m;
  - seablite (*Suaeda* spp.), low succulent cushions 0.3-1 m, greyish to red-tinged;
  - tamarisk (*Tamarix* spp.), feathery grey-green shrubs to 4 m;
  - umbrella thorn (*Acacia/Vachellia tortilis*), flat-topped, 3-8 m;
  - mesquite thickets (*Prosopis juliflora*), dense, thorny, 2-6 m, as the "invasive grit" option.
- Keep plants sparse: single shrubs tens of metres apart, dust-coated and half-dead.

**Wildlife signs (inference).**
- Camel and donkey dung, hoof prints, bones and carcasses on the track.
- Jackal tracks at camps.
- Flies around dung and carcasses.
- Possibly birds (ravens, vultures) over carcasses. Unsourced; a common pattern in desert caravan routes.

### Gaps
- I found no plant survey of the Asale pan margin or Hamed Ela specifically. All species beyond acacia and *P. juliflora* (Afar-wide) are unverified.
- No sourced wild animals on or at the pan.
- No sourced brine-fly or insect life at Karum.

## 7. Air, weather, heat, light, mirage and night

### Takeaway
This is one of the hottest inhabited places on Earth. Dallol's 1960-66 mean annual temperature was 34.6 °C, and the June mean maximum 46.7 °C. Midday on the pan exceeds 40 °C, and nights barely cool, to about 36 °C in one traveller's report. The air shows strong mirage and glare: caravans "disappear into a mirage", and the white salt blinds. Work and travel run before dawn and through the night. Southerly winds push brine across the pan, and rain is near zero.

### Cited findings
**Temperatures**
- Dallol (−92 m), 1960-66: mean annual 34.6 °C; mean daily high 41.2 °C and low 28.2 °C — [Wikipedia: Dallol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallol,_Ethiopia)
- Monthly mean max/min:
  - Jan 36.1/24.6
  - Mar 38.9/26.0
  - May 44.4/28.5
  - Jun 46.7/30.4
  - Jul 45.6/31.8
  - Sep 42.8/31.6
  - Nov 39.4/27.1
  - Dec 36.7/25.7
- The record high there is 49 °C. Other sources say daytime can exceed 50 °C — [Wikipedia: Danakil Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Desert); [AFP 2017](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1085506/amp)
- Midday temperatures well above 40 °C — [SwissEduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Hamed Ela reached 115 °F (46 °C), with about 97 °F (36 °C) overnight at camp. Each stop felt "like a thick, smothering blanket" — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)

**Rain (conflicting)**
- Under 25 mm a year, per the text; but the climate table there lists 84 mm a year on about 50 rain days — [Wikipedia: Danakil Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Desert)
- June-September rains flood the flats from plateau runoff — [Travel-Tour-Guide](https://www.travel-tour-guide.com/ethiopia_danakil_lalibela_pictures/7_salt_flats_camel_caraven_trip_photos.htm)

**Wind**
- Wind is frequently southerly and drives brine sheets — [Warren 1/4](https://www.saltworkconsultants.com/downloads/4%20Danakhil%20(1%20of%204).pdf)
- Camp wind is southerly from March to September and east-west from September to March — [Woldekiros p. 106](https://www.chungara.cl/Vols/2019/51-1/008-H_WOLDEKIROS.pdf)

**Light and rhythm**
- Caravans vanish into distant mirage — [SwissEduc](https://www.swisseduc.ch/stromboli/perm/erta/salt-caravan-2008-en.html)
- Intense glare off cracked white salt — [Ecotourismnet](https://www.ecotourismnet.com/2026/05/where-salt-shapes-survival-afar-trails.html)
- A camel train marches into the sunset — [Hole in the Donut](https://holeinthedonut.com/2018/06/09/danakil-depression-ethiopia/)
- Sky mirror on brine sheets — [Road to Danakil](https://www.roadtodanakil.com/danakil-depression/lake-assale-karum/)
- Night travel only — [The One-Armed Crab](https://theonearmedcrab.com/hamed-ela-and-lake-asale/)
- Tourist stops last 20-30 minutes because of the heat — [Brilliant Ethiopia](https://www.brilliant-ethiopia.com/region/danakil-depression/salt-lakes)

### Inferences
**Lighting states for the zone.**
1. Pre-dawn blue: cutters walking out, lanterns or torches, salt glowing faintly blue-grey.
2. Low gold sunrise: long shadows that pick out every polygon ridge. This is the best state for reading crust texture.
3. Midday white-out: near-shadowless, overexposed white, heat shimmer, and a mirage band that floats the escarpment and caravans above a false lake.
4. Late-afternoon departure: warm light and long caravan shadows.
5. Night: caravans by starlight and moon. The salt pan reflects moonlight so the ground glows; no light pollution.

The night-sky claim is inference.

**VFX.**
- A screen-space heat-haze layer, strongest near the ground.
- Mirage horizon strips: an inverted reflection of distant silhouettes.
- Low salt-dust drift on wind gusts; little dust on the crust itself, more where the track crushes it.
- Glare bloom on wet sheets.

### Gaps
- No sourced descriptions of night sky, dust storms or haze colour at Asale.
- No humidity data.
- Sunset colour descriptions are thin; the only source is the generic "into the sunset".

#### (b) BRINE-POOL MARGIN (pan's lowest ground)

1. **Ground**
   - Thin, damp, translucent grey crust over sticky black-grey mud. It breaks underfoot to show black anoxic mud.
   - Bathtub rings at the pool edges.
   - Cauliflower efflorescence.
   - Mudcrack polygons (10-40 cm cells) where fan silt reaches the margin.

2. **Landforms**
   - Shallow pools (5-50 cm deep, matching Karum's sampled depths) set in low hollows.
   - Collapse pits and dolines near springs.
   - Gypsum rim benches 0.2-1 m high (old shoreline) on the upslope side.
   - Skyline as in (a).

3. **Water and mineral**
   - Main sheet: clear, glassy, turquoise-milky over white salt.
   - Small evaporating pools: pink to red (stylised, haloarchaea-justified).
   - Floating halite rafts and skins.
   - Pool-floor hopper crystals.
   - Concentric white rim rings.
   - Brine seeping from crust cracks.

4. **Vegetation**
   - None at the brine. Microbial colour only.

5. **Wildlife**
   - None sourced. A few dead insects in rim crust is an option (inference).

6. **Human traces**
   - The flooded floors of old quarry pits turned into rectangular pink pools. This is a strong, readable story beat: the captives' workings fill with jewel-coloured brine.

7. **Air**
   - Hottest and stillest air.
   - Faint sulphide stink VFX where mud is churned (inference).
   - Strong reflections.

8. **Palette (approximate)**
   - Clear brine over salt `#BFE3DF`; shallow turquoise `#7CC6C0`.
   - Pink pool `#E8A3A9`; deep rose concentrate `#C9616F`.
   - Wet grey crust `#A9ADAF`; black mud `#262321`; rim white `#FAF9F6`.

9. **Asset seeds**
   - Brine water shader with three tints (clear, turquoise, pink) and a depth gradient.
   - Bathtub-ring edge decal.
   - Floating salt-raft particle and decal.
   - Hopper-crystal pool-floor mesh.
   - Efflorescence cauliflower clusters (5-30 cm).
   - Wet-crust and black-mud blend material with footprint-breakthrough decals.
   - Mudcrack material.
   - Rectangular flooded quarry pit kit (modular edges 1-4 m).
   - Gypsum bench rock set.
   - Bubbling mud VFX.
   - Heat-shimmer volume.

10. **Beauty and grit**
    - Beauty:
      - a chain of pink and turquoise pools like jewels on white;
      - a sunrise mirror of the escarpment in a still pool.
    - Grit:
      - black mud boot-holes;
      - the stink of broken crust;
      - a discarded slab dissolving into a pink pool.

#### (c) SALT CUTTINGS (west arm) and SALT TRACK

1. **Ground**
   - Rectangular strip workings 5-15 cm deep (one or two slab thicknesses) in rows.
   - Axe-groove lines.
   - Cut faces banded white with thin brown mud partings.
   - Offcut chip drifts.
   - Dirty heaps of scraped underside mud.

2. **Landforms**
   - The near escarpment and fan toes rising to the west (in reality about 5 km away), so the cutting site has the strongest skyline.
   - The track runs west off the pan onto fan gravel and rock-strewn gullies.

3. **Water**
   - Brine seeping into the newest pits, which become pink pools as they age.

4. **Vegetation**
   - None on the pan.
   - From the fan toe westward along the track: sparse acacia shade trees (sourced as camp shade), dusty halophyte shrubs and mesquite as verified-or-not options (see section 6).

5. **Wildlife**
   - Caravan animals: camels, donkeys, a few mules.
   - Jackals at camps.
   - Dung, hoof prints, bones.

6. **Human traces and material culture**
   - Tools:
     - long-handled axes with a narrow blade (inference on form);
     - wooden lever sticks about 1-1.5 m (length inferred) jammed in grooves;
     - small trimming axes or adzes;
     - mud scrapers.
   - Slabs:
     - hero gole'o 24 × 36 × 6 cm (sourced);
     - ankarabe 22 × 30 × 5 cm (sourced);
     - half tile;
     - fibre-wrapped amole bars about 20 × 4.5 × 6 cm (museum-sourced).
   - Stacks:
     - slabs stood on edge in rows;
     - ready-to-load stacks of 20-30 per camel-load (sourced counts);
     - two guarded rows of salt at camps (sourced).
   - Loading gear (Woldekiros-sourced names):
     - gula/kuda pack pads (hide, wool or cloth, stuffed);
     - wooden koree/koyeta vertical hooks and anabu horizontal hooks;
     - grass-fibre rope and leather straps;
     - goatskin water sacks, jerry cans, flour sacks.
   - Camp and village structures:
     - acacia-branch huts (Hamed Ela);
     - three-stone hearth rings 15-25 cm;
     - bread griddle hearths 73-171 cm;
     - saddles in rows as beds;
     - a corral with a central feed pile.
   - For the story:
     - an overseer or tax post with a tally (Shumbahri-style departure slots);
     - captive cutters' meagre camp at the pan edge, styled on Hamed Ela huts but harsher.

7. **Air and light**
   - Pre-dawn work under lamps.
   - Noon stop.
   - Caravans leaving at dusk into the night.

8. **Palette (approximate)**
   - Cut-face white `#EEEBE4`; mud parting `#6E5B49`.
