# Walking the Danakil from pass to plateau

Act I can be built as one colour journey through a single drying rift. The player moves from varnished black boulder fans under a red-ochre wall, past a green spring ribbon at the falls, across buff stone pavement and rust-stained washes, to a grey-white dead oasis. From there come the act's loudest colours (acid green and sulfur yellow on white salt), then the blinding salt pan, cream fossil reefs and chalky gypsum, black and rust basalt, a red-veined lava lake, and finally a cream, red and black gorge that climbs to red-brown terraced fields on the plateau. The notes give strong, cited detail for the salt pan and the Afar salt trade (slab sizes to the centimetre, tools, roles, camp layouts), for the Dallol hydrothermal field (pool sizes, pH, temperatures, the order its colours age in), for Erta Ale's lava lake and flows, for the Pleistocene fossil reefs, for Afar huts and graves, and for the Tigray escarpment, villages and dryland fields. They are weakest where artists most need walking-scale numbers: fan clast sizes, pavement, mud cracks and dune heights mostly come from other deserts. No real waterfall, dying spring or gypsum dune field in the Danakil was found, so those three zones are built on labelled analogues. No hex value was sampled from a photograph. The real places offer more than twenty strong side areas. The best-grounded are a sulfur mine in the Acid Pits (Dallol was actually mined for sulfur in 1949–54, and Kawah Ijen supplies the working method), the salt-block ruins of the Dallol potash camp, the CO2 "bird graveyard" lakes, a captive salt-cutters' camp with flooded pink quarry pits, caravan night camps laid out as the archaeology records them, lava tubes with skylights, grave fields of warrior tally plinths, a stripped light-railway terminus, and relict tufa dams and limestone caves in the gorge.

**How to read this guide.** A claim followed by a linked source is a **sourced fact**. **[Analogue: place]** marks a fact from somewhere else, used as a stand-in; it is never Danakil fact. **[Inference]** marks studio reasoning or general knowledge that the researchers could not verify for the Afar. Where the notes conflict, the conflict and the range are given. Every hex value is an estimate. "The vault" means George's ARPGStoryBible ([vault]); author decisions quoted from it are marked as such.

## The basin at a glance: one climate, one plant ladder, one caravan grammar

These region-wide facts apply to every zone below. The zone sections refer back here rather than repeat them.

### Heat that barely drops at night

The basin floor is among the hottest inhabited places on Earth. At Dallol (−92 m) in 1960–66 the **mean annual temperature was 34.6 °C, the mean daily high 41.2 °C and the mean daily low 28.2 °C**. Monthly mean highs and lows run from **36.1/24.6 °C in January to 46.7/30.4 °C in June** and 45.6/31.8 °C in July. The record is **49 °C** ([dclim]; [ghost]). The cracked salt lakebed reaches **50 °C** ([afp]). A traveller at Hamed Ela recorded 46 °C by day and about **36 °C overnight**, every stop feeling "like a thick, smothering blanket" ([hitd]). At Dallol it was nearly 40 °C by 10 a.m. ([ebp]), and winter daytime there can still exceed 45 °C ([eurek]). Night barely cools. The design consequence is real: caravans, salt-lifters and volcano hikers all move at night and in the dark before dawn (see the caravan grammar below).

Rain is close to absent on the floor, but the figures conflict. Dallol rain is "extremely rare" ([ghost]); one page says under 25 mm a year while its own table lists 84 mm on about 50 rain days ([danw]); the ecoregion gives 100–200 mm ([xeric]); Lake Abbe gets under 172 mm ([abbe]). A 2024 rangeland study at 659–775 m gives both 325–340 mm and 500–600 mm without reconciling them ([ecop]). Those sites are far wetter than a below-sea-level floor. Rain falls in two short seasons on the Afar rangelands, **June–September and March–April** ([fclim]), and plateau runoff in June–September floods the salt flats ([ttg]). Floods therefore arrive from the escarpment under a clear basin sky *[Inference]*. At the Dobi springs in central Afar, floods reach **4 m deep, and 15 camels were lost in 2023** ([fensham]). Winds are southerly from March to September and east–west from September to March ([wold]); southerly winds push the brine edge across the salt pan ([w14]). Dust devils form from intense surface heating and can reach about **1 km tall** ([usgs]).

### Light and air through a day

The notes support one day cycle that every zone can share, with zone-specific accents.

**Pre-dawn (about 3–5 a.m.).** Salt-lifters leave Hamed Ela as early as 3 a.m. and caravan camps break at about 3 a.m. ([swc]; [wold]). Blue starlight; the salt glows faintly grey-blue; lamp and fire light *[Inference]*.

**Sunrise.** The west-rim escarpment faces east into the basin, so its red face is lit first and glows red-orange *[Inference from the map's west-rim position]*. Low light rakes across every salt polygon ridge, lava rope and gypsum crystal; this is the best state for reading ground texture *[Inference]*. The Lake Asale surface mirrors the sky in the morning ([ebp]).

**Midday.** Above 40 °C by noon on the pan, glare off white salt and caravans vanishing into mirage ([swc]; [andy]). Heat haze blurs distant figures ([andy]). The salt "looks pink, grey or white depending on the time of day" ([ajcar]). Light is flat and near-shadowless, the sky bleached to white at the horizon *[Inference]*.

**Late afternoon.** The escarpment foot, including the Slums, falls into the wall's own shadow first *[Inference]*. Dust hangs and turns the light amber, then rust *[Inference]*.

**Dusk and night.** Caravans travel through the night ([swc]; [oac]). Very clear stars, no light pollution, and moonlight on salt that makes the ground glow *[Inference]*. A night breeze leaves sand in the hair ([andy]). Black basalt radiates the day's heat after sunset *[Inference]*.

Mirage, glare and heat shimmer are the region's signature effects. Inferior mirages (silver "water" bands that reflect the sky and float distant silhouettes) are documented only as "caravans disappear into a mirage" ([swc]); their exact look is *[Inference]*. Haboobs (brown dust walls) are plausible but undocumented for the Danakil.

### Plants by habitat, and the rule of where nothing grows

The basin floor belongs to the Djibouti xeric shrublands: dry shrub steppe and Acacia–Commiphora thorn scrub, with about **200 plant species, about 25 of them endemic** ([xeric]; [oneearth]). The single strongest planting rule across the act is **density falls from the escarpment foot to the pan, and green means either water or invasion** *[Inference built on the habitat facts below]*. Nothing grows on the salt pan, the brine margin, the acid dome, young lava or the volcano's caldera ([karum]; [eurek]; [swvw]).

### Wildlife: rare, water-bound, and mostly signs

The sourced wild fauna is small and specific. The **African wild ass** (*Equus africanus*) is critically endangered, with **as few as about 600** left in the Danakil of Ethiopia and Eritrea; distance to water is its key habitat predictor, distance from settlements matters in the dry season, and its optimal dry-season habitat is only about **207 km²** ([wildass]). (The ecoregion page places the last population on Eritrea's Buri Peninsula ([xeric]); the 2017–2020 survey studied Ethiopian animals, so prefer it.) Grazers include **Soemmerring's gazelle** (*Nanger soemmerringii*), **dorcas gazelle** (*Gazella dorcas*), **beira** (*Dorcatragus megalotis*), **gerenuk** (*Litocranius walleri*), reduced **beisa oryx** (*Oryx beisa*) and **Salt's dik-dik** (*Madoqua saltiana*); the **Berbera gerbil** is endemic; **Archer's lark** is the endemic bird; reptiles are relatively rich, including the endemic geckos *Hemidactylus arnoldi* and *Tropiocolotes somalicus* ([xeric]; [oneearth]). **Prosopis thickets draw hyenas, lions, wild cats and foxes** into villages ([gtp]), and **jackals and foxes** threaten caravan camps ([wold]). At the waters: **killifish (*Aphanius*), about 30 mm**, in warm Dobi spring ponds ([fensham]); the endemic cichlid *Danakilia franchettii* and pupfish *Aphaniops stiassnyae* in Lake Afrera ([afrera]); **flamingos** on Lake Abbe ([abbe]). Herded **camels, goats, sheep and cattle** are the commonest living things, with **donkeys and mules carrying water** ([fclim]; [andy]); one Afar herder is down from 50–100 cattle to 10 cows ([gtp]).

Vultures (lappet-faced, white-backed, Egyptian), ravens, ostriches, striped hyena, hyrax and baboons are all plausible and widely reported for the Afar and escarpment, but **none was cited** *[Inference]*. Track and dung sizes are general field knowledge *[Inference]*: camel pads are broad ovals about 15–20 cm, goat slots about 4–5 cm, hyena prints about 9–10 cm with four toes and claws; hyena dung is chalky white from bone.

### Afar building logic and the salt-caravan grammar that ties the zones together

Two material cultures run through the whole act: the Afar pastoral camp and the salt caravan. They give the act its continuity props.

**The Afar portable house (*daboyta*)** is a low hemispherical dome **3.6–4.4 m across and 2.0–2.8 m high** in the Lower Awash (measured examples 4.3 × 2.3 m and 4.4 × 2.25 m), lower in windy places (2–3 m across, 1.2–1.5 m high), and about half size in the rainy season ([chocian]). Its frame is about **50 flexible bentwood arches** of acacia, roots and elastic *Cordia* crossing at right angles, primary arches anchored every 25–35 cm and aligned with the wind, secondary arches 3–15 cm apart, joints lashed with sisal, bark or cloth, a forked centre pole of about 6 cm in larger houses ([chocian]). Women build it and cover it with **5–10 layers of doum-palm mats** (reed mats near Gewane), bought at Asayita or Elidar markets or woven from raw leaves and replaced every couple of years, often with **plastic sheeting at the bottom**; stones can be piled up to **1 m** around the base, and the entrance, **50–60 cm wide and 100–150 cm high**, faces the current wind behind a rolled mat ([chocian]). Three or four women take it down and one camel plus two donkeys carry it; often only the mats travel and the **frame is left standing** ([chocian]). Compounds (*daala*) hold up to 10 houses with separate pens; fences are 1.5 m (small) to 2–3 m (main) of acacia branches, dead trunks and *Dobera glabra* stalks reinforced with thorn bush, and a thorn "second skin" keeps livestock off the mats; volcanic stone is dry-stacked or clay-bonded into enclosure walls 1.5–2 m high, **corbelled stone domes shelter young animals at night**, and stones weight mats, ring hearths and line bread ovens ([chocian]). Settled Afar along trade routes build *ch'qa* houses (poles plastered with mud and straw, **corrugated-iron roofs**, stone foundations) ([chocian]; [tuwien]). Afar dress for the act's people is not sourced; one traveller saw domed tents "like upturned boats" covered in **brightly coloured cloth** ([stewart]).

**Graves** are everywhere. Clan graves (*qabri*) cluster like small stone villages; warrior monuments (*wadal* or *das*) are plinths about **0.9–1.8 m high topped with rows of stones counting the enemies the man killed**; a single large stone marks where someone died; **tens of thousands** of markers dot the land and people navigate by them ([salopek]). The ***jile*** dagger is 30 to over 50 cm with a curved, asymmetric leaf blade that cuts on its concave side, often forged from **truck leaf springs**, with a three-toothed metal pommel and a leather-over-wood sheath with a long knobbed tip ([jile]).

**The salt caravan** is the act's economic spine and the reason captives are here (vault: salt and sulfur cut by captives buy the city's food). Its real grammar, summarised once here and applied zone by zone:

- **Roles** ([swc]): the ***Focolo*** salt-lifters, poor Tigrayan migrants at the bottom of the social scale, who walk out as early as 3 a.m.; the ***Hadali Mera***, always Afar, who shape the blocks; the ***arho*** cameliers, who load each camel equally for taxing; and the ***Shumbahri*** chief, who gives each caravan its night departure slot. Woldekiros adds warehouse and shop owners, women who make water sacks, and tea and coffee sellers ([wold]).
- **Slabs.** Woldekiros, the strongest source, records three standard forms once used as currency: ***gole'o* 24 × 36 × 6 cm, *gerawayni* 23 × 30 × 6 cm, *ankarabe* 22 × 30 × 5 cm** ([wold]). Other sources name tiles by weight: ***ganfur* about 4 kg, *ghelao* about 8 kg** ([w14]; [indt]), or a standard **7 kg** block ([swc]; [afp]). Smaller ***amole*** bars are re-cut in the highlands; a museum *amoli* measures **198 × 45 × 58 mm, salt bound with natural fibre** ([boc]). *[Inference: at halite's ~2.16 g/cm³, the three Woldekiros slabs weigh about 11, 9 and 7 kg solid, so the 7 kg block matches the ankarabe or gerawayni.]*
- **Loads.** **20 blocks per camel to Mekelle, about 140 kg** ([swc]); **up to 30** blocks of about 4 kg ([danw]); **~200 kg** in one photo essay ([hamed]). Some loads make camels collapse ([ajcar]). **Conflict:** 120–200 kg; use 140 kg as the default.
- **Gear** ([wold]): pack pads ***gula*** and ***kuda*** of hide, wool or cloth stuffed with feathers or chaff; horizontal hooks ***anabu*** and vertical hooks ***koree*** and ***koyeta*** (koyeta only for salt); ropes of grass fibre and leather; goatskin water sacks (***sar***), jerry cans, flour sacks (***madaberia***). Camels' backs are scarred under thin pads ([hitd]). Camels are tied nose to tail behind a lead handler, loads in large baskets slung over the humps; donkeys shelter in the camels' shade ([andy]).
- **Volume and route.** About **2,000 camels and 1,000 donkeys a day** carry salt about **75 km to Berahile** ([w14]); about **5,000 blocks a day** arrive at a trading post **on a dry riverbed** at Berahile's edge ([afp]). The whole trail is about **160 km from over 2,000 m to about 200 m below sea level**; caravans total 10,000–20,000 t a year on about 70,000 animals ([wold]). The Afar *arho* goes only as far as Berahile, the edge of Afar country ([swc]). Trucks have been replacing caravans since about 2010 ([trojer]).
- **Night camps** ([wold]): every **~20 km**, arriving about 7 p.m. and leaving about 3 a.m.; up to **700–1,000 people and 3,000–6,000 animals**; a circular corral with fodder piled in the centre, a gear area where men sleep on their saddles, hearths upwind; tea hearths of **three stones 15–25 cm**; bread hearths **73–171 cm** across of small rounded stones that form a griddle; on the return, **salt stacked in two rows ringed by sleepers** against theft; sites near villages for food, water and protection from jackals, foxes and raids; deposits **30–40 cm deep** with obsidian and chert flakes, ~5th-century Aksumite brown-ware sherds, grain, dung and cooking stones; modern and ancient camps lie side by side.

**A flag for George, not a decision.** The *daboyta*, the *wadal* tally plinth, the *jile* and the salt-trade roles are a living people's culture, photographed and named in the sources. If the game's peoples are fictional, the studio could keep the **construction logic** (bentwood dome under layered mats, a piled-stone base, unmortared basalt, nose-to-tail strings, slabs on hooks) and change the **ornament** (mat patterns, cloth colours, pommel and sheath forms, grave-top arrangements, names) to avoid copying the Afar one-to-one. The same applies to Tigrayan *hidmo* houses and churches on the plateau.

## 1. Caravan Approach: varnished boulder fans under a red notch

### Overview

Zone 01 is the low south-west pass where the road into the basin leaves the escarpment foot and spreads across alluvial fans, and where the captive caravan is attacked (zone 01 is locked in the vault). It should feel stony, hot and wind-stripped: dark, glossy boulders, pale braided channels, rust-red cut banks, a few browsed thorn trees, and grave cairns on every rise, with the red wall and its pass notch behind. The road is the one living line through dead ground.

### Ground (walking scale)

The real fan zones of the Danakil show **large alluvial cones over red, weathered Tertiary bedrock, cut by a wadi**, photographed where the Asa Ali Ridge meets the Danakil plain ([gp1684]). Soils are thin and stony: **lithosols over lava and regosols over basin deposits** ([xeric]). The surface is open desert scattered with **wind-smoothed stones** ([salopek]); stony desert pavement, a sheet of rock fragments left when wind and water strip the fines, covers nearly half the world's deserts ([usgs]).

The walking-scale numbers are general desert values *[Inference]*. At the fan apex, angular to sub-angular basalt boulders of **0.5–3 m** sit in a cobble-gravel matrix, fining downslope to cobbles of 6–25 cm and gravel of 2–6 cm at the toe. Old fan surfaces are varnished pavement; active channels are pale and fresh, so the fan reads as **braided pale channels between darker "islands"**. Apex boulders flake in rinds and shed grus (crumbled rock) at their base. If the fan drains the escarpment's own strata, the gravel is a colour mix of red sandstone, cream limestone, black basalt and grey-green basement rock *[Inference from the escarpment sequence in zone 11]*. The road surface is hoof- and wheel-crushed gravel with pale fines showing.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

Desert varnish, the dark shiny coat on long-exposed rock, is manganese and iron oxides with clay; ventifacts are stones grooved and polished by blown grit ([usgs]). Both belong on the apex boulders. Wadi cut banks are **1–4 m** high, layered gravel and sand with boulders hanging out of the face and fallen blocks at the toe; fan slopes run about 2–10° near the apex and under 2° at the toe *[Inference]*. The escarpment's Edaga Arbi tillite holds glacial **boulders up to 6 m** across in a purple matrix ([geba]); a few huge, out-of-place erratics on the fan are a grounded landmark *[Inference: placement]*. The skyline is the escarpment wall stepping up in benches with red interbeds, and the notch of the pass.

### Water and minerals

Dry channels most of the year. Afar dig **scoop holes into seasonal riverbeds** for water ([fclim]); one wet-sand pit in the wadi bed, 0.5–2 m across with a dark ring and a trampled hoof field, is the zone's only water *[Inference: form]*. Flood debris lines (branches, dry grass, dung, plastic, Prosopis pods) catch on shrubs 0.3–1.5 m above the bed *[Inference]*; the sourced basis for floods is the Dobi record of floods 4 m deep ([fensham]) and the highland rains above.

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

Umbrella thorn along channel edges and at the fan toes, flat-bottomed from browsing (goats reach about 1.8–2.5 m on hind legs, camels about 3 m *[Inference]*); Sodom apple on the trampled road verge, since it dominates grazed land and animals avoid its toxic sap ([ecop]); hook thorn and *Rhigozum* on any lava knoll ([xeric]); dead grey snags and lopped limbs where wood was cut for hut arches *[Inference]*. Caravans rest in the shade of large acacias or rock shelters ([wold]), so one big shade tree by the road is grounded. Spacing: trees 10–50 m apart along the wadi, 50–200 m on open fan *[Inference]*. Nothing grows on the varnished pavement islands.

### Wildlife and signs

Jackals and foxes threaten caravan camps ([wold]); Prosopis brings hyenas near people ([gtp]). Vultures on snags marking the ambush, raven pairs, hyena tracks after dark, agama lizards on warm boulders and scorpions under turned stones are all *[Inference]*.

### Human traces and material culture

The road, and graves on every rise: *qabri* clusters, *wadal* plinths with tally stones, single death-marker stones ([salopek]); *[Inference: qabri heaps about 1.5–2.5 m long and 0.3–0.8 m high]*. The caravan itself follows the real grammar (nose-to-tail strings, lead handler, baskets slung over humps ([andy]); pads, hooks, grass-fibre rope, goatskin *sar* and jerry cans ([wold])). Guards protecting camels from bandits are real ([danw]), which grounds armed escorts on the attacked caravan. Neck yokes, chains, rope coffles and cage carts are fiction *[Inference]*.

### Air, weather and light

Morning haze off the escarpment, dust kicked up by the caravan, dust devils on the fans *[Inference]*; heat haze and glare ([andy]). The pass is in shadow late in the day; dawn light comes through the notch.

## 2. The Slums: a green ribbon at the foot of the falls

### Overview

The Slums are the poor quarter of the slaver city, about 9,000 people (placeholder) on a terrace at the escarpment foot, with the upper city on a shelf directly above, a short switchback apart, and the falls between (vault, 2026-11-06). The city takes the thin, shrinking stream upstream of the Slums (vault, 2026-11-13). The zone should read as the only living green in the act's first half: a hard-edged ribbon of moss, reed and palm along one channel, then instantly bare black rock; a dense mixed fabric of mud, stone and mat houses; and great grey-white stacks of salt slabs in a caravan yard. It is alive, but the dry end of the palm line already shows it failing.

### Ground (walking scale)

*[Inference throughout, built on the sourced uses below.]* Packed, pale-tan silt paths rutted by hooves; dark wet mud pugged by hooves along the channel (prints 8–15 cm); black volcanic gravel and cobbles of 5–30 cm on the terrace; trampled dung in the animal yards; a white salt skin and spilled salt chips (1–10 cm) around the depot; drifts of palm leaflets and fibre under the palms. Organic rubbish dries and mummifies rather than rotting; plastic bleaches and shreds; dung dries into flat cakes collected for fuel.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

A layered scarp face rises behind; the cascade steps down ledges of 2–10 m into a plunge pool of 5–15 m *[Inference]*. Seeps emerge in horizontal lines where permeable limestone or sandstone sits on shale, tillite or basement, so green forms bands along one bench *[Inference]*. Tufa is defensible: calcium-rich water from the Antalo limestone deposits travertine at **spring outlets, waterfalls and rapids around Mekelle**, the Alula Aba Nega spring has **active stalactites and stalagmites**, and the western Mekelle Outlier holds **many relict tufa dams** ([geba]; [dramis]). Afar hydrothermal water also precipitates carbonate (Lake Abbe's chimneys, zone 5). So a tufa apron of cream-to-honey lobes and curtains is grounded **if** the falls stream drains limestone *[Inference; a cartography check]*. Skyline: the wall hundreds of metres high behind, the upper city's walls on its shelf, and the white pan glaring to the east.

### Water and minerals

Real Afar springs are **fault springs at graben flanks and escarpment feet, and many are hot**. At Teo, more than **100 springs rise within about 10 ha** on the downthrown side of a fault; the highest vents boil and people draw cooler water (**~40 °C**) from hand-dug holes. At Alalo-bad about 30 vents run **58–100 °C**, pH 7.3–9.3, with an outflow wetland of ~981 ha; Dobi's clusters run 36–57 °C under ridges ~500 m high, with the water table under 2 m ([fensham]). Uses are drinking, bathing, watering stock, **steam and mud cures, cooking in hot water, gathering salt for cooking, mud smeared on milk animals, and palm thatch soaked in the spring** ([fensham]). *[Inference:]* the Slums' stream is a cool or warm cascade topped up by springs at the fault foot, with hot vents kept off to one side so the hub does not read as a hazard; hand-cut channels 30–80 cm wide, stone- or mud-lined, split to basins at the palms; the stream sinks into the boulder fan within a few hundred metres in the hot season (a losing stream), so the Slums crowd the pool and the last surface flow; white efflorescence on channel walls; orange iron stain where water leaves sandstone.

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

The splash zone bands from wet to dry *[Inference/Analogue]*: black-green algal film and dark wet stone directly behind the falls; bright moss cushions and **maidenhair fern** (*Adiantum capillus-veneris*, fronds 10–30 cm) in narrow horizontal bands where water leaks from the rock (**[Analogue: Texas seep cliffs]** [seep]); the closest rift analogue for a spring-fed desert oasis is Ein Gedi on the Dead Sea Rift, with two perennial streams and a flora mixing Sodom apple, acacia and jujube (**[Analogue: Ein Gedi]** [eingedi]); sedge and reed on the ledges and pool rim; then bare dry rock 1–3 m beyond the spray line. On the pool rim and outflow, use the real Afar spring plants: ***Cyperus laevigatus*** at the spring heads, **cattail *Typha latifolia***, **reeds *Phragmites australis* and *P. karka***, **Bermuda grass *Cynodon dactylon***, and **doum palm** ([fensham]). The planted line is **date palm** (author decision, provisional), using the FAO form ([fao]): a single trunk to 20 m and about 1–1.1 m round, 100–125 green fronds of 3–6 m, base spines up to 24 cm, about 12 fruit bunches a year, 3–25 offshoots at the base. *[Inference:]* the trunk is clad in a diamond lattice of cut frond bases standing 10–20 cm proud; fruit hangs on long orange-yellow stalks below the crown, turning green, then yellow or red, then brown; tended palms are pruned clean, neglected ones wear a skirt of dead fronds. Doum, the native Afar palm, forks into Ys and stands at the wild edges ([doum]). The condition gradient *[Inference]*: healthy crowned palms within 5–10 m of the live channel; then stressed palms with a brown skirt of dead fronds and a thin crown; then a few **beheaded poles** 5–15 m tall at the dry end, the classic dead-oasis silhouette ([Analogue: Morocco, curled dead fronds on the ground at Tighmert] [ngmor]). **Prosopis** thickets at the margins, since it has displaced doum and pasture at the Dobi springs and is cut there for charcoal ([fensham]). A sycomore fig (*Ficus sycomorus*), the classic northern Ethiopian spring tree, is a plausible shade landmark *[Inference]*. Fence wood is acacia and *Dobera glabra* ([chocian]). **Nothing grows beyond the last channel: the edge is a straight line**.

### Wildlife and signs

Killifish about **30 mm** live in warm Afar spring ponds ([fensham]), so tiny fish dart in the pool. Goats, donkeys and camels drink at the channel; young and lactating animals stay inside compounds and the rest go in pens at night ([chocian]). Weaver nests in palms, ravens and kites over the rubbish, frogs calling at night, flies on dung *[Inference]*.

### Human traces and material culture

A mixed fabric *[Inference built on sourced types]*: a core of **ch'qa** (mud on pole frames, corrugated-iron roofs, stone foundations) and volcanic-stone rooms, since permanent Afar houses along trade routes use clay-plastered wood frames and earth is the region's only plentiful building material ([chocian]; [tuwien]); an edge of domed mat **daboyta** for new arrivals and nomads; thorn and dead-wood fences everywhere; corbelled stone kid pens; stone hearths and bread ovens; acacia-frame beds pulled outside (men sleep outside in the hot season) ([chocian]). The caravan yard copies **Berahile's depot on a dry riverbed**, where about 5,000 blocks a day were stacked for onward transport ([afp]): big stacks of slabs (zone 7 standards), camel-loading ropes, a tally table. The market sells **amole bars** bound in fibre ([boc]). Real Hamed Ela, the salt village, is a ramshackle settlement of huts of about 4,000 people with little water and "sometimes bad food", miners paid about 500 birr a day ([afp]; [hamed]); its huts are acacia branches, with open cooking fires on hauled wood and water by truck ([hitd]). Salt-block walls (grey-white, rounded, dissolving where wetted) are grounded by Dallol's salt-block buildings ([ghost]) and belong away from the spray. Frond-rib shade roofs, palm-trunk posts, rolled doum mats, smoked grass milk bowls ([chocian]), and thatch soaking in the channel ([fensham]).

### Air, weather and light

Caravans leaving at dawn in assigned order is sourced ([swc]). Within 5–15 m of the plunge, a drifting spray haze; on cooler mornings low sun through the spray throws a small rainbow; elsewhere heat shimmer over the terrace and amber dust at dusk; the terrace falls into the wall's shadow first in the afternoon *[Inference]*.

## 3. Ruined Outpost and the gravel flats: a fading road across varnished pavement

### Overview

Mid-basin, the old caravan road crosses open stone pavement and fades out (vault, 2026-11-05: the road through the Ruined Outpost fades mid-basin). This is the act's emptiest, flattest, most shimmering ground: a tight dark mosaic of pebbles, isolated thorn trees, grave clusters, abandoned hut skeletons, and one ruin of an older order, re-used by herders. It should feel exposed and timeless, the place where heat haze is strongest.

### Ground (walking scale)

*[Inference: general desert geomorphology, unverified for the Afar.]* A tightly fitted mosaic of **1–6 cm pebbles** over a pale, vesicular silt layer 2–10 cm thick; turn a stone and its underside is lighter and reddish, while the top carries brown-to-black varnish (sourced composition: [usgs]). Feet, hooves and wheels break the varnish and expose paler fines, so **an old caravan road stays readable for decades as a pale, slightly sunken band 3–8 m wide**, fading where sheetwash or sand has smoothed it, with stones cleared into lines along its edges in places. Sand sheets are only 1–30 cm deep, lie in the lee of stones and shrubs, and carry ripples about 5–15 cm apart and 0.5–1 cm high; ripples run across the wind with coarse grains on their crests ([usgs] for the mechanics). Small sand shadows trail every shrub and stone.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

Dead flat. The only landmarks are the outpost, isolated acacias, grave clusters, a far blue escarpment, and the white pan shimmering on one horizon *[Inference]*.

### Water and minerals

None, apart from mirage bands. A salt efflorescence band rises on any wall near the pan *[Inference]*.

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

Isolated umbrella thorn or desert date **50–200 m apart** (species sourced for sandy plains [xeric]; spacing [Inference]), grey dead tussocks of *Aristida* ([ecop]), possibly *Panicum turgidum* tussocks 0.5–1 m (unconfirmed). Wood cut for hut arches and corrals leaves stumps and lopped limbs near old camps *[Inference]*. The pavement itself is bare.

### Wildlife and signs

Wild ass and gazelle at distance (sourced species, [wildass]; [xeric]); bleached livestock bones and skulls along the road, dung piles, ostrich pairs and scrapes (1–3 m, eggs ~15 cm) *[Inference; ostrich unconfirmed for the floor]*.

### Human traces and material culture

**The Dallol ghost town is the best real model of an Afar-basin ruin.** It was a potash and salt camp of 1917 to the 1960s at about 130 m below sea level; as of 2004 some structures still stood, **all built of salt blocks**; a **600 mm Decauville light railway** from Mersa Fatma in Eritrea opened in April 1918 and ended about **28 km short of Dallol**, and the British administration dismantled it after WWII and removed nearly every trace; an Italian company mined about **25,000 t of sylvite in 1925–29**; the US Parsons company drilled about **10,000 holes at 65 locations by 1965** ([ghost]). (Treat that page's caption calling it "an abandoned Italian sulphur mine" as unreliable; see zone 6 for the real sulfur history.) A former military camp at Logya is now a school ([chocian]). Afar frames are often left standing when a camp moves ([chocian]), giving ready "ghost hut" lattices; stone corrals, graves and the road complete the scene ([salopek]).

*[Inference] Outpost build recipe:* basalt rubble walls 50–70 cm thick in mud mortar, the mortar eroded so stones stand proud; wall tops collapsed into talus cones at the foot; a wind-scoured windward base and lee sand ramps 0.5–1.5 m high; a white salt band 20–80 cm above the ground with sugary "salt rot"; corrugated iron rusted through or peeled back to bent purlins; rusted steel lintels and, as a Decauville echo, rail lengths re-used as lintels and posts (fitting the sourced stripping of the line); doors and timber gone, since timber is scarce; the shell re-used as a goat pen with dung floors, a thorn-brush plug in the doorway and hearth soot on inner walls; graves near the walls.

### Air, weather and light

The act's strongest heat shimmer, dust devils (up to ~1 km, [usgs]) and a white noon; mirage bands under the outpost silhouette *[Inference]*.

## 4. Mineral Washes: rust and ochre channels to a saline pool

### Overview

A corridor of wadi channels runs between terrace benches toward a saline mineral pool. The washes should read as a muted, washed-out echo of Dallol's colours carried downslope (rust, ochre, pale yellow, white crust, black manganese), so the full palette stays saved for the Acid Pits *[Inference, an art-direction choice]*. The pool at the end is the one place mid-basin where water stands, and everything that lives comes to it.

### Ground (walking scale)

### Water and minerals

The salinity gradient is sourced: fan water rises from **about 760 ppm to over 23,400 ppm toward the salt flat** ([w14]), and salt-flat pore brines at air temperature hold **over 260 g/L** of dissolved ions ([wd]). An eyewitness saw a **"green-tinted open-air pond"** amid blinding white salt at Lake Asale at noon ([ebp]). ### Air, weather and light

## 5. Dead Spring: a failed oasis in rings of salt

### Overview

The Dead Spring is the act's deliberately dreary place, to the south: on the before map a living freshwater oasis, now a few small stagnant mineral pools (vault, 2026-11-05). It should feel still, heavy and hot, a basin of cracked mud and white salt rings, grey dead reeds, beheaded and fallen palms, sand-choked channels and a dead well, where the only thriving green is thorny invasive mesquite. Grit is the dominant mode here by design. **No source describes a dying spring inside the Danakil**, so the zone is assembled from real Afar decline (Lake Abbe, Prosopis at springs, Lake Afrera) and labelled analogues (Morocco's oases, the Dead Sea shore).

### Ground (walking scale)

*[Inference, consistent with the evaporite and spring sources.]* Polygonal cracked mud in plates 10–40 cm across with curled lips 1–3 cm thick and white salt along the crack lips, the outer cracks filling with blown sand; a white salt crust 1–5 cm thick, crunchy and blistered; greasy grey-black anoxic mud at pool edges; sand drifts filling old channels; black ash patches where reed beds burned.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

A low, wide basin tens to a few hundred metres across; old shoreline benches stacked with salt rings marking each lower water level; low ridges of dark rock around it; a hazy skyline with no green *[Inference]*. Optional **collapse sinkholes** **[Analogue: Dead Sea]**: as the Dead Sea falls more than 1 m a year (it has lost about a third of its surface since the 1960s), fresh water dissolves buried salt and the ground collapses into **thousands of sinkholes, some more than 10 m deep**; near Ein Gedi about 3 km of shore between the old spa and the water is "dotted with holes and crevices" ([deadsea]). *[Inference: pits 1–10 m+ across with sharp rims and salt-crusted walls.]*

### Water and minerals

Real Afar decline is documented at **Lake Abbe**: by 1984 it had shrunk to about two-thirds of its 1940 area and about **11,500 ha of new salt flats** formed to its south-west, from drought and irrigation withdrawals on the Awash; salt flats up to **10 km wide** line its south-west and south shores; it is an "amplifier lake" whose level swings sharply with small climate changes ([abbe]). **Lake Afrera**, hypersaline and fed by hot springs on its shores, stood about **50 m higher in the African Humid Period**; its salt became inedible after **acid contamination from the 2011 Nabro eruption**; brine was once pumped into artificial evaporation ponds ([afrera]); a freshwater hot spring beside it is used as a "natural jacuzzi" ([brafrera]). *[Inference]* The pools: three to eight, 0.5–5 m across and 5–50 cm deep, clear but tinted; green to olive-brown algal scum at the edges, orange-rust iron floc on the bottom where iron-bearing water seeps, grey-black anoxic mud with a sulphur smell in the deeper ones; a white salt rind on every rim, often a stack of rings (a "bathtub ring" record of falling levels); one faintly warm seep with sulphur-yellow staining; evaporite crystals growing on twigs and bones.

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

Prosopis (Afar *woyane*) has spread at Afar springs since about 2009 at Alalo-bad and about 20 years ago at Dobi, covering about **1,235 ha at Dobi and 101 ha at Alalo-bad**, displacing doum palm and pasture ([fensham]). Make it **the only saturated green**, thriving at the margin. *Cyperus laevigatus* survives right at springs ([fensham]), so a few salt-tolerant tufts can persist at the last seep. Everything else is dead *[Inference built on sourced forms]*: grey-straw cattail and reed, standing or lodged flat, black at the base, stubble burnt in patches; dead doum and date palms as beheaded poles 5–15 m, fallen trunks and frond skirts half buried in sand (**[Analogue: Morocco]**: drought-curled fronds on the ground at Tighmert, palms "overrun by sand" at Tanoueest, about 10,000 palms burning in Moroccan oases each year ([ngmor])); grey skeletal acacia; tamarisk-like bushes with salt-crusted twigs (tamarisk unconfirmed for Afar springs). Nothing lives on the white crust.

### Wildlife and signs

Bleached, sun-split camel and goat bones with mummified hide (camel ribs roughly 60–80 cm, skull roughly 50–60 cm); vultures or ravens; flies on bones and wet mud; mosquito larvae in stagnant pools and adults rising at dusk; perhaps one flamingo at a brine pool, since flamingos use Afar saline lakes ([abbe]) *[the rest Inference]*.

### Human traces and material culture

*[Inference unless cited.]* A stone-ringed well with a fallen frame and frayed rope; sand-filled channel lines (bank humps 30–60 cm high, 0.5–1 m apart); rectangular dried irrigation basins; collapsed mat domes (bare acacia ribs with shredded mats); crumbling ch'qa and stone walls; empty thorn pens; a cold hearth ring; a cracked clay pot or broken grass bowl; a **Prosopis charcoal mound** or kiln scar (charcoal-making is sourced, [fensham]). **[Analogue: Morocco]** gives the logic of abandonment: one well deepened from **7 m in 1996 to 16 m**; residents digging **more than 40 m** against 7–10 m in the 1980s; brackish pumped water raising soil salinity; sand filling neglected canals in a few windy days; the oasis edge losing more than ~90 m a year to sand; the population down a fifth in 20 years; at Bounou, 200 families reduced to five, rammed-earth walls crumbling and ground floors given over to sand ([ngmor]; [anmor]). **[Analogue: Dead Sea]**: a ghost-town tourist complex with gutted pavement, overturned lampposts and an **abandoned date plantation** on a whitened, salt-crusted shore ([deadsea]).

### Air, weather and light

Still, hot, heavy air; heat shimmer on the crust; glare; a faint sulphur-tinted haze over the pools; flat noon light by design; dusk gives only a dull bruise-violet, no golden beauty *[Inference]*.

## 6. Acid Pits: the act's loudest colours on a low salt dome

### Overview

The acid field sits on a low dome at the salt pan's southern edge (approved fix, vault 2026-11-06), with amber sulfur-crust ground so acid-green pools stand out (vault, 2026-11-05); the author wants it to stand out "even beyond what real Dallol would justify". Captives cut sulfur here for the slaver city. Real Dallol gives a precise kit: a white salt base, live pools of acid green, lime and sulfur yellow whose rims age through gold and orange to rust and brown, black iron-rich salt domes, mushroom-shaped salt hoodoos in canyons on one flank, and lethal lakes ringed with pickled birds. Colour is a clock here, and hazards can be read by it.

### Ground (walking scale)

The real dome is white salt that turns white, yellow and brown in its active deposits ([f19]). At the Yellow Lake Fissure: **polygonal salt plates several cm thick, some tilted into tepees**, with **5–10 cm ridges** along plate edges; **red, soft, sticky salt-mud** patches; red salt crust standing up to **about 50 cm** above the flat; **red circular sulfur patches 1–2 m across at regular spacing**; salt-encrusted bubbles up to a few cm; holes with haloes of halite crystals ([f23]). The crust over acid water is described as **"eggshell-thin"** ([xweb], a popular source), and at Gaet'ale the crust **may not bear a person's weight** ([av15]). The map's amber sulfur crust is a game ground built on the native sulfur that occurs as yellow deposits around degassing vents ([f19]). *[Inference]* Footing sounds: the crunch and crack of thin crust; the sticky suck of red salt-mud.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

**Dome.** A complex structure **40 m high** ([f19]) or **about 60 m** ([dhydro]; [av15]) above a salt plain about 120–125 m below sea level, about **3 × 1.5 km**, with a central crater about **1 km across and 15 m deep**; it probably formed in the last **~6,000 years** ([f19]). **Conflict:** 40 vs 60 m, probably different bases.

**Walking-scale forms.** Salt cones from centimetres to decimetres; **pillars and chimneys up to about 2 m** ([f19]); temporary mini-geysers that build cones; "water-lily" and flower-like crystals, **egg-shaped crusts and pearl-like spheres** ([dhydro]); pillar shapes described as "artichoke", "mushroom" and "barnacle" ([xweb]); terraced acid pools in green, orange and yellow ([f19]). **"Black domes"** are dense dark halite rich in hematite with **hexagonal joints** ([f19]). A **"Skating Rink"** is a circular structure about **300 m across** ([f19]). *[Inference sizes:]* micro-cones 5–30 cm; terrace pools 0.3–3 m with rims 1–10 cm; spheres 1–5 cm; anything above 2 m is stylisation.

**Flanks.** The western dome is cut into **deep salt canyons, caves and sinkholes**; less-soluble **anhydrite caps protect the halite** below, leaving isolated buttes, pinnacles and **demoiselles** (hoodoos), with collapsed anhydrite-gypsum breccia near dark sinkholes ([f19]); the south-west slopes have water-eroded canyons, pillars and blocks ([dhydro]). The **Black Mountain**, about 2 km south-west of the crater, carries big salt boulders thrown out of active fissures and frequent massive **bischofite (MgCl₂) flows**; the Round Mountain lies about 3–3.5 km west ([f19]). *[Inference]* Hoodoos 2–10 m with cream-to-grey anhydrite caps over banded pinkish-white to buff halite waists; canyon walls striped white, buff and red-brown.

**Skyline.** A low, pale, lumpy dome only 40–60 m above the flat white pan, a **vapour column** over it as the landmark (one stood over the dome in January 2019, [f19]), the dark Black Mountain as counterweight, the red-ochre escarpment far beyond *[composition Inference]*.

### Water and minerals

**Chemistry.** Brines at **pH at or below 0**, salt above 30%, temperatures **up to about 110 °C**; spouters at about **108 °C**; one degassing hot spot at about 200 °C ([f19]); more than **26 g/L iron**, about ten times saltier than seawater, anoxic ([dhydro]); in the 1920s, springs near Black Mountain and Mount Dallol reached about 130 °C ([f23]). The **yellow-green colour comes from reduced iron** ([eurek]).

**Colour as a clock.** Fresh deposits are white, then green, lime, yellow, gold, orange, red, purple and ochre; old inactive springs turn **dark brown**; this is **inorganic iron oxidation, not microbes** (unlike Yellowstone); minerals include halite, jarosite, hematite, akaganeite, gypsum, anhydrite, sylvite and carnallite ([dhydro]). **Springs switch on and off, and new ones appear, often within days** ([dhydro]); pond levels change by centimetres in a few days; the active area was **0.087 km² (2012), 0.038 km² (January 2016) and 0.144 km² (January 2017)** ([f19]). *[Inference]* Use it for telegraphing: white or acid green means live and dangerous now; yellow and gold, days old; orange, rust and red, dying; brown and purple-ochre, dead crust. The greener the pool, the deadlier.

**Gas, steam and sound.** Main gases **CO₂, H₂S, N₂ and SO₂** ([dhydro]); fumaroles and steaming ground ([f19]); **"bubbling sounds can be heard from the chimneys"** at the Yellow Lake Fissure ([f23]); phreatic (steam) explosions in **1926** forming a ~30 m crater ([dhydro]) or **1929** ([av15]), and an ash cloud in January 2011 ([av15]). *[Inference]* Hiss of spouters, popping brine, a rotten-egg and struck-match smell, and an oily, high-gloss sheen on pools (a pool at the fissure has an oil-film look from dissolved salts, [f23]).

### Wildlife and signs

### Human traces and material culture

**Real sulfur mining at Dallol.** Native sulfur occurs in **small open pits** ([f19]). Sulfur was mined near the Dallol mining village from **1949 to 1954** by the Dallol Potash, Magnesium and Sulfur Mines company, then by the Ralph M. Parsons Co. from **1954 to 1968** ([f19]). **Conflict:** Wikipedia describes Parsons's 1960s work as surveys and drilling, not production ([ghost]). The Dallol method is not recorded.

**The potash camp.** Many Italian-era camp buildings were **salt blocks; left to decay, the walls now lean outward before collapsing** ([swsalt]); full history in zone 3 ([ghost]).

**The working method [Analogue: Kawah Ijen, Java].** Ijen is a high, wet, cool crater, so only its mining practice transfers, not its landscape. Since 1968, **ceramic pipes** set on a vent condense the gas into liquid sulfur that drips onto hard mats; miners spray water on the pipes ([ijen]; [ajijen]). Molten sulfur **oozes out deep red** and **turns bright mustard-yellow** as it cools ([ijen]; [hc]); miners break the slabs with **metal bars** and heap chunks into **a pair of baskets on a shoulder pole**, **68–91 kg** ([ngijen]), **75–90 kg** ([ijen]) or about 100 kg a day ([ajijen]). The climb is about **300 m to the rim at 45–60°**, then **3 km down to the weigh point**, usually two trips a day; about 200–300 miners move about **14 t a day** ([ijen]; [ngijen]). Most wear **wet scarves or rags**, few have gas masks; the gas makes eyes water, burns throats and can dissolve teeth; deformed spines and bent legs are common ([ngijen]); miners wear cheap rubber boots or flip-flops, often bare chests, and carry a **reddish-brown callus on the shoulder** where the load rests ([hc]). At the weigh station loads go on a scale at about **7 US cents a kilo**, then by truck to a factory where the sulfur is **melted and spread into sheets** ([hc]). A **goat-head offering** is buried in the pipes each mid-December, a ritual begun in **1976 after four miners died**, and miners pray in a cabin near the crater ([ajijen]). At night burning sulfur gas makes **blue flames up to 5 m high**, at up to 600 °C ([ijen]).

## 7. Salt Flats and brine margin: a white honeycomb, mirror sheets and the cuttings

### Overview

The salt pan and its brine pools are the lowest ground in the basin (approved fix, vault 2026-11-06). The real Asale pan is about **40 km long and 10 km wide**, in the deepest sump of the northern Danakil, about **5 km from the foot of the western escarpment** ([w14]); its water surface stands at about **−115 m** ([w14]), −116 m ([nasaeo]) or −120 m ([karumw]). The zone has three faces: the open pan (a blinding white polygonal honeycomb where any vertical form floats in mirage); the brine margin (damp grey crust over black mud, mirror sheets and jewel-like pools); and the west-arm salt cuttings and the salt track, where captives cut slabs and the caravans form. The author wants **bright white salt** rather than realistic grey.

### Ground (walking scale)

**Real crust.** A bedded halite crust, **"mostly megapolygonal"** and "polygonally ridged", built of stacked upward-pointing halite chevrons in layers **centimetres thick separated by mm-to-cm mud layers** ([w14]). Visitors see snow-white flats broken by "polygonal crazing" ([hitd]), a pattern of "squares" rather than hexagons ([rtd]), something "more like an Arctic desert than a saline lake" ([brsalt]). Deeper, the Upper Rock Salt is coarse massive halite in **brown-reddish, yellow and grey-to-colourless** varieties, which is what a deep cut face would show ([w14]).

**Polygon numbers [Analogue: Owens Lake, Badwater, Sua Pan].** Polygons about **1–2 m** across (measured wavelengths 0.53 ± 0.20 m to 3.02 ± 1.40 m); narrow ridges covering about **24% of the surface**; relief **0–20 cm, peaks about 25 cm**; crusts under 1 cm to about 30 cm thick; ridges grow about twice as fast as centres (1.4–1.8 vs 0.6–0.9 mm a month at Owens Lake), and a crust takes about **six months** to develop ([lasser]). No Asale-specific polygon size was found.

**Renewal (real).** Frequent southerly winds push brine sheets **kilometres across the pan in a few hours**, leaving new halite; rare sheetfloods off the wadis lay **silt-mud layers** on the pan ([w14]); in the June–September rains plateau runoff floods the flats and **a new crust forms each year**, a "virtually virgin" surface for the cutters ([ttg]); cut areas leave **empty white spaces** ([indt]).

*[Inference] Ground tiers:* fresh flat crust (near-smooth, crisp white, faint 1–2 m polygon lines 1–3 cm high); mature polygon crust (1–2 m cells, ridges 5–20 cm, doubled or upturned plates at ridges); old broken crust (tilted, buckled plates with dust in the hollows); a second, larger crack network of tens of metres implied by "megapolygonal"; compacted grey track crust; brown silt drapes where sheetfloods reached; mudcracks of 10–40 cm cells where fan silt meets the pan edge. *Colour states:* dry crust chalk-white with a faint blue sky cast; ridge tops whitest; hollows grey-beige with dust; wet sheets near-black at grazing angles and mirrors at low sun; damp crust translucent grey.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

Wadi-fed **alluvial fans (bajada)** fringe the pan, most active on the west; the Musley fan has cut into the gypsum pavement and feeds water and sediment straight onto the flat ([w14]). **East and north-east, an extensive gypsum plain** marks a higher, less saline lake stage, and gypsum rim deposits stand above pan level along the west ([w14]). Brine springs feed dry mudflats; hydrothermal springs and **collapse dolines** lie near the Dallol mound ([w14]). North, rain has cut huge **salt mountains into steep valleys with sharp spines** ([oac]); grey-brown salt towers rise in jagged shapes near Dallol ([hitd]). The **Asale Red Rocks** rise abruptly from the salt sheet with a small water hole beside them ([hitd]). South-west of Lake Karum stands **Gada Ale, a 287 m basaltic stratovolcano** with gentle slopes, its youngest flows darkest ([esa]); a **2 km salt dome south-west of it has pushed ancient lava up to 100 m high** ([nasaeo]). The depression lies between the Danakil Alps to the east and the Ethiopian Plateau to the west ([nasaeo]).

*[Inference]* At 1–100 m: fan-toe gravel lobes with braided channels 0.5–3 m wide; gypsum pavement steps 0.2–1 m (old shore benches); lone salt spines 2–15 m, fluted by rain; red rock knobs standing out of white salt. Skyline from the west-arm cuttings: the layered escarpment close to the west; the pale gypsum plain east; salt spines and the dome north; dark basalt cones south. Because the pan is perfectly flat, use a few strong silhouettes floating in mirage rather than clutter.

### Water and minerals

**Lake Karum** is "elongated, shallow, ephemeral, and highly saline", flooding the Asale plain after rain and fed by groundwater that dissolves older salt; sampled sites were **emergent crust, 5–10 cm of water, and 40–50 cm at the deepest**; water **28–30 °C**, pH 6.7–8.0; ions (mg/kg) **Na 92,500–137,000, Ca 20,100–25,700**, K 4,110–6,050, Mg 3,880–4,500, so the brine is unusually calcium-rich; microbes are **60–88% haloarchaea**, with *Salinibacter* and halophilic cyanobacteria; the red-orange brine alga *Dunaliella* was **not** detected; the paper gives no water colour ([karum]). Eyewitnesses describe the pan becoming "submerged in **clear water**" so gradually that the shore is hard to find ([brsalt]), a thin film that **mirrors the sky** ([rtd]), and a brine-and-mud pool beneath the crust ([rtd]). A small water hole by the Red Rocks dries on skin within seconds, leaving salt ([hitd]). **Lake Afrera** looks shimmering green, with salt mounds on its shores and **rectangular shoreline evaporation basins** ([brsalt]); modern lake-fed evaporation ponds also work near Asale, their salt better but dearer than mined blocks ([afp]). In false-colour satellite views Karum looks black and Dallol pink, both display artefacts ([esa]).

*[Inference] Pink, kept defensible:* haloarchaea and *Salinibacter* carry red pigments (bacterioruberin), and still, concentrated brine in saltworks crystallisers turns pink to red. So show **pink only in small, shallow, isolated evaporating pools at the margin and in old quarry pits**, keep the main brine sheet clear and glassy, grading to milky turquoise over white salt. *Rims:* concentric bathtub rings of white halite a few cm apart; floating halite rafts and skins that sink as hopper crystals; cauliflower efflorescence on wet edges. The calcium-chloride brine is hygroscopic, so margins stay dark and damp-looking even in heat, with sticky grey-black mud under a thin crust; a rotten-egg stink only where black mud is broken.

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

**Nothing grows on the pan or at the brine**: no source mentions life there beyond brine microbes ([karum]). The first plants appear on fan toes and wadi channels where fresh water seeps in ([w14] for the low-salinity fan water; species [Inference]): sparse umbrella thorn (camps seek acacia shade, [wold]), dusty toothbrush tree, seablite and tamarisk (unverified), and mesquite. Hamed Ela's huts are of acacia branches ([hitd]). Keep plants single and tens of metres apart, dust-coated and half-dead *[Inference]*.

### Wildlife and signs

None live on the pan. Camels chew salt at the lake centre to recover energy ([swc]); hundreds of camels rest in the sun while donkeys shelter in their shade ([andy]); Hamed Ela keeps goats and sheep, drinking their milk and blood ([hitd]); jackals and foxes threaten the camps ([wold]). Dung, hoof prints, bones and carcasses along the track; flies; ravens and vultures over carcasses *[Inference]*.

### Human traces and material culture

**Cutting.** Hundreds of men cut salt each morning with **hand axes** ([afp]); they chop long grooves in the crust, **jam wooden sticks into the axe grooves as levers** and lift each large layered slab ([indt]; [w14]); homemade tools **strip the mud layer** off and square the slabs ([indt]); axes, wedges and poles are listed elsewhere ([ecot], uncited). A father and son work as one crew ([indt]). Men work with axes, long wooden levers and **adzes**, cut blocks to size by eye, strap them together and pack them into baskets over the humps ([andy]). Work runs every day except Friday ([hitd]; [ttg]). Pay: about **1 birr a tile, up to 200 tiles a day**, after a 2-hour walk each way ([indt]; [fairp]), or **500 birr (about $22) a day** in 2017 ([afp]). Merchants pay a salt tax before leaving ([fairp]). Slab standards, roles, loads, gear and camps are in the region section. **Conflict:** FairPlanet's 750 registered miners and 1.3 million t a year clash with Woldekiros's 10,000–20,000 t ([fairp]; [wold]); prefer Woldekiros.

**Rhythm.** Before dawn the *Focolo* walk out; at dawn thousands of camels leave Hamed Ela in assigned order for the lake centre; they arrive mid-morning and chew salt; they turn back at noon, and caravans travel through the night ([swc]). A second account has caravans leaving the lake each late afternoon and travelling only by night ([oac]). The last ~7 km to the flat is a "rugged, wet sandy plain" ([wold]). Tourist stops on the pan last only 20–30 minutes because of the heat ([brsalt]).

**Hamed Ela**, the real cutters' village: about 4,000 people wholly dependent on salt ([oac]); rudimentary acacia-branch huts, no electricity, open fires on hauled wood, water by truck ([hitd]).

*[Inference] The cuttings:* rectangular strip workings one or two slab-thicknesses deep (5–15 cm) in long rows of grooved lines; pried-up plates propped on edge; sticks still jammed in grooves; trimmed blocks stood on edge in rows to dry; offcut chips in white drifts; dirty heaps of scraped underside mud; cut faces banded white with thin brown mud partings; the newest pits flooding with brine first, the old ones aging to pink pools. *The salt track:* a braided band of hoof-trodden crust 2–10 m wide, crushed to grey-brown granular salt and dust, with a single-file centre groove 30–50 cm wide; dung pellets, dropped broken slabs and chips (plausible but undocumented), frayed grass-rope ends, the odd camel skeleton; off the pan, stone-strewn gully paths. *The loaded camel:* two banks of slabs on edge, one each flank, about 10–15 slabs a side between vertical wooden hooks, lashed with pale grass rope and dark leather over a lumpy pad; the load stands about 40–60 cm above the hump and is about 1–1.2 m wide (check against the 2013 loading photos below). *Navigation cairns* of salt blocks are pure inference.

### Air, weather and light

Midday on the pan is above 40 °C, caravans vanish into mirage, and the glare blinds ([swc]; [ecot]); a camel train walks into the sunset ([hitd]). *[Inference]* Five lighting states: pre-dawn blue with cutters' lamps and the salt glowing faintly grey-blue; low gold sunrise picking out every polygon ridge; midday white-out with a mirage band floating the escarpment and caravans above a false lake; late-afternoon departure with long caravan shadows; night caravans under starlight on moonlit, glowing salt. ## 8. Fossil Terraces and gypsum: an old sea's shore stepping down to the salt

### Overview

East of the washes, the ground steps down toward the pan in benches: cream fossil-reef cliffs capped with dark lava gravel, then lower, lighter steps of bedded gypsum with chalky pavement, then the salt edge; gypsum dunes stand off the pan (vault overview). The real Danakil margins carry **Pleistocene coral-and-red-algae ("coralgal") fringing reefs** that record **at least four Red Sea incursions, each followed by drying out**; the two youngest date to **~122 ka (MIS 5e) and ~210–231 ka (MIS 7)** ([egu21]; [jv19]). Most of northern Afar was probably under the Red Sea until about **30 ka** ([bon17]). This zone is deep time made walkable: a dead sea's shore, its giant clams and stone tools, choked with crystals.

### Ground (walking scale)

**Reef benches.** The reef carbonate is "indurated" (hard, well cemented) and full of "reef skeletons and various types of shells" ([ant17]); at most sites **alluvium covers the reef, including lava gravels** typical of the western margin ([ant17]). *[Inference]* Cream reef-flat limestone pavement, rough and pitted; shell hash of broken shells (0.5–3 cm); scattered black basalt and obsidian gravel washed over from the fans; patches of chalky gypsum fill.

**Gypsum steps [Analogue: Abu Dhabi desert soils, Shahid & Abdelfattah 2009]** ([shahid]): a **powdery surface** on porous gypsum; a **5 cm hard capping** of jumbled lens crystals, reddish from iron, over a 5 cm hardpan at 30 cm depth; **"honeycomb" (puffed) gypsum** that dissolves easily and **causes subsidence**; massive outcrop gypsum light reddish or pinkish, often half-buried in sand, whitish when pure; **desert roses** with shiny petals from under 1 cm to 5 cm; greenish **lenticular crystals 5–40 cm across and 2–5 cm thick**, and ~1 cm crystals covering **30–40% of the ground** in places; white gypsum **fans 3–4 cm wide standing 30–40 cm proud** where wind stripped the soil; tubes 5–15 cm in old root channels; **"bottlebrush" gypsum grown over dead plants**; silky satin spar. Gypsum is **softer than a fingernail** ([nps21]). *[Inference]* It crunches and powders underfoot (soft crunch, not halite's crack); selenite cleavage faces glint glassy-pearly on a matte chalky ground.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

**Reef terraces.** The main reef terrace is **3–8 m thick**, resistant and at Girawi'en **steep**; its top survives as **"intact ledges and rolling blocks"** broken from the terrace ([ant17]). At every locality there are **two marine terraces separated by a sharp erosion surface bored by *Gastrochaenolites*** (flask-shaped bivalve borings); the lower, older unit is harder; in the north, uplift can lift the older terrace above the younger, while further south the younger is a thin skin over the older ([jv19]). MIS 7 beds on the northern and western margins are uplifted **up to 170 m**, while outcrops near the rift axis subsided below sea level ([egu21]); western-margin outcrops range from **about −35 m to +80 m** relative to sea level, against a floor near −120 m ([jv19]). Warren places a well-defined set of reefs **atop the eastern bajada**, with basin rims stepping down from an earlier reef phase to a later **gypsum phase** ([wd]); the papers cover mainly the west, so reefs occur on both margins, which fits the map's eastward steps. At Girawi'en, north-west of Hamed Ela, a steep reef terrace sits on an escarpment above a floodplain running several km toward the lake ([ant17]).

**What is on the reef.** The older (MIS 7) reef is topped by **up to 2 m of red-algal framestone** (encrusting coralline algae, *Lithophyllum* gr. *kotschyanum*, *Porolithon onkodes*), with crusts filling **cavities up to 30 cm**; most aragonite has turned to calcite and **gypsum fills the remaining pores** ([jv19]). The younger (MIS 5) reef is **isolated coral frameworks, not a continuous reef** ([jv19]). Microbial and fibrous crusts reach **30 cm thick**, in microdigitate and columnar forms with **columns up to 6 cm**, shallow **domes from a few cm to 1 m** with a **"brain-like" surface** in deeper-water crusts, and spherules up to 1 cm in beds under 10 cm ([jv19]). Fauna: the small mussel ***Brachidontes pharaonis*** (often with both valves joined), giant clam ***Tridacna***, the horn snail ***Pirenella conica***, and common **serpulid worm tubes**; **no coral genera are named** ([jv19]). Small stromatolites and thrombolites grow on reef slopes and in cavities ([eos]).

**Salt canyons.** Near Dallol, salt canyons are made of **layers of halite and gypsum** ([laf1]); halite "pillars" alternate salt with thin clay films, probably one layer a year ([eos]).

*[Inference]* Risers 3–8 m with overhangs where soft lower beds undercut; talus aprons of fallen reef blocks 0.5–3 m; bench treads as a design choice (roughly 20–200 m, one combat arena each); gypsum scarps 0.5–3 m of white-to-cream laminated bands 1–30 cm thick with brownish clay partings; domed blister mounds (**tumuli**, often hollow) from anhydrite swelling, as at Sorbas, Spain **[Analogue]**, size unsourced; small sinkholes. Coral props from other raised Red Sea reefs (massive *Porites* domes, brain-coral heads, branching *Acropora* rubble) are **[Analogue]**, not confirmed for Danakil. Weathering (honeycomb and tafoni pitting, sharp solution edges, a darker case-hardened rind, patchy varnish, white efflorescence at the base) is unsourced for Danakil reefs. Skyline: the dark escarpment behind, the white pan glowing on the eastern horizon.

**Gypsum dunes [Analogue: White Sands, New Mexico].** No gypsum dune field was found in the Danakil; the map's dunes are an authored feature. White Sands covers **about 710 km²** of gypsum dunes blown from a playa ([lucero]). Forms: **dome** dunes nearest the source (up to ~9 m a year), **barchans** (horns downwind, can move over 100 m a year), **transverse** ridges where barchans merge, and **parabolic** dunes at the edges held by plants (under ~2.4 m a year) ([nmbg]). Dune hearts move about **3.7–4 m a year**; the perched **water table is 30–60 cm** below the interdunes; edge dunes are held by plants and soil crust; the sand looks snowy because scratched grains reflect light ([nps21]); after rain the dunes look **tan** until they dry ([lucero]). Sand-moving winds of at least 15 mph blow mostly from the south-west, February to April; plants are left standing on **sand pedestals** after a dune passes ([dusa]). Selenite crystals form under the playa crust: "long jagged spars" and disks **"the size of bicycle tires"** (~60–70 cm) ([nps21]), up to **~0.9 m** long ([dusa]); wind lifts thin flakes ("cornflakes") ([nps21]). *[Inference]* Dunes 3–10 m with 32–34° slip faces and long gentle backs; ripples 5–15 cm apart and 0.5–1 cm high; damp, darker grey-cream interdune flats with a thin crust, polygon cracks and exposed root mats; a small dome-and-barchan patch of hundreds of metres to a few km suits a 100 km basin better than a White Sands–scale field.

### Water and minerals

Dry on the benches; optional seeps at the base of a riser with a white tide line *[Inference]*. Platy gypsum crystals form in Lake Afrera's bottom sediment, and halite, gypsum and sulphides may still precipitate there ([bon17]); Black Mountain dolines reach 50–70 °C at pH ~3–3.5 ([wd]). Interdune seep pools are plausible from the shallow water table **[Analogue: White Sands]**. Pools at the gypsum edge share the Mineral Washes pool kit (zone 4).

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

Reef rock and gypsum are essentially barren. At Hamed Ela, palm groves and shrubs grow by a dry riverbed where camels are kept, women and children gather palm leaves to thatch roofs, and **yellow dry grass and small wildflowers** grow on the lava route south ([ebp]). *[Inference]* In the wash channels between benches: scattered doum, umbrella thorn and toothbrush tree; near pools, seablite cushions; on the benches, dead stems salt-crusted into **bottlebrush gypsum** (a real form, [shahid]). On dunes, only plant-on-pedestal survivors at the edges (the White Sands soaptree yucca, which stretches its stem about 30 cm a year, [dusa], must be replaced by a Danakil-plausible species).

### Wildlife and signs

**"Golden goats" graze among the rocks** at Hamed Ela ([ebp]). Raptor whitewash on ledges, goat and camel tracks along washes, lizard drag-marks *[Inference]*. **[Analogue: White Sands]** most dune wildlife is nocturnal and leaves morning tracks; white-adapted animals include a pocket mouse, camel crickets, a white grasshopper and the **bleached earless lizard** (~10 cm, white with two black side spots, burying itself at midday) ([dusa]; [lizard]); a pale lizard critter is a fitting borrowing.

### Human traces and material culture

At Girawi'en a small scatter of freshly eroded **Middle Stone Age stone tools** lies on or just under the reef deposits: mostly flakes, two prismatic cores and a convergent **Levallois flake**, in **obsidian, basalt and patinated chert**, some still in carbonate matrix; artefacts are rare overall ([ant17]). Salt is hacked out with axes and **hoes** and carried by caravan on a 4-day round trip to Berahile ([voa]). Reef-stone quarrying, lime burning and fossil collecting were **not documented**; stone hut walls of reef blocks and lime kilns are authored options *[Inference]*.

### Air, weather and light

Nearly 40 °C by 10 a.m. at Dallol, the white ground reflecting intense sun ([ebp]); about 34 °C average and almost no rain ([voa]). *[Inference]* Heat shimmer and mirage over the pan beyond; a pale gypsum haze when wind lifts flakes (sourced at White Sands); hard white glare at midday; at sunrise and sunset the white dunes take the sky's colour (peach, pink, lavender) with strong blue shadows on slip faces; low light raking honeycomb reef faces.

### SIDE-AREA IDEAS (ideas, not decisions)

- **The old shore (Girawi'en).** *Basis:* a steep reef terrace with Middle Stone Age obsidian, basalt and chert tools on it ([ant17]). *Find:* the remains of people who lived on this coast over 100,000 years ago, eroding out of the reef. *Why:* the act's deepest-time lore beat and a quiet, beautiful lookout over the pan.
- **The clam bench.** *Basis:* *Tridacna*, *Brachidontes* and *Pirenella* on the reef terraces ([jv19]). *Find:* giant fossil clams standing open on a cream ledge. *Why:* an instantly readable "this was sea" image; good loot containers.
- **The crystal-choked reef (grotto).** *Basis:* gypsum filling the reef's pore space and crusts filling cavities up to 30 cm ([jv19]) *[Inference: a cave-scale version]*. *Find:* a reef cave whose walls bristle with selenite blades. *Why:* the zone's signature beauty interior, tying reef to gypsum.
- **The hollow ground.** *Basis:* honeycomb gypsum that dissolves and causes subsidence **[Analogue: Abu Dhabi]** ([shahid]); tumuli **[Analogue: Sorbas]**. *Find:* a field of blister domes, some hollow, some collapsing, with sinkholes. *Why:* a terrain-hazard puzzle.
- **The selenite gully.** *Basis:* **[Analogue: Lake Lucero]** steep gullies exposing selenite in an old lake floor ([lucero]). *Find:* a gully floor of large clear crystal blades. *Why:* a crafting-material or treasure pocket, and a glittering beauty shot.
- **The stromatolite margin.** *Basis:* stromatolites and thrombolites at Danakil lake margins and on reef slopes ([eos]); brain-surfaced domes up to 1 m ([jv19]). *Find:* a garden of living-rock domes. *Why:* uncanny, and pairs with the zone 4 idea if only one is built.
- **The interdune pools.** *Basis:* **[Analogue: White Sands]** water table 30–60 cm below the interdunes ([nps21]). *Find:* brackish seeps between white dunes with tracks of night animals. *Why:* a breather between dune fights.

### Beauty moments

Looking east from a reef ledge: cream benches stepping down to a blazing white pan with a mirror sheen; low sun lighting selenite blades growing out of a reef cavity; gypsum dunes taking sunset pink with blue shadows on their slip faces; a teal pool with a white gypsum lip beside a giant fossil clam. *[Inference]*

### Deliberate grit

Collapsing tumuli and hidden sinkholes; bleached bones salt-crusted into bottlebrush gypsum; ancient stone tools next to recent dead campfires; glare as a visibility hazard, gypsum haze when the wind rises. *[Inference]*

### Story hooks the real place suggests (ideas for the narrative skill)

The sea came in at least four times and dried out each time ([egu21]; [eos]): this basin has died before. The reefs grew into the basin and gypsum then choked them ([jv19]), a geological image of a living thing smothered by salt. People made tools on this shore in the Middle Stone Age ([ant17]).

### Departures from the real place

Gypsum dunes are not documented in the Danakil; they rest on White Sands. Tread widths, dune heights and coral genera are design choices. Reefs are documented on both margins, so the map's eastern terraces are consistent. **Conflict within the analogue:** dunes began forming ~25 ka ([nmbg]) vs a ~7,000-year-old dunefield ([lizard]).

## 9. Basalt Fields: rust-brown old lobes under glossy black tongues

### Overview

North of the basin floor, below the fault wall, weathered older basalt lobes are overrun by fresh charcoal-black tongues (vault overview; brief). It is a daylight zone: rust-brown, varnished old lava with cream dust seams and sparse grey thorn scrub, crossed by glossy young flows and white-dusted rift cracks, with the red wall on one side and the volcano's plume beyond. The same rock as the Volcano, read through age.

### Ground (walking scale)

**Real Afar lava.** Erta Ale's ground is mostly **pahoehoe**: very small lava ropes, several pahoehoe types side by side, a **miniature lava tube**, and **fine light-coloured ash settled between the ropes** ([swlava]); **Pele's hair** (fine glass strands) lies among wavy ridges, and large areas are **intricately folded and twisted crust** of older pahoehoe ([va15]). The 2017 flank flows were **numerous pahoehoe flows fed by lava tubes**; a'a is not mentioned ([gvpe]). **Colour with age is sourced:** young basalt reads black or dark grey, and over time its iron oxidises and it turns **lighter and redder** ([nasaafr]). In the Dabbahu rift, dark flows are **streaked and cut by white wind-blown ash and dust**, with buff ash bands on fresh fault faces ([leeds]).

**Forms [Analogue: Hawaii]** ([npslava]): ropy pahoehoe forms as moving lava drags its cooling crust into ridges; **shelly** pahoehoe has thin crusts over open blisters and tubes; **slabby** pahoehoe is jumbled overturned plates grading into a'a; toes are small lobes up to a few feet wide that inflate and merge; a'a is thick with a jagged clinker top over a dense core.

*[Inference] Ground by age, blendable:* (a) old lobes, matte rust-brown with varnish on upper faces and greyer undersides, softened but readable ropes, breaking into slabs; (b) cream and buff silt-and-ash pans filling hollows and cracks, with ripples and hoof prints, making a strong black-and-cream crack pattern; (c) mid-age grey-black pahoehoe with dull sheen and dust in the rope troughs; (d) fresh tongues, glossy black with a silver-grey to faint blue or bronze sheen in raking light, ropes 2–10 cm wide in bands 0.5–2 m, toes 0.2–1 m, shelly crust 1–5 cm thick collapsing into hollows 10–50 cm deep, entrail pahoehoe (10–30 cm tubes) on steeper slopes, golden Pele's hair mats in the lee of rocks, Pele's tears in hollows; (e) trails of crushed grey-white crust and camel dung.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

**Sourced Afar forms.** **Hornitos** about 7 m (2017) to 10 m, then "dozens of metres" (2022–23) ([gvpe]); hornitos along a flank fissure on the western flank ([swclimb]). The 2017 NE flow ran **16 km** with **skylights** and overflows, and breakouts 10–15 km from the vent; the flow field was about **1.4 km wide** and reached the alluvial plain by March 2018; the SW field reached about 5.1 km ([gvpe]). **Rift cracks at Dabbahu (2005):** a 60 km segment opened by up to **8 m in about 3 weeks**; a **vent about 60 m wide** opened explosively beside a 200 m wide, 4 km long zone of open fissures and normal faults; cracks about **0.5 m wide**; fault scarps moved **more than 3 m**, fresh slip showing as a buff band; a later vent was about 500 m long with layered ash, **a tunnel at its south end** and blown-out pale rhyolitic blocks ([leeds]). In 2025, a **dike ran about 30 km south-east** from Erta Ale and a **graben about 2 km wide subsided** along its southern part ([comet]).

*[Inference / Analogue]* Tumuli 1–5 m high and 5–30 m long with a 0.3–1 m axial crack; pressure ridges 1–5 m high; squeeze-ups 0.3–2 m; skylights 1–10 m; collapse pits over drained tubes 5–50 m across and 2–15 m deep; spatter cones 3–20 m; flow fronts 1–3 m (pahoehoe); **kipukas**, islands of older ground ringed by younger flow, from 10 m to km, the best device for this zone: rust-brown islands carrying dust and scrub inside fresh black. Crack fields 0.3–1 m wide (jumpable or blocking), gaping fissures several metres wide, scarps 1–5 m stepping the floor, all cutting old and young lava alike. Skyline: the red-ochre fault wall to one side and the low black shield with its smoke plume beyond.

### Water and minerals

None on the lava. *[Inference]* White salt or alkali efflorescence in crack bottoms near the pan; rust bleed where old lobes meet damp ground; a seep with doum at the foot of the fault wall. Dodom, the Afar village at Erta Ale's foot, has **wells** ([swclimb]).

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

On basaltic lava fields, **hook thorn (*Senegalia mellifera*) and *Rhigozum somalense*** dominate; umbrella thorn, Nubian thorn and desert date grow on sandy plains; doum stands in depressions and wadis ([oneearth]); gravel plains carry thin scrub ([stewart]). The caldera floor is barren ([swvw]). *[Inference]* Zero plants on flows under ~50 years old; scrub only where silt has filled cracks and hollows, about one shrub per 10–50 m², often in a line along a crack; the densest scrub on kipukas and lobe margins against sand; dead grey stumps and dry grass tufts in silt. Lichens are probably minimal in this heat (unverified).

### Wildlife and signs

The ecoregion fauna (region section) applies: wild ass, Dorcas and Soemmerring's gazelles, Salt's dik-dik, gerenuk, beira, beisa oryx, the endemic Berbera gerbil, Archer's lark, and geckos including Arnold's leaf-toed gecko ([oneearth]). *[Inference]* Gazelle tracks in silt pockets, dik-dik dung middens, geckos on warm rock, raptors or crows on spatter cones, hoof trails polished across lava, bleached bones in a fissure; the wild ass as a rare encounter.

### Human traces and material culture

People live at the lava's edges, not on it. Afar camps are round domed tents **"like upturned boats", covered in brightly coloured cloth**, inside thornbush corrals for camels and goats ([stewart]); small Afar villages line the descent over dusty sand and lava fields ([va15]). 4x4s lurch over rocks and cracks, and lava slows them to walking pace ([ab]; [stewart]); camels carried GPS and seismic stations into the Afar rift ([leeds]). *[Inference]* Trail cairns; dry-stacked basalt windbreak crescents and corral rings 0.5–1.5 m high; a collapsed hut frame at the margin; wheel ruts in silt pans; an abandoned sensor station; bottles.

### Air, weather and light

Days over 40 °C, with the Danakil regularly reaching 45 °C ([leeds]; [ab]); sand whips against car windows on the approach ([ab]); haze hides the distant highlands ([stewart]). *[Inference]* White-hot noon; heat shimmer over black rock; dust devils over silt pans; low warm raking light at dawn and dusk to show the sheen and ropes; at night the volcano's red glow on the skyline.

- **The well village.** *Basis:* Dodom, an Afar village with wells at the volcano's foot ([swclimb]). *Find:* the last people before the summit, cloth-covered domes, a thorn corral. *Why:* the staging post for the Volcano.

### Story hooks the real place suggests (ideas for the narrative skill)

The ground here opened 8 m in three weeks in 2005 ([leeds]), and in 2025 a dike ran 30 km toward Hayli Gubbi, which then erupted in late November 2025 for the first time in roughly 12,000 years (headlines only, not read: [ajhayli]; [ksat]). A dormant mountain waking after millennia is a ready hook for a world where the machine stirs things long asleep.

### Departures from the real place

Real Afar lava fields such as Erta Ale's lie on the rift floor (its flows spread NW of Lake Afrera, [nasaafr]); the game places them north below the fault wall. Tumuli, pressure ridges, squeeze-ups and kipukas are not described in the Afar sources; they are Hawaiian analogue forms. A'a is not documented at Erta Ale.

## 10. The Volcano: a red-veined lake on a broad black shield

### Overview

An Erta Ale–style shield stands on the north fault block, with a summit caldera, pit craters and a small, play-exaggerated lava lake (brief); the vault confirms the north-east volcano is active (2026-11-03) and the north rim is raised between the plateau and the volcano (2026-11-13). It is a dusk-and-night zone: black pahoehoe, golden Pele's hair, a dark crust broken into plates by glowing red seams, a red sky-glow, gas, and stone huts on the rim. "Erta Ale" means **"smoking mountain"** in Afar ([ab]).

### Ground (walking scale)

The caldera floor is **barren black pahoehoe**; one group camped on it for six days ([swvw]). Near the summit: very small ropes, ash between them, a miniature tube ([swlava]); Pele's hair and intricately folded old crust ([va15]). Big lake bubbles fling lava that lands as **sticky black blobs that cool to rock** at the pit rim ([stewart]). The hike crosses large rocks, and the solidified flow inside the caldera is rough ([ab]; [stewart]). *[Inference]* Flanks of fresh and mid-age pahoehoe with frequent shelly crust and spatter fields near fissures; pit-rim spatter agglutinate (rough welded blobs), glassy splash blobs and smooth glossy overflow sheets.

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

Erta Ale is a basaltic shield about **50 km across with about 500 m of relief** (1971); its summit caldera is elliptical, about **0.7 × 1.6 km**, elongated NNW–SSE, with North and South pit craters in its northern half; a larger **1.8 × 3.1 km depression** bounded by curved fault scarps lies south-east of the summit ([gvpe]). From the rim at dawn other shield silhouettes stand on the horizon, and a "look-out hill" rises inside the caldera ([va15]). Pit sizes over time: North pit about 200 × 300 m (1971); South crater 160 × 130 m (2002) to 190 × 150 m (2009); South lake about 200 m (2021–22); a new North Pit lake about **350 m across and 15 m deep** (January 2023) ([gvpe]). In April 2017 a lava lake about **80 × 175 m** formed at a flank vent ([gvpe]). A hornito about 10 m high grew in the South Pit in December 2022 and reached "dozens of metres" by March 2023 ([gvpe]). *[Inference]* For play: caldera 150–400 m, the lake 30–80 m across and 10–30 m below the rim, black overflow terraces and "bathtub rings" on the pit walls, a shore hornito with drip stalactites; gentle 2–6° shield slopes, a broad low skyline, not a cone.

### Water and minerals

**The lava lake.** Dark solidified slabs shift over a bright orange glow, with orange lava in the cracks and **spatter tens of metres high** (2022–23); spatter to **30 m** in bubble bursts (January 2017); the level cycled every **~30 minutes between 40 and 50 m below the caldera floor** (April 2017) ([gvpe]). Crust **sinks at one shore while glowing lava squeezes up through cracks**; stretched crust shows red; plates sometimes override each other, exposing an incandescent edge; crust too thick to sink **piles up on the shore as shiny folds**; upwelling at the opposite shore lifts glossy crust and **spills bright yellow lava** over darker crust; waves reach about **1 m**; bubble waves build **lava stalactites on a shore hornito's lips** (photos from about 70 m away) ([swlake]). The crust breaks into slabs edged with bright lines like a jigsaw, moving at **centimetres per second** ([allgeo]). Glowing "rift zones" streak like lightning across the surface, sometimes three meeting at the centre; fountains pull the surface toward themselves; **at dusk blue sky light reflects off the dark lava** ([swlava]). Fresh lava is bright yellow, cooled crust darker, stressed zones red ([swlake]); at dusk zigzag cracks and the margin glow red while the crust goes dark ([va15]). Observers describe red and black veins sliding like a churning sea, bubbles every few minutes, heat like standing near hot coals, faces lit red ([stewart]); the lake "blisters and explodes" and smokes over after about an hour ([ab]). Fumaroles and sulfur sublimates at Erta Ale are **not described** in the sources; yellow sulfur and white salt-sulfate crusts in rim cracks are an accent *[Inference]*. Lake temperature was not sourced (1,150–1,200 °C is commonly cited, unverified).

**Recent change.** Rim overflows in 2016–17 covered about 70% of the rim and about 1 km² of the floor; on 21 January 2017 a south-east-flank fissure opened 3–4 km from the South crater and **the lake dropped 80–100 m**, recovering to 40–50 m below the rim by mid-February ([gvpe]). On **15 July 2025** an explosive eruption put ash and SO₂ to about 2 km, SO₂ output was 4–10 kt a day, and **both pit craters collapsed, by at least 170 m (North) and 57 m (South)**, with a "lava lake collapse" reported (preliminary) ([comet]). **Whether a lake has re-formed by November 2026 was not found.** Most lava-lake photo reference predates July 2025.

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

None in the caldera or on young flanks ([swvw]). The last scrub is at the shield foot by the wells *[Inference]*.

### Wildlife and signs

Almost none on top. Camels carry luggage, food, mattresses and gear and are unloaded at the rim ([va15]; [stewart]). Crows or ravens at the litter *[Inference]*.

### Human traces and material culture

The walk starts from **Dodom**, an Afar village with wells, about **10 km by camel caravan** over the western flank past hornitos, to **shelters on the western caldera rim** ([swclimb]). Hikes start around 6 p.m. to avoid heat and take about 3 hours; guests sleep on thin mattresses in a **stone Afar hut** or under the stars on **makeshift wooden beds**; the ground near camp is littered with **plastic bottles and toilet paper**; **gas masks** are handed out; **two escorts carry automatic rifles**, one at the back ([ab]); an armed man is silhouetted against the sky ([stewart]). Viewers watch from the vent rim or the old caldera rim at dusk and dawn ([va15]). *[Inference]* Huts of dry-stacked basalt roofed with sticks, mats or tarp; a guard post; a camel unloading point; a cairn or rope line to the viewing edge. (The escorts are commonly linked to a 2012 attack on tourists, unsourced.)

### Air, weather and light

A red glow over the summit with sparks, lava light on faces, otherwise only torches and stars ([stewart]); golden evening light on the lake at sunset, red seams appearing as daylight fades, dawn viewing ([va15]); SO₂ from hornitos with noisy degassing and a steam plume (2022) ([gvpe]). *[Inference]* A struck-match, throat-catching SO₂ smell; heat radiating from the rim; steam veils that hide and reveal the lake; a constant surf-like roar and hiss, sharp cracks as plates break, deep whumps as bubbles burst, the patter of falling spatter, the tinkle of cooling glass crust.

## 11. Escarpment, falls gorge, switchback climb and plateau: the way out

### Overview

The red-brown fault escarpment on the basin's west and north rim; the gorge where the falls stream leaves the plateau; the act's only graded caravan climb, past the upper city on its shelf; and the highland plateau with small stone terraces of arid crops along the stream (vault, 2026-11-13). The real analogue is the salt road from the Danakil via Berahile up to Mekelle in Tigray. Seen from the basin it is a banded wall; walked, it is a cool, colour-striped gorge with cream tufa and green seeps, then hairpins on dry-stone walls, then thick-walled flat-roofed stone houses, then red-brown fields framed by pale stone bunds under a crisp dark-blue sky. It is the act's exit and its first relief from the heat.

### Ground (walking scale)

**Climb.** Highland caravan trails mostly follow rivers and streams because valleys let travellers through steep country at good slopes; surfaces are **silty clay, gravelly paths and grassland**; lowland trail surfaces are dense gravel, sand, fine river sediment and pebbly dry riverbeds ([wold]). *[Inference]* A tread 1.5–3 m wide (camels need about 2 m and gentle grades; donkeys manage steps of 20–30 cm); worn steps polished smooth with dished tops; dung and urine stains concentrated at hairpins and rest spots; scattered broken salt fragments, lost straps and pads.

**Gorge.** *[Inference]* Wet, water-polished basement slabs at the bottom; tufa crusts; rimstone pools; sand bars; flood debris lines at a fixed height.

**Plateau.** Ploughed red-brown and dark grey soil, stubble, stony grazing land; bunds turn 12% slopes into near-level 3% terraces ([wocat1]).

### Rock and landforms (1–100 m) and skyline

**The real rock stack** on the Tigray side, base to top, from geological mapping beside the rift escarpment ([geba]): **Precambrian basement** of green or grey metavolcanics, slates and phyllites; **Enticho sandstone** (to ~100 m, white, cross-bedded, iron-stained layers); **Edaga Arbi tillite** (150–180 m, purple, red, olive-green and grey, **boulders up to 6 m**); **Adigrat sandstone** (up to ~700 m, **yellowish to red or pink** with white upper beds, exposed in deeply incised gorges); the **Antalo limestone** supersequence (~1,100 m in total; marl light yellowish fresh and yellow-green weathered; a karstified limestone cliff ~8 m thick); **Agula shale** (to ~400 m, laminated light-to-dark grey with **gypsum layers**); **dolerite sills** 80–130 m thick forming steep cliffs or flat tops; **Amba Aradam sandstone** (variegated white, purple, red and yellow); and **Trap basalts** (40–50 m of fine black basalt, ~15 m of **white lake sediment**, then 60–80 m of coarser black cliff-forming basalt). Faults cut the area into blocks. The Antalo limestone's sub-horizontal layering gives **alternating cliffs and flats**, and it holds **dissolution caves** (St Mika'el's cave at Kurkura) ([antalo]); the western Mekelle Outlier has caves, speleothems, **karren** (fluted, pitted bare limestone) and **many relict tufa dams** ([dramis]). A botanical transect from the Tigray highlands to the Afar lowlands ran from about 2,740 m to 1,000–1,400 m, roughly **1,300–1,700 m of relief on the upper face** ([tropentag]).

*[Inference] What to show on the game face:* Adigrat red-ochre sandstone as the dominant mid-face band on a dark grey-green basement plinth, capped by pale Antalo limestone ledges, with a black basalt cornice only on the highest skyline. This is a selection, not a cross-section of one real place. Hard units (sandstone, limestone cliff beds, dolerite, basalt) make walls tens of metres high; soft units (marl, shale, tillite) make debris slopes and benches, so the city shelf is most plausible where shale or marl has retreated above a sandstone cliff. Talus breaks by rock: sandstone into rectangular slabs 0.3–3 m with rounded edges, limestone into angular fluted blocks, basalt into columns, tillite releasing odd rounded erratics. A gorge cut through the stack bands top to bottom: black basalt rim, cream limestone ledges with dark karst holes and tufa curtains, red-pink cross-bedded sandstone walls with desert-varnish streaks, and a grey-green basement slot at the bottom polished by water. Gorge: a slot 5–30 m wide with walls 30–100 m. Cliff bands 20–80 m; benches 10–200 m wide.

**Skyline.** From the basin, the banded wall; from the plateau (2,000–2,700 m), crisp dark-blue sky and hard shadows, and, looking down, a flat milky haze layer swallowing the far rim: an "edge of the world" view from the city shelf *[Inference]*.

### Water and minerals

**Tufa is sourced on the plateau:** travertine forms at **spring outlets, waterfalls and rapids around Mekelle**; the Alula Aba Nega spring near Agbe has **active stalactite and stalagmite growth**; streams have cut into the travertine since forest loss began about **3,000 years ago** ([geba]). In the highlands, runoff collects in pockets along riverbeds after the rains, and caravaners time travel so rivers are neither in flood nor fully dry ([wold]). The Agula shale's gypsum layers are a source of white crusts and bitter water ([geba]). Check-dam ponds hold good surface water **September to January** ([wocat2]). *[Inference]* The falls: a limestone-fed stream drapes a tufa apron of rounded cream-to-honey lobes and "frozen curtains" 1–20 m tall over the lip, moss-green and black algae streaks where water runs, dry white crust where it no longer does; rimstone pools with curved rims 5–50 cm step down the gorge; **relict, dry, cut-through tufa dams upstream show the stream was once bigger**, a real and ready "dying world" device; seep lines form horizontal green bands along one bench; black-brown manganese and algae streaks under every seep; orange stain where water leaves sandstone; white efflorescence near the hot foot.

### Vegetation (and where nothing grows)

Use the escarpment and plateau rows of the region plant table. Sourced anchors: acacia woodland in the Afar lowlands within ~5 km of the highland sites; species richness lowest at 1,000 m (as few as 2 species per 2,500 m² plot), peaking at **up to 45 species per plot at 1,900–2,100 m**; ***Juniperus procera* with *Olea*** dominating the Desa'a escarpment forest, which is degraded, with **many stumps and many dead junipers and olives still standing**, invaded by *Tarconanthus* and *Calpurnia* ([tropentag]). On the plateau, exclosures hold 60 woody species against 28 on grazed land, and grazed land is about 83% shrubs ([exclosure]); **cactus pear** covers ~7.4% of Tigray on stony slopes, is planted spineless around farmsteads and spiny as live fence, has its pads fed to stock (often after burning off the spines) in drought, and is now being killed in the south by **cochineal released in 2004** ([lrrd]); stone bunds carry *Leucaena* and *Sesbania* at about 1,000 plants a hectare plus grasses ([wocat1]). Caravans rest under large acacias or rock shelters ([wold]). *[Inference]* By belt: at the foot and fans, umbrella thorn browsed into hourglass shapes, Prosopis along wadis, doum only at shallow water, bare between; on the lower face, hook thorn, *Commiphora* with peeling papery bark, aloes on ledges with coral-red spikes in the dry season, and candelabra euphorbias standing on cliff ledges where goats cannot reach; on the upper face, hop bush, *Euclea*, *Carissa*, wild olive with silvery leaf undersides, and figs at seeps; at the rim, juniper (10–25 m, reddish fibrous bark) with silver-grey dead snags. At springs at any height, buttressed sycomore or *Ficus vasta*, maidenhair and moss on wet rock, reeds in pools. Drought look: leafless acacias, grass grazed to grey-blond stubble, shrivelled leaning euphorbias, wrinkled yellow cactus pads, cochineal-killed cactus as grey-white crusted skeletons (white waxy fluff, crimson stain when crushed). **Crops** (vault, provisional: sorghum, millet, barley, teff and chickpeas): teff (*Eragrostis tef*) is a fine grass 0.4–1.2 m, bright green in August and a soft red-brown or straw haze by October; sorghum 1.5–3 m with drooping cream, red-brown or dark red heads, the strongest silhouette crop; finger millet 0.6–1.5 m with claw heads; barley and wheat 0.6–1 m, golden in November; chickpea and faba bean low blue-green rows 30–60 cm *[Inference: crop forms and calendar]*. Sourced: crops of teff, barley, wheat, sorghum, faba bean and sunflower, **one season, longest July to December (~150 days)**, free grazing after harvest ([wocat1]); growing season 90–120 days, rains roughly June to September ([exclosure]).

### Wildlife and signs

Jackals and foxes threaten caravan camps ([wold]). Hyrax latrines (white urine streaks on ledges), baboon troops, vultures and lammergeier on thermals, goat tracks, oxen on stubble *[Inference, unsourced]*.

### Human traces and material culture

**The road.** The real salt road is about **160 km** from the salt flat to the Tigray highlands, about 110 km of highland section (Mekelle to Desi'a) and about 72 km lowland, with three highland descents: the **Desi'a route** (most used), the **Usot route** (Atsbi caravaners) and the **Shiket pass** (~28 km south of Desi'a, 1,350 m, flat and good for camels but too hot) ([wold]). Berahile is the lowland Afar market and tax town where the Afar *arho* stops ([swc]); highlanders prefer donkeys and lowlanders camels (the sample: 196 donkeys, 11 mules, 566 camels) ([wold]). Lowland caravaners take 3–4 days each way and make 38–54 trips a year; highlanders take 7–10 days and make about 14 ([wold]). A paved road from Mekelle cut the Berahile drive from five hours to three, and miners fear it will bring mechanised salt firms ([gtsalt]). Night camps follow the region-section layout ([wold]). *[Inference]* Switchbacks on dry-stone revetment walls 1–4 m high of unmortared blocks with a battered uphill face; drainage cross-bars of laid stone; a low kerb of 20–40 cm on the drop side; rest flats at hairpins with a shade tree or overhang, a three-stone hearth and soot on the rock; dead donkey bones in gullies; an old route of narrow valley paths, cairns and hearths, and a newer graded road with cut-and-fill scars, gabion retaining walls and culverts. The game's slaver road can mix both, a carved, revetted road built by forced labour beside erased older paths.

**The city and villages.** The Tigray farmhouse (***hidmo***) has walls of stacked local stone (*mendeg*), around Mekelle mostly **Antalo limestone in black, white and yellowish-white**; it is **absent in basalt areas**; the top course has at least two layers of thin broad stones (*qatsela*) to keep water out; rows of **scaffold holes (*bukko*)** are left in walls ([shimizu]). Timber is scarce and prestigious (eucalyptus commercial; juniper and olive rare and protected): a central pillar (*'amdi*) carries a main beam (*gadi'm*), smaller beams (*serayt*) in alternating pairs, planks (*mi'buts*) usually in a **V (herringbone) pattern**, a cross pattern in churches ([shimizu]). The roof is **flat or slightly sloped, gravel and soil with hard-packed mud in red and/or white soils**, used for sleeping, watching plots and **drying straw** ([shimizu]). The **door is about 1.02 m high, deliberately low**, with timber panels and lintels, never arched; **small high windows (*meshkot*)** with stone lintels, often partly blocked, leave interiors dim; some doors have a **stone eave that grows grass** ([shimizu]). Curved piled-stone walls enclose a courtyard (*mereba*) with house, kitchen, cattle space and small-animal pen; inside are mud-plastered stone benches (*medeb*) built by women, a tool rail, a grain box (*gotera*), wall niches, an upper grain store and lower livestock space; parcels are curved on hillsides in Inderta, rectilinear in 'Agame; a round variant (*kibi-hidmo*) exists; the type dates to at least the late first millennium BC ([shimizu]). *[Inference]* Walls of flat slabs 10–40 cm, chinked, no visible mortar, likely 50–80 cm thick, with bands where black, white and yellow stone mix; flat earth roofs with slab parapets, straw, fuel stacks and spouts, ideal for an isometric camera; churches on hilltops or ledges in groves of old juniper, olive or fig inside a stone compound, rock-hewn churches in sandstone cliffs. *Slaver city mapping [Inference]:* dense walled courtyards stepping down the shelf, timber as visible wealth, low doors and small windows as defence, animal pens repurposed as holding pens.

**Fields.** **Stone-faced soil bunds**: a stone wall on the downslope side with a soil embankment above, along the contour, **0.5–0.75 m high, 1 m wide at the base and 30 cm at the top**, of medium stones on a ~30 cm foundation trench, **60–80 m apart at 1 m vertical intervals**, built January to April and repaired every January ([wocat1]). **Check dams** of stone, concrete or gabions, **1–2 m high and 1–2 m wide**, spanning gullies, with ~1 m sluice gates, store water and trap silt ([wocat2]); semi-circular stone bunds and stone-faced trenches also exist ([wocat3]; [wocat4]). Highland forest has been largely cleared over the past 2,000 years ([wold]). *[Inference]* Threshing floors (*awdi*) 6–12 m across on raised spots, dung-and-mud plastered, ringed with stones; oxen circling; winnowing into the wind with a chaff plume; the *maresha* ox plough, a wooden ard 2–3 m long with a single iron point; conical haystacks 2–4 m; silt wedges behind check dams as small dark fields; red-brown gully walls 1–5 m with fresh slumps; cactus-pear and euphorbia hedges; in the dry season (January to May) bare ploughed soil and stubble with bunds standing out as pale lines across every slope.

### Air, weather and light

The depression averages about 34 °C with almost no rain ([gtsalt]); caravans climb overnight from about −100 m to about 2,300 m ([swc]); camp winds are southerly March–September and east–west September–March, with hearths upwind ([wold]); highland rains fall roughly June–September ([exclosure]). *[Inference]* The west-rim face glows red-orange at sunrise and falls into its own shadow by mid-to-late afternoon; the raised north rim faces south and is lit most of the day; afternoon storms with dark anvils over the plateau in the rains while the basin stays clear; flash floods down the gorge; morning mist spilling over the rim lip on cool mornings; spray rainbows in morning sun at the falls; chaff plumes from threshing floors.

### SIDE-AREA IDEAS (ideas, not decisions)

- **The dead dams.** *Basis:* relict tufa dams cut by streams after forest loss about 3,000 years ago ([geba]; [dramis]). *Find:* a staircase of dry cream tufa dams upstream of the falls, cut through, with dry pools and fossil leaf casts *[Inference]*. *Why:* the gorge's own proof that the water is failing; climbing puzzles.
- **The limestone cave.** *Basis:* dissolution caves in the Antalo limestone (St Mika'el's cave at Kurkura) and speleothems ([antalo]; [dramis]). *Find:* a cave system in the gorge wall with flowstone, a shrine, an escape route around the city. *Why:* a cool, dark dungeon and a smuggling path past the slavers' road.
- **The dead forest.** *Basis:* the Desa'a juniper-olive forest with many dead trees still standing and stumps everywhere ([tropentag]). *Find:* a grove of silver juniper snags on the rim, invasive shrubs, charcoal burners. *Why:* the plateau's grit beat and a striking silhouette.
- **The hairpin camp.** *Basis:* the Woldekiros camp layout, every ~20 km, near villages ([wold]). *Find:* a camp on a flat below the city: corral, hearths, salt in two rows ringed by sleepers. *Why:* the last stop before the city gate, for rumours and an ambush.
- **The erased road.** *Basis:* three real highland descents, including the flat but too-hot Shiket pass ([wold]). *Find:* an old foot route around the city, cairns and hearths, hard and hot. *Why:* the vault allows rough foot routes elsewhere; a fugitives' path.
- **The check-dam fields.** *Basis:* check dams 1–2 m high that pond water September to January and trap silt ([wocat2]). *Find:* a gully of stepped dams, silt fields, a pond, farmers. *Why:* the plateau's small living beauty, and food the city never has enough of.
- **The cactus blight.** *Basis:* cochineal released in 2004 now killing cactus pear "out of control" ([lrrd]). *Find:* fields of white-crusted dead cactus, starving goats. *Why:* a slow, real image of a famine food dying.
- **The hilltop church.** *Basis:* *[Inference]* Tigray churches in groves of old trees on hills and ledges; church ceilings in a cross pattern ([shimizu]). *Find:* a compound with the only big trees left, a cistern, a keeper. *Why:* sanctuary and lore at the act's exit. ### Story hooks the real place suggests (ideas for the narrative skill)

Berahile is a lowland market and tax town where caravans are counted ([swc]); the slaver city at the head of the climb is its inverse, a checkpoint taxing goods and people on the way up *[Inference]*. Tufa dams were cut when the forest was cleared about 3,000 years ago ([geba]): people caused the water to fail before. A paved road is killing the caravan trade ([gtsalt]). Timber is scarce and prestigious ([shimizu]), so in the city wealth shows as wood.

### Departures from the real place

The falls are invented, though grounded in Tigray tufa. The layer order on the game face is a selection, not one real section; no source describes the face above Berahile. Switchback engineering and gradients are unsourced (Woldekiros gives none). The real road follows river valleys rather than one graded climb past a city. The north rim is raised by author decision.

## Shared and unique materials: a continuity table for the whole act

Varnish darkens on undisturbed stone and is broken pale wherever feet pass ([usgs]). Dallol's pools run from white and green to rust and brown ([dhydro]). ## Open questions for George

1. **Real cultures or fictional peoples?** If the game's peoples are fictional, should the studio keep Afar and Tigrayan construction logic (dome frames, mat layers, dry-stone bases, hidmo masonry, slab sizes, caravan roles) and change the ornament and names? Should real terms such as *daboyta*, *Focolo*, *Shumbahri* and *gole'o* appear in the game, be adapted or be replaced?
2. **Which palm at the Slums?** Date palms (author decision, provisional) are not confirmed in the Danakil, while doum is native. Should the planted line be date palm and the wild edge doum, as the guide suggests?
3. **What feeds the falls?** Tufa needs a limestone catchment. Is the falls stream cold, warm, or fed by hot fault springs, and may a hot-vent field sit to one side of the Slums?
4. **How much pink?** Pink only in small evaporating pools and old quarry pits, as recommended, or wider?
5. **Blue flames at the sulfur workings?** They come from Ijen, not Dallol. Accept the licence for the night beauty beat?
6. **How do captives cut sulfur?** By Ijen-style condensing pipes, by Dallol-style open pits, or both?
7. **The lava lake's state.** Active lake, the post-July 2025 drained pit, or the lake on the before map and the drained pit on the present one?
8. **Gypsum dunes scale.** A small dome-and-barchan patch, or a larger field?
9. **Who left the industrial ruins?** The railhead, drill holes, potash camp and evaporation works suggest an older extractor. Do they belong to a human empire, to the machine's era, or are they cut?
10. **Is Prosopis the machine's plant?** Recast it as a fictional invasive tied to the corruption, or keep it as a natural invader?
11. **Does the pan still flood?** If not, cut scars never heal, which is a strong visual record of extraction. Is that the intended reading of "the basin is drying"?
12. **Pack animals.** Camels and donkeys both (the real split is lowland camels, highland donkeys), and is the slaver road graded for camels?
13. **Real fauna?** Is the African wild ass (critically endangered) in the game as itself, or replaced by a fictional animal?
14. **Mineral Washes pool.** Non-acid teal brine (recommended) or a muted acid pool?
15. **Dead Spring geology.** Does the Dead Spring sit over evaporites (needed for sinkholes), and was it a lake on the before map (needed for the chimneys)?
16. **Which side areas to keep?** The master list has 55; the strongest ten are suggested above.

## Conclusion

The real Danakil gives Act I one rule that ties every zone together, which the per-zone facts only hint at: **every material shows its age through colour**. Fresh is pale or bright, old is dark, red or brown. That holds for Dallol's pools, for basalt, for varnish, for palms, for salt crust and for tufa. Built into shaders and kits once, it lets the player read hazard, history and the drying of the basin anywhere without text. The second finding is narrative. The real basin's history is a stack of extractors: a sea that dried at least four times, Aksumite-era caravans, an Italian potash railway, 1950s sulfur mining, a US drilling programme, and trucks now killing the camel trade. The slavers can be the latest layer in that stack, and the best side areas are its ruins.

The evidence is weakest exactly where the game already departs from reality on purpose: the falls, the Dead Spring and the gypsum dunes. For those three, more research will not change much, and art-direction sign-off on the labelled analogues is the next step. The gaps that would most improve the asset lists are elsewhere: photo-calibrated colour, walking-scale ground numbers for the basin floor, the salt-cutting tools and loads in Trojer's film, and Erta Ale's present state.
